@chapter Laws against vagabonds
@title text="Bloody Legislation and the Forcing Down of Wages" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 28" art=thumb
The chapter opens with the people the last lesson left on the road. The new manufactures couldn't absorb them as fast as they were turned off the land, and they couldn't at once adapt to the discipline of their new condition.

@art vagrancy-laws
So across Western Europe came laws against vagabondage. Under Henry the eighth in fifteen thirty, sturdy vagabonds were whipped and sent home to put themselves to labour. What grim irony, Marx writes. Under Edward the sixth in fifteen forty seven, a man who refused to work could be condemned as a slave to whoever denounced him.

@words text="Davies, 1966 · Beier: masterless men"
Historians have tested the list. Davies showed in nineteen sixty six that the slavery statute was repealed by fifteen fifty and hardly enforced. Beier, working from arrest records, found a real and growing population of masterless men, mostly young and looking for work. Marx's catalogue is of the law's text. Enforcement was uneven.

@chapter Force, then dull compulsion
@art dull-compulsion
Then the turn. Once capitalist production is fully developed, the reserve army keeps wages where capital needs them. The dull compulsion of economic relations completes the subjection of the labourer. Direct force is used only exceptionally. But at the genesis, the rising bourgeoisie used the state to regulate wages and lengthen the day.

@chapter The Statute of Labourers
@quote text="It was forbidden, under pain of imprisonment, to pay higher wages than those fixed by the statute, but the taking of higher wages was more severely punished than the giving them." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 28"
The Statute of Labourers fixed a tariff of wages for town and country. It was forbidden, under pain of imprisonment, to pay higher wages than those fixed by the statute, but the taking of higher wages was more severely punished than the giving them.

@art black-death
Marx dates it thirteen forty nine, the year of the royal Ordinance; Parliament enacted it in thirteen fifty one. Historians add the event behind it. The Black Death killed perhaps a third or more of England's people, labour grew scarce, wages rose, and the statute tried to hold them down. Yet real wages in the century after were among the highest of the pre industrial era.

@quote text="indeed a maximum of wages is dictated by the State, but on no account a minimum." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 28"
The law's aim and its effect are two questions. But its spirit is plain. Indeed a maximum of wages is dictated by the State, but on no account a minimum.

@chapter What a ceiling does
@math heading="A 12-hour day, 3s reproduced in 6 h" items="value created: 12 × 3s / 6 = 6s@six shillings;;at the value: (6s − 3s) / 3s = 100%@a hundred percent;;statute 2s: (6s − 2s) / 2s = 200%@two hundred;;combination 4s: (6s − 4s) / 4s = 50%@falls to fifty"
The lab computes what a ceiling does, with illustrative figures. A twelve hour day, labour-power worth three shillings and reproduced in six hours, so the day creates six shillings. Paid at the value, the rate is a hundred percent. Fix the wage by law at two shillings, and it's four over two, two hundred percent. If masons combine for four shillings, it falls to fifty.

@art wage-ceiling
A third off the wage doubles the rate, because the shilling taken from the wage moves to the other side of the ratio. And the masons' combination is exactly what the statutes declared void and punished.

@chapter Five centuries
@art statute-scroll
The statutes lasted. The Statute of Apprentices of fifteen sixty three let justices fix wages by season and prices. In seventeen ninety six, Whitbread proposed a legal minimum wage for farm labourers. Pitt opposed it. The wage laws went in eighteen thirteen, and the laws against combinations fell in eighteen twenty five, only in part.

@art trades-union-of-capitalists
Marx's verdict on five centuries is that Parliament had held the position of a permanent trades union of the capitalists against the labourers.

@words text="A ceiling, not a floor"
Wage laws you know today are the reverse: a minimum wage is a floor. Whether a floor helps or harms low paid workers is argued in another literature, which this lesson doesn't compute.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="A wage law protects with a floor@The first|A lower legal wage lowers labour-power's value@The second|The statute's text as what happened@And the third"
The first slip is thinking these wage laws protected the worker with a floor. They set a ceiling. The second is thinking a law that lowers wages lowers the value of labour-power. It moves the gap, and the rate. And the third is reading the statutes' text as a record of what happened.
