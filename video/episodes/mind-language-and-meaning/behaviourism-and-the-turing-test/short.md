title: What passing the Turing test actually shows

@art identical-rows
A perfect mimic can answer forever, and the judge learns nothing.

@math items="P(answer | human) = P(answer | machine)@equally probable;;Bayes factor = 1@a Bayes factor of one"
If every answer is equally probable from the human and the machine, each has a Bayes factor of one. The judge's belief never moves.

@words text="A pass measures imitation"
So passing shows the judge couldn't tell them apart. That's a fact about imitation. Whether the machine thinks is a further question.
