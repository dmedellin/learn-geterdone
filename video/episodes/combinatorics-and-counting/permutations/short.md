Five people, one round table. Why isn't the answer five factorial?

@math items="in a row: 5! = 120@a hundred and twenty;;each seating appears 5 times@five times;;120 / 5 = 24 = 4!@twenty four"
In a row, five people can line up in a hundred and twenty ways. But around the table, every rotation is the same seating, so each one was counted five times. Divide, and you get twenty four, which is four factorial.

@words text="Unless the chairs are numbered"
That division only holds when rotations count as the same. Number the chairs, and it's a hundred and twenty again.
