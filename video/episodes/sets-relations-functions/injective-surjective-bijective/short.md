title: The function that is one to one but not onto

@art shift-chain
Can a function from a set to itself be one to one, and still miss something? Yes.

@math items="f(n) = n + 1  on ℕ@n plus one;;nothing maps to 0@nothing maps"
Take n plus one, on the natural numbers. Different inputs always give different outputs. But nothing maps to zero.

@words text="Only infinite sets can do this"
On a finite set, one to one would force onto. That this function gets away with it is exactly what makes the naturals infinite.
