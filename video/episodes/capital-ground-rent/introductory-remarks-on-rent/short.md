title: Land gets dearer when nothing on the farm changes

@art price-seesaw
A field can rise in price by a quarter without a single change on the farm.

@math items="£200 at 5%: £4,000@five percent;;£200 at 4%: £5,000@four percent"
Marx's example: a rent of two hundred pounds a year. At five percent interest, the land's price is four thousand. Let the rate fall to four percent, and it's five thousand.

@words text="The price of the rent, not the land"
The rent didn't move. The price of land is just the rent, capitalised at the rate of interest.
