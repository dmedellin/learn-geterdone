title: The 1 you must not drop when factoring
---

@art leftover-one
Five x squared plus five x. Factor it, and there's a term people lose.

@math items="5x² + 5x@five x squared;;✗  5x(x)@five x times x;;5x(x + 1)@x plus one"
The common factor is five x. Divide each term by it. Five x squared gives x. But five x divided by five x leaves one. So it's five x times x plus one, not five x times x.

@art peeling-off
Expand to check. Five x times x alone is only five x squared. A whole term vanished.
