@art splitting-groups
Capital's last chapter asks what makes a class, and then the manuscript stops.

@math items="wages, profit, rent: 3 groups@three;;add physicians and officials: 5@five;;split the landlords: 7@seven"
Marx tries the obvious answer: a class is everyone living on the same source of revenue. That gives three groups. But physicians and officials make five. Split landlords by vineyards, farms, forests, mines and fisheries, and it's seven.

@words text="The test doesn't stop at three"
That's where the book breaks off.
