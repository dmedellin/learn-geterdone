@chapter Costs make the price?
@title text="Illusions Created by Competition" sub="Capital, Volume III, Chapter 50" art=thumb
You know the usual explanation of prices. Wages go up, so the firm's costs go up, so prices go up. It's how every business prices a job.

@art adding-up
Adam Smith put it carefully. A commodity's natural price is the sum of the natural rates of wages, profit and rent needed to bring it to market. Marx's claim is that this reverses the order of things.

@quote text="In reality the value of commodities is the magnitude which exists first, and it comprises the sum of the total values of wages, profit and rent, whatever may be their relative magnitudes." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 50"
In reality the value of commodities is the magnitude which exists first, and it comprises the sum of the total values of wages, profit and rent, whatever may be their relative magnitudes. In the wrong conception, they're three independent magnitudes that add up to the value.

@chapter Marx's 650
@math heading="A wage rise of 50" items="before: 400c + 100v + 150s = 650@four hundred constant;;after: 400c + 150v + 100s = 650@still six hundred;;profit and rent: 75 + 75 → 45 + 55@forty five"
His example: four hundred constant, a hundred variable and a hundred and fifty surplus value, six hundred and fifty. Wages rise to a hundred and fifty for the same labour, which adds the same two hundred and fifty. So the product is still six hundred and fifty. Surplus value is now a hundred, which Marx splits as forty five of profit and fifty five of rent.

@art working-day
In a ten hour day, the worker used to work four hours for himself and six for the capitalist. Now he works six for himself and four for the capitalist. The rate of surplus value falls from a hundred and fifty percent to sixty six and two thirds. The price doesn't move.

@chapter When wages do follow prices
@art less-productive
When does a wage rise go with a dearer product? When labour becomes less productive, each piece contains more labour, and wages may rise with it. But then the rise in wages is a result of the change in value, not its cause. And if only the worker's food gets dearer, the yarn's value doesn't change. The spinner pays more wages out of the same value.

@chapter Going round in circles
@words text="“The price of labor is determined by itself.”"
Now Marx takes the adding up view at its word. Wages are the price of the worker's necessaries. The price of those necessaries is made of wages, profit and rent. So the price of labor is determined by itself.

@art circle-arrows
Profit fares no better. Competition can equalise rates of profit, but can't say why the equal rate is ten percent rather than a hundred. His verdict: competition has to shoulder the duty of explaining all the economists' inexplicable ideas, when the economists should rather explain competition.

@chapter One figure, many divisions
@quote text="A given figure of 100 always remains the same, whether it is divided into 50 + 50, or into 20 + 70 + 10, or into 40 + 30 + 30." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 50"
A given figure of one hundred always remains the same, whether it is divided into fifty plus fifty, or into twenty plus seventy plus ten, or into forty plus thirty plus thirty.

@math heading="A wage rise of £20 in £200" items="wages: 100 + 20 = £120@a hundred and twenty;;surplus value: 200 − 120 = £80@eighty;;rent: 3/10 · 80 = £24@twenty four;;interest: 1/5 · 80 = £16@sixteen;;profit of enterprise: 80 − 24 − 16 = £40@forty"
The lesson's own illustration. A value product of two hundred: a hundred of wages, a hundred of surplus value, with rent three tenths of it and interest a fifth. Wages rise to a hundred and twenty. Surplus value falls to eighty. Rent becomes twenty four, interest sixteen, and profit of enterprise forty. Together they lost twenty, exactly what wages gained.

@chapter Why it looks that way
@art contract-first
Why does everyone see it the other way round? Even with competition removed, the illusion would remain, because the wages are stipulated by contract, before the value equivalent to them has been produced. Interest is fixed when the loan is made, rent when the lease is signed. To the capitalist each is a cost given in advance, and his own profit is whatever's left.

@quote text="To himself as a wage worker he pays his wages, to himself as a capitalist he turns over his profit, and to himself as a landlord he pays his rent." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 50"
It goes so far that a small farmer working his own land is counted as three people. To himself as a wage worker he pays his wages, to himself as a capitalist he turns over his profit, and to himself as a landlord he pays his rent.

@art fixed-bar
Piero Sraffa later derived the same inverse movement of wages and profit from physical inputs and outputs, with no labour values at all. And with paper money, a general rise in money wages can raise the price level, which the lesson doesn't model.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="A wage rise raises prices because wages are added@The first|The price of labour from the price of necessaries@The second|The illusion is a careless mistake@And the third"
The first slip is thinking a wage rise raises prices because wages are a component added to them. The second is explaining the price of labour by the price of necessaries. And the third is thinking the illusion is just a careless mistake. Contracts make it look that way to everyone.
