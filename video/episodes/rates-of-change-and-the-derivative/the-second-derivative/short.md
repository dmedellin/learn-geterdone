Here's a special point on a graph where the slope isn't zero at all.

@math items="p(t) = t³ − 3t²@t cubed;;p″ = 6(t − 1)@second derivative;;at t = 1:  p′ = −3@At one"
Take t cubed minus three t squared. Its second derivative is six times t minus one, which changes sign at one. At one, the slope is minus three. The graph is falling steeply.

@words text="Turns read p′. Bends read p″."
That's an inflection point, where the graph changes its bend. Turns come from p prime. Bends come from p double prime.
