@chapter Someone signs for you
@title text="Persons, Authors and Actors" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 16" art=thumb
Think of the last time someone signed for you: a parcel, a lease, a form at the doctor's. Someone acted, and your name was the one the signature carried. Hobbes builds a political theory out of that.

@art stage-mask
He starts with the word. Person is Latin. A persona was the disguise, the outward appearance, of a man on the stage, and particularly the mask. From the theatre the word moved into the courts. So a person is the same as an actor, on the stage and in ordinary life.

@list heading="Two kinds of person" items="Natural: his words are his own@his own|Artificial: his words represent another's@representing"
A person is someone whose words or actions are considered either as his own, or as representing someone else's. If his own, he's a natural person. If another's, he's feigned, or artificial. Nothing requires the one represented to be a human being.

@chapter Author and actor
@quote text="And then the Person is the Actor; and he that owneth his words and actions, is the AUTHOR: In which case the Actor acteth by Authority." cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 16"
Some artificial persons have their words and actions owned by those they represent. And then the person is the actor; and he that owneth his words and actions is the author: in which case the actor acteth by authority.

@art owner-and-agent
The word comes from property. What in goods is called the owner, in actions is called the author. And the right of doing an act is called authority. To act by authority is to act by commission from the one whose right it is.

@words text="No less than if he had made it himself"
Now the consequence. When the actor makes a covenant by authority, he binds the author no less than if he'd made it himself. But only as far as the commission goes, and no further.

@chapter The edge of a commission
@art lease-and-sale
Take the lesson's example. Ines owns a flat, and tells her agent Omar he may let it for a year. Omar signs a lease with Tobias: that's inside the commission, so Ines is bound. Then Omar signs a paper selling Tobias the flat. That's outside it, so Ines isn't bound. The sale binds Omar alone.

@art commission-box
So whoever deals with an actor without knowing his authority does it at his own peril. No man is obliged by a covenant of which he isn't the author. Ask to see the authority, and if it isn't shown, you're no longer bound. Evident authority binds the author. Feigned authority binds only the actor.

@chapter As an argument
@math heading="The full preset" items="p: the actor had authority@let p;;q: within his commission@q;;r: the author is bound@r;;p ∧ q → r,  p,  q  ∴  r   valid@valid"
The lab sets it out. Let p be: the actor had authority. Let q be: the covenant was within his commission. And r: the author is bound. Hobbes's rule is, if p and q, then r. Add p and q, and r follows. It's valid.

@math heading="When it fails" items="no authority: p false → one row with r false@Without authority;;beyond commission: q false → one row with r false@beyond;;r → p ∧ q,  ¬p  ∴  ¬r   valid@other sentence"
Without authority, p is false, and the lab finds one row where the author isn't bound. Beyond the commission, q is false, and again one row fails. Those show only that the rule can't make him bound. To show he's not bound, you need Hobbes's other sentence: no man is obliged by a covenant he didn't author.

@chapter Bridges and commands
@art bridge-overseer
Things that can't own an act, a church, a hospital, a bridge, can be personated by an overseer. But inanimate things can't be authors, so the authority comes from their owners. Children and the mad can be represented by guardians too. And none of this has a place before a civil state.

@words text="Not he, but the Author breaketh the Law of Nature"
One step further. If the actor does something against the law of nature on the author's command, and he was already bound to obey, then not he, but the author, breaks the law of nature. The act is the author's.

@art crowd-and-mask
Why does this matter for a commonwealth? Hobbes wrote in a civil war over who spoke for the people. In chapter nineteen he complains that petitioners to the king were called the people's representatives, and the king wasn't. This chapter decides what it would take to answer that.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Only the one who signed is bound@The first|Authority has no edge@The second|A person means a human being@And the third"
The first slip is thinking only the one who signed is bound. The author is. The second is thinking authority has no edge. It binds only as far as the commission. And the third is taking a person to mean a human being. For Hobbes, a person is a role.
