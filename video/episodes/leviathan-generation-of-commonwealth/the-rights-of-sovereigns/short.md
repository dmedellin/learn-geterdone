title: Hobbes's sovereign never signed the contract
---

@art vote-and-authorise
In Hobbes, the ruler can't break the social contract, because he never signed it.

@words text="Covenant only of one to another, and not of him to any of them"
The covenant is made by every subject with every other subject. The sovereign is the one they authorise, not a party.

@art held-in-trust
So no subject can be freed by the sovereign's breach. Locke denied the premise: power is held in trust, and can be withdrawn.
