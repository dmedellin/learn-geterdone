@chapter The limit
@title text="Transformational Leadership" sub="Accelerate, Chapter 11" art=thumb
For their first three years, the authors of Accelerate admit, they hadn't studied leadership directly. The twenty seventeen survey is where they set out to measure it. And their answer comes with a limit.

@quote text="we found evidence that leaders alone cannot achieve high DevOps outcomes." cite="Forsgren, Humble and Kim, Accelerate, Chapter 11"
In their words: we found evidence that leaders alone cannot achieve high DevOps outcomes. Let's see the model behind that, and the evidence.

@chapter Why leaders matter
@art ripple
Leadership, the authors say, is about inspiring and motivating those around you. It isn't about who reports to you on an organization chart. And its effects on profitability, productivity and market share are indirect: they run through the technical and Lean practices that leaders back.

@art air-cover
They also own up to a fault in their community. DevOps people have sometimes maligned leaders, like middle managers who stand in the way. Yet leaders hold the authority and budget for large changes, can give a transformation air cover, and can change the incentives of whole groups.

@chapter Five dimensions
@list heading="Rafferty and Griffin, 2004" items="Vision@Vision is|Inspirational communication@Inspirational communication|Intellectual stimulation@Intellectual stimulation|Supportive leadership@Supportive leadership|Personal recognition@personal recognition"
The authors adopted five dimensions from Rafferty and Griffin. Vision is knowing where the organization is heading. Inspirational communication lifts people, even when the ground shifts. Intellectual stimulation pushes people to see problems afresh. Supportive leadership shows regard for people's needs and feelings. And personal recognition praises achievement, in person. They chose this model over servant leadership, which focuses on followers' development, because in other settings it predicts performance better.

@art rated-from-below
The questions ask a person to describe their own leader or manager, three for each dimension. So the score is the followers' report, not the leader's own account.

@chapter What they found
@list heading="The findings" items="High performers: strongest leaders, on all five@strongest|Bottom third: half the chance of high performance@half the chance|Highly correlated with employee NPS@Net Promoter"
High performing teams reported leaders with the strongest behaviours on all five dimensions, and low performers the lowest, and the differences were statistically significant. Teams whose leadership ratings fell in the bottom third had half the chance of being high performers. And transformational leadership was highly correlated with the employee Net Promoter Score.

@art amplifier
Now the surprise. Teams with the top ten percent of reported leadership were no more likely, and possibly less likely, to be high performers than teams overall. A leader can't reach goals alone. Teams must do the work, on a fitting architecture, with sound technical practices and Lean principles. Leadership amplifies those practices; it isn't a separate set of them.

@chapter Scoring a leader
@art six-ratings
The lab scores one invented leader, rated by six invented reports on a seven point scale, with two invented items per dimension. Each dimension has twelve answers.

@math heading="Preset ratings, twelve answers each" items="vision: 73/12 = 6.083@six point oh eight three;;communication: 64/12 = 5.333@five point three three three;;stimulation: 57/12 = 4.75@four point seven five;;support: 67/12 = 5.583@five point five eight three;;recognition: 36/12 = 3@thirty six over twelve"
Vision adds to seventy three, a mean of six point oh eight three. Communication, five point three three three. Stimulation, four point seven five. Support, five point five eight three. And recognition, thirty six over twelve, just three. The lowest is personal recognition.

@art five-bars
But the lab never grades a leader. A low dimension is a question for the people who gave the ratings, and a high mean isn't evidence that the team will perform.

@math heading="Preset later" items="recognition: 49/12 = 4.083@four point oh eight three;;still lowest: stimulation is 4.75@still the lowest"
Later, the same six answer again, and only the recognition items change. They now add to forty nine, a mean of four point oh eight three. It's still the lowest, since stimulation is four point seven five, but it's moved the most.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="The strongest leaders make the strongest teams@strongest leaders|Leadership means people report to you@org chart|The lowest dimension is a verdict@verdict"
Three slips. Thinking the strongest leaders deliver the strongest teams. Equating leadership with a place on the org chart. And treating the lowest dimension as a verdict. It's six people's perception of one person: a question to take back to them.
