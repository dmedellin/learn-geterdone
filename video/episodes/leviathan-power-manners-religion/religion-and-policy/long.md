@chapter Two sorts of gardener
@title text="Religion and Policy" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 12" art=thumb
Last time, Hobbes found four natural seeds of religion. Now he asks what people have done with them, and what makes a religion lose its hold.

@art two-gardeners
The seeds, he says, have been tended by two sorts of men. One sort ordered them by their own invention. The other did it by God's command. But both aimed to make people more apt for obedience, laws, peace, charity and civil society.

@list heading="Two sorts" items="Founders and lawgivers of the Gentiles: human politics@the Gentiles|Abraham, Moses and Christ: divine politics@divine politics"
The first sort includes all the founders and lawgivers of the Gentiles, and their religion is part of human politics. The second sort is Abraham, Moses and Christ, and theirs is divine politics.

@chapter Religion as policy
@art numa-nymph
The Gentile founders took three cares. They made people believe their precepts came from a god. Numa claimed the Roman ceremonies came from the nymph Egeria. The first king of Peru claimed he and his wife were children of the sun. They made what the laws forbade seem displeasing to the gods. And they prescribed ceremonies to appease them.

@words text="And thus you see how the Religion of the Gentiles was a part of their Policy."
So misfortune could be blamed on neglected worship, not on rulers. And thus you see how the religion of the Gentiles was a part of their policy. Where God himself planted religion, among the Jews, it was the other way round: the civil laws were part of religion.

@words text="Not invented, but delicate"
Hobbes isn't saying Christianity was invented by a lawgiver. He puts Christ in the second class. But contemporaries read his account as one of religion in general, and Bishop Bramhall, among others, attacked the book for it.

@chapter Four ways to lose faith
@art one-person-pillar
Every formed religion rests at first on a multitude's faith in one person, believed wise, working for their happiness, and holy. When those who govern it are suspected, the religion is suspected too, and without fear of the civil sword, rejected.

@list heading="Four causes of change" items="Wisdom: enjoining contradictories@wisdom|Sincerity: scandal@sincerity|Love: private ends@love|Calling: no miracles@miracles"
So there are four causes. Wisdom is lost by enjoining belief in contradictories. Sincerity is lost by scandal, signs the teachers don't believe it themselves. Love is lost when private ends are detected. And a divine calling needs miracles, true prophecy, or extraordinary good fortune.

@chapter The argument from contradiction
@quote text="For both parts of a contradiction cannot possibly be true: and therefore to enjoyne the beliefe of them, is an argument of ignorance;" cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 12"
Take the first. For both parts of a contradiction cannot possibly be true, and therefore to enjoin the belief of them is an argument of ignorance. That detects the author, and discredits him in all else he offers as revelation.

@math heading="The chain" items="c: he enjoins contradictories@let c;;g: his ignorance is detected@g;;d: he is discredited in all else@d;;c → g,  g → d  ∴  c → d   valid@valid"
Let c be: he enjoins belief in contradictories. Let g be: his ignorance is detected. And d: he's discredited in all else. If c then g, and if g then d, so if c then d. The lab checks all eight cases and finds none against it. It's valid.

@art broken-link
Both links are needed. Drop the second, and there's a case where a founder is shown ignorant and still believed on everything else. Run it backwards, from discredit to contradiction, and it's invalid. A teacher can be discredited three other ways.

@art scales-reason
So a defender of a mystery like the Trinity doesn't attack either link. He denies c, saying the doctrine is above reason, not against it. Hobbes's own sentence allows that: revelation may be of many things above, but of nothing against natural reason. The quarrel is over which side a doctrine lies.

@chapter Faith failing
@art golden-calf
Hobbes ends with examples. With Moses gone forty days, Israel made a golden calf. Miracles failing, faith also failed. Samuel's sons took bribes, and the people asked for a king. Justice failing, faith also failed.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="All religions were invented to control people@The first|Contradiction is the only way faith is lost@The second|The lab can say which doctrines are contradictions@And the third"
The first slip is thinking Hobbes says all religions were invented to control people. He divides the founders in two. The second is treating contradiction as the only cause. And the third is thinking the lab can say which doctrines are contradictions. The chain is valid however c is filled in.
