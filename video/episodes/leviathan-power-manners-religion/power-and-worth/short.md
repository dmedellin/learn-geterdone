The same man can be worth thirty seven, or twenty. Nothing about him changes.

@math items="war: general 37, judge 25@war;;peace: general 20, judge 34@peace"
In the lesson's example, a city at war prices a general at thirty seven and a judge at twenty five. A city at peace prices the general at twenty and the judge at thirty four.

@words text="Not the seller, but the buyer determines the price"
Hobbes's point: worth is price, and the buyer sets it.
