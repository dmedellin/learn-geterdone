title: The college rents that lost three quarters of their value
---

@art corn-third
In 1576, English colleges were made to take a third of their rents in corn.

@words text="Corn third ≈ 2 × the money two-thirds" sub="Blackstone, as Smith reports"
Two centuries later, Smith reports Blackstone saying that corn third brought near double the other two thirds.

@words text="Sunk almost to a fourth"
So the old money rents had sunk almost to a fourth of their value, with the coin barely changed. Silver itself had cheapened. Over centuries, corn held value better.
