title: Why barter breaks down at twelve goods
---

@art pairs-web
A market with twelve goods and no money has to quote sixty six prices.

@math items="no measure:  12 · 11 / 2 = 66@twelve times eleven;;one good as measure:  12 − 1 = 11@one good"
Every pair of goods needs its own rate: twelve times eleven, over two, is sixty six. Pick one good to price everything in, and you need only eleven.

@art early-moneys
That's the saving a common measure makes. Smith lists cattle, salt, shells and nails before metals won.
