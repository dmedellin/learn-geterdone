title: A hundred pounds repaid with a third of the silver
---

@art creditor-loss
Lend a hundred pounds, get a hundred pounds back, and lose about two thirds.

@math items="Edward I:  240 pence to a pound of silver@two hundred and forty;;Smith's day:  744 pence@seven hundred and forty four;;240 / 744 = 10/31@ten thirty firsts"
Under Edward the First, a pound weight of silver made two hundred and forty pence. In Smith's day, seven hundred and forty four. So each penny holds ten thirty firsts of its old silver.

@words text="Favourable to the debtor, ruinous to the creditor"
Same number of pence, much less silver. The creditor pays for it.
