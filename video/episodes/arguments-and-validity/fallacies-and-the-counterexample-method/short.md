title: It's raining, so it's not Tuesday

@art rainy-tuesday
It's raining or it's Tuesday. It's raining. So it's not Tuesday. Spot the problem?

@words text="It can be both."
It can be raining on a Tuesday. Both premises are true, and the conclusion is false.

@words text="Same shape, true premises, false conclusion"
That's the counterexample method. Copy an argument's shape with plainly true premises and a plainly false conclusion, and the shape is refuted.
