@chapter The square
@title text="The Square of Opposition and Existential Import" sub="Arguments and Validity" art=thumb
All unicorns are white. Is that true? And if it is, does it follow that some unicorns are white? The answer depends on a choice you might not know you were making.

@art the-square
Start with the traditional square of opposition. It arranges the four forms in a box. A, all S are P, at the top left. E, no S are P, at the top right. I, some S are P, at the bottom left. And O, some S are not P, at the bottom right.

@list heading="The relations" items="A and O, E and I: contradictories, exactly one true@contradictories|A and E: contraries, not both true@contraries|I and O: subcontraries, not both false@subcontraries|A to I, E to O: subalternation@subalternation"
The diagonals are contradictories: exactly one of each pair is true. A and E across the top are contraries: not both true. I and O along the bottom are subcontraries: not both false. And down each side runs subalternation: A true, so I true, and E true, so O true.

@chapter The Boolean reading
@art diagonals-hold
Now read the forms the Boolean way, as claims about regions. The contradictions hold in every case, because A empties the very region that O occupies, and E empties the region I occupies. Those two diagonals never fail.

@art empty-circle
But the rest fail on one pattern. Let nothing be an S. Then all S are P is true, and so is no S are P, so the contraries are both true. Some S are P and some S are not P are both false. And A is true while I is false, so subalternation fails.

@chapter All unicorns are white
@math heading="All unicorns are white ∴ Some unicorns are white" items="premise: unicorns outside white: empty@The premise empties;;Boolean: every region empty satisfies it@every region empty;;conclusion: unicorns inside white: occupied@the conclusion needs;;Boolean: fails there, invalid@So on the Boolean reading"
The premise empties the unicorns that aren't white. That region is empty, because no unicorn is anywhere, so the statement is true. The pattern with every region empty satisfies it. But the conclusion needs a white unicorn. So on the Boolean reading, the step from all to some is invalid.

@chapter Existential import
@art import-switch
The repair is called existential import. The Aristotelian reading assumes every term names something, so each circle has at least one occupied region. Then an A statement, which empties the outside of P, leaves only the overlap to hold the S. So the overlap is occupied, some S are P follows, and every relation on the square comes back.

@art trespassers-sign
So the reading is a choice, with a price on each side. The Boolean reading lets all trespassers will be prosecuted be true when there are no trespassers, and a law isn't shown false by a quiet year. The price is that the square collapses to its two diagonals.

@words text="All unicorns are white" sub="can't be said without saying there are unicorns"
The Aristotelian reading keeps the whole square. Its price is that it can't say anything universal about what may not exist, without also saying that it does. The logic can't tell a trespasser from a unicorn.

@chapter What the switch changes
@list heading="Run it both ways" items="Run it on the Boolean reading@run an inference|Run it with every term non-empty@every term non-empty|Compare the verdicts@Compare|State the assumption@state the assumption"
So run an inference on the Boolean reading. Then run it with every term non-empty. Compare the verdicts. If the step is invalid on the first and valid on the second, import is the extra premise doing the work, so state the assumption: assuming there are S.

@art contradictory-fixed
Some things never move. Contradictories hold on both readings. Converting no S are P is valid on both. Only the steps from a universal claim to a particular one change.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Thinking All S are P implies Some S are P@The first|Thinking Boolean means there are none@The second|Supposing the switch changes every verdict@the third"
The first slip is thinking all S are P implies some S are P. It does only if some S exists. The second is treating the Boolean reading as the claim that there are no such things. It doesn't say unicorns are absent. It declines to assume they're present. And the third is supposing the switch changes every verdict.

Here's one to try. All trespassers will be prosecuted, so some trespasser will be prosecuted. On which reading is that valid? Test it on both, and say what assumption carries the step.
