@art not-both-vs-neither
She's not both rich and famous. Does that mean she's neither?

@words text="rich, not famous" sub="not both: true · neither: false"
No. Suppose she's rich but not famous. Then not both is true, and neither is false.

@math items="¬(r ∧ f)  ≡  ¬r ∨ ¬f@not rich or not famous"
By De Morgan's law, not both means not rich or not famous. Negate both parts, and flip the and to or. Neither is a stronger claim.
