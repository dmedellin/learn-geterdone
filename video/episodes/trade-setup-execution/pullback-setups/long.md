@chapter The question
@title text="Pullback Setups" sub="Trade Setup & Execution" art=thumb
A trend is rising, and price has started to fall back. How far is too far? Is there a percentage that settles it? This lesson's answer is that no fixed percentage decides it. One structural fact does.

@words text="Depth is context" sub="the protected swing is the decision boundary"
Depth is context. The protected swing, and the evidence that the pullback is recovering, matter more than any retracement number. Volatility, timeframe and structure all change what deep means.

@words text="Process and measurement" sub="no live data, no recommendations, no signals"
As with the rest of this course, it's about process and measurement. It doesn't give live market data, personal recommendations, or trade signals.

@chapter Three requirements
@art impulse-leg
Trend first. A pullback setup needs an established impulse, a meaningful trend leg, and a protected swing. Without those, there's nothing to pull back from.

@art pullback-zone
Location second. The correction should reach a preplanned area, like prior breakout structure, a support or resistance zone, or another zone you marked in advance. Not just an arbitrary price where it happened to stop.

@words text="Touch · reaction · local shift · continuation"
And recovery third. How you enter depends on the model you chose: on the touch, on a reaction, on a local shift, or on continuation.

@chapter Two pieces of evidence
@art reaction-candle
The lab uses two kinds of recovery evidence, and defines both. A reaction candle is the first close back in the trend's direction from the pullback's low. For a short, it's from the pullback's high.

@art local-shift
A local structure shift is a close beyond the last minor high the pullback made on the way down. Or, for a short, beyond the last minor low it made on the way up. It's more evidence than a reaction candle, and it comes later.

@chapter Deep versus failed
@art shallow-vs-deep
Now the key picture. Here are two pullbacks, one shallow and one deep. Both still hold above the protected swing. So both are still valid pullbacks. The deep one isn't automatically worse.

@art failed-swing
But this one is different. Price has broken the protected swing. That's not a deeper pullback. It's a failed one, and the original thesis has to be reassessed. That single fact, the protected swing, separates the two, whatever the retracement percentage says.

@list heading="The lab's three controls" items="Retracement depth@retracement depth|Reaction candle@reaction candle|Local structure shift@local structure shift"
The lab gives you three controls. You set the retracement depth, then switch on a reaction candle, a local structure shift, or both. Watch what changes the verdict as you go. The lesson's point is that the protected swing and the recovery evidence matter more than the depth itself, so try a deep pullback with good evidence, and a shallow one with none.

@chapter The process
@list heading="Pullback setup process" items="Confirm the impulse@Confirm the impulse|Define the pullback area@Define the pullback area|Monitor validity@Monitor validity|Require recovery evidence@Require recovery evidence"
Confirm the impulse: the trend leg and the protected swing. Define the pullback area. Monitor validity: reject the original setup if the protected swing breaks. And require recovery evidence, using the exact reaction or structure shift rule in your model. Validity is something you keep checking while the pullback unfolds, not something you decide once and forget.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common mistakes" items="Entering because price moved far@The first|Calling structural failure a deeper pullback@The second|Entering at a location with no trigger@The third|One retracement percentage everywhere@And the fourth"
The first mistake is buying or selling because price moved far. Distance travelled doesn't prove the pullback has ended. The second is calling structural failure a deeper pullback. The third is entering at a location with no trigger. A zone can fail. And the fourth is using one retracement percentage in every market.

@words text="A zone can fail"
That third one deserves a moment. Reaching your planned area is only location. The zone can still give way. Your entry model decides how much confirmation you need before you act on it.

Here's the lesson's standard. For any depth and any evidence in the lab, say trade, wait, or no trade before you press Evaluate. And say which single fact separates a deep pullback from a failed one. You already know it: the protected swing.
