@chapter The question
@title text="Reversal Setups" sub="Trade Setup & Execution" art=thumb
A long uptrend prints a huge red candle. Is this the top? It's one of the most tempting calls in trading, and one of the most expensive to get wrong. This lesson replaces the guess with a sequence of evidence.

@words text="Reaction → break → retest → continuation"
The idea is to wait for evidence that control is changing, instead of predicting the exact top or bottom. A temporary countertrend reaction isn't a reversal. A reversal needs a break of prior structure, a retest, and continuation from the opposite side.

@words text="Process and measurement" sub="no live data, no recommendations, no signals"
As with the rest of this course, the lesson teaches process and measurement. It doesn't give live market data, personal recommendations, or trade signals.

@chapter Three requirements
@art mature-trend
First, a prior trend. A reversal setup starts with a mature trend approaching a meaningful location. Mark its protected swing, and the structural level that would signal it has lost control.

@art structure-break
Second, a structure change. The countertrend move has to break a structural level that mattered to the prior trend. Not just any level. One the trend was relying on, like the last higher low in an uptrend that buyers had been defending.

@art retest-swing
And third, new structure. A retest, and a swing on the opposite side, give you evidence that the other side is in control, and a usable point for invalidation.

@chapter The sequence
@art reaction-at-level
It starts with a reaction. At a meaningful area, look for rejection, a sweep, exhaustion, or a failure to continue. That's the first step, but it's only a reaction. Plenty of trends react, and then carry on as before.

@art big-candle-only
Then you need the break. A countertrend candle isn't enough, however large. The prior sequence of highs and lows has to be disrupted. This candle is big, but the structure it would need to break is still intact.

@words text="Opposite-side swing → invalidation" sub="then verify room to target"
Finally, the retest and continuation. Confirm an opposite side swing, define your invalidation from it, and verify there's room to the target. Only now is there a reversal candidate with a plan attached.

@words text="Not the exact top" sub="fewer false starts · a clear invalidation"
Yes, waiting means you won't catch the exact top or bottom. That's the trade off. Predicting the turning point increases false starts and leaves your invalidation unclear. Waiting for the sequence gives you a defined place to be wrong.

@chapter Hold the label
@list heading="Confirmation hierarchy" items="The prior side lost control@lost control|The opposite side built structure@established structure"
Reversal is a process. The exact labels vary from trader to trader, but the evidence should show two things. That the prior side lost control, and that the opposite side established structure.

@art failed-retest
And it can still fail. If price can't hold the reclaimed level on the retest, the reversal model may be invalid. A failed retest isn't a detail to ignore. It's the evidence telling you the old trend may still be in charge.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common mistakes" items="Every big countertrend candle is a reversal@The first|Entering before the structural break@The second|Ignoring a failed retest@The third|Mixing up reversal and pullback@And the fourth"
The first mistake is calling every large countertrend candle a reversal. Size alone doesn't show structure has changed. The second is entering before the break, which increases false starts and leaves invalidation unclear. The third is ignoring a failed retest. And the fourth is using reversal and pullback as if they meant the same thing.

@words text="Reversal ≠ pullback" sub="different context, triggers and failure conditions"
They don't. The two setups need different context, different triggers, and different failure conditions. Naming the setup correctly is how you know which rules apply.

@chapter Try it
@words text="Reaction? Break? Retest?" sub="say it at every step"
Here's the lesson's standard. Stop the lab at any step and say whether the chart shows a reaction, a structure break, or a retest. On the failed tab, choose wait until the break actually fails, not before. Waiting is the right answer there while the evidence is still incomplete. And choose reversal candidate only once the retest has formed the opposite side swing that gives the plan its invalidation.
