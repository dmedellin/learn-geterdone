@chapter A mist over Christendom
@title text="Spiritual Darkness from Misinterpretation of Scripture" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 44" art=thumb
Three Parts of the book have built one sovereign. Why go on? Because a claim abroad makes two sovereigns out of one. And in sixteen fifty one, after a war fought partly over bishops and presbyters, you didn't have to look far for it.

@art mist
Why has Christendom had so much war, almost since the apostles? Hobbes's answer is that it's night amongst us, or at least a mist, and we are therefore yet in the dark.

@quote text="the Kingdome of Darknesse, as it is set forth in these, and other places of the Scripture, is nothing else but a Confederacy of Deceivers" cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 44"
So the kingdom of darkness, as Scripture sets it forth, is nothing else but a confederacy of deceivers.

@art lamp-put-out
To get dominion over men in this present world, they try by dark and erroneous doctrines to put out the light of nature and of the Gospel. It isn't a place. It's people, and what they want is dominion.

@list heading="Four causes of darkness" items="Abusing the Scriptures@abusing|The heathen poets' demonology@demonology|Greek vain philosophy@philosophy|False tradition and feigned history@tradition"
The enemy works four ways: by abusing the Scriptures, by bringing in the demonology of the heathen poets, by the vain philosophy of the Greeks, and by false tradition and feigned history. This chapter takes the first.

@chapter The main abuse
@words text="The kingdom of God = the present church?"
The greatest abuse, to which almost all the rest are consequent, is wresting Scripture to prove that the kingdom of God is the present church.

@art crown-in-waiting
Hobbes's reply has three steps. God's kingdom by pact began with Moses, over the Jews only, and ended when they chose Saul. Since then God is king as he always was, by his power. And he promised to restore his kingdom through his Son.

@words text="Which second coming not yet being, the Kingdome of God is not yet come"
Which second coming not yet being, the kingdom of God is not yet come. And we're not now under any other kings by pact, but our civil sovereigns.

@chapter The argument in letters
@math heading="Three letters" items="k: the present church is the kingdom of God@let k;;c: the kingdom of God has come@c is;;s: the second coming has happened@and s;;k → c,  c → s,  ¬s  ∴  ¬k@not k"
Write it as an argument. Let k be: the present church is the kingdom of God. c is: the kingdom has come. And s: the second coming has happened. If k then c. If c then s. Not s. So, not k.

@math heading="Counting the rows" items="¬s: 4 rows left@four rows;;c → s, s false: c false: 2 rows@two rows;;k → c, c false: k false: 1 row@one row;;no counterexample: valid@valid"
Count the rows by hand. Not s leaves four rows. If c then s, with s false, needs c false: two rows. If k then c, with c false, needs k false: one row. There, not k is true. No counterexample, so it's valid.

@art missing-link
Now remove each premise. Drop if c then s, and there's a counterexample: the church is the kingdom, the kingdom has come, the second coming hasn't. Drop if k then c, and it fails again. Each premise is needed. But if k then c is hardly in dispute. The middle premise does the work.

@chapter Who denies it
@art geneva-and-rome
Cardinal Bellarmine held that Christ's kingdom is the church now on earth. Theodore Beza, Calvin's successor at Geneva, read Mark nine as saying it began at the Resurrection. And the Presbytery claimed power to excommunicate its own kings, no less than the Pope claims it universally. Both deny if c then s.

@words text="Thy kingdom come"
Hobbes answers from the Gospels. The apostles ask, wilt thou at this time restore the kingdom? Christians pray, thy kingdom come, which would be pointless if it were here. The weak point a critic would press is reading kingdom in the ordinary sense. If it's a spiritual rule over the faithful, the lab can't decide between them.

@chapter Consecration and the soul
@art consecrated-bread
The second abuse turns consecration into conjuration. To consecrate changes not the thing, but only its use. Hobbes reads this is my body as this signifies my body. Catholic theologians answered that substance is one thing and appearances another. He attacks that distinction in Chapter forty six.

@words text="Soul = the life, or the living creature"
The third error is that every soul is immortal by nature. In Scripture, Hobbes says, the soul always means the life, or the living creature. Eternal life is a gift of grace to the faithful. From the rejected doctrine, he says, come purgatory, walking ghosts, exorcism and indulgences.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Valid means good@The first|Hobbes denies God reigns now@The second|He denies eternal life@And the third"
The first slip is thinking valid means good. The middle premise is the whole quarrel. The second is thinking Hobbes denies God reigns now. He reigns by power. And the third is reading the chapter as denying resurrection or eternal life. He affirms both, for the faithful.
