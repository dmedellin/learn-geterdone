@chapter What philosophy is
@title text="Vain Philosophy and Fabulous Traditions" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 46" art=thumb
Hobbes starts, as always, with a definition. Philosophy is knowledge got by reasoning from how a thing is generated to its properties, or from its properties to some possible way of generating it, to produce the effects human life needs.

@art compass-and-figure
The geometer, who goes from the construction of a figure to its properties, is his model.

@list heading="Not philosophy" items="Experience@Experience|A false conclusion@false conclusion|Revelation@revelation|What books say@books"
So experience isn't philosophy. Nor is a false conclusion, since someone reasoning rightly in words he understands can never conclude an error. Nor revelation. And what's got from books is not knowledge, but faith.

@art idle-athens
Philosophy needs time. Leisure is the mother of philosophy, and commonwealth the mother of peace and leisure. The Greek schools began when Athens grew rich and idle men had nothing to do but talk. Plato taught in the Academy, Aristotle in the Lyceum, Zeno under the Stoa.

@words text="Rather a dream than science" sub="natural philosophy · moral · logic"
What use were they? Geometry owes them nothing. Their natural philosophy was rather a dream than science, their morals a description of their own passions, their logic captions of words. Bacon, Galileo and Descartes had attacked the schools too.

@chapter Is names nothing
@art universe-is-body
The universe is body, with length, breadth and depth. What isn't body is no part of it, so it's nothing, and nowhere. Spirits aren't nothing, because they have dimensions. Incorporeal is a name of more honour, fitter for God, devised to honour him, not to describe him.

@quote text="They are therefore no Names of Things; but Signes, by which wee make known, that wee conceive the Consequence of one name or Attribute to another" cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 46"
Then the word is. In a man is a living body, the is doesn't name a third thing, being. It only says: if he's a man, he's a living body. They're no names of things, but signs that we conceive the consequence of one name to another.

@art empty-labels
So entity and essence, which come from that verb, aren't names of things either. They're the names of nothing.

@chapter The lab's reckoning
@math heading="Definitions" items="body = dimension, one place@body;;man = body, lives@man;;soul = life@soul;;cannot add: dimension + incorporeal, one place + many@cannot be added"
The lab gives three definitions. A body is a dimension in one place. A man is a body that lives. A soul, as Scripture uses the word, is life. Dimension and incorporeal cannot be added, nor one place and many places.

@math heading="Hobbes preset: 8 claims" items="true: a man is a body; a soul is life@true;;false: a soul is a body@false;;absurd: a body is in many places; a man is incorporeal@absurd;;insignificant: entity, separated essence, nunc stans@insignificant"
Eight claims. A man is a body, and a soul is life, are true. A soul is a body is false, but nothing clashes. A body in many places, and a man incorporeal, are absurd. And a man is an entity, a soul is a separated essence, and eternity is a standing now, are insignificant, because those names were never defined.

@art empty-doublet
That's the lab's sorting, not Hobbes's. For him a false universal claim is absurd too. But change one definition. Make the soul life that is incorporeal, as the schoolmen do, and a soul is a body becomes absurd, not just false. Hobbes says the schools then have to say the soul is in a place definitive, not circumscriptive. Mere words.

@words text="Many places: now true" sub="by changing what a body is"
And let a body be in many places by definition, and that claim turns true. That's awkward for both sides. A definition can make the claim true, which is Hobbes's own lesson. What the schoolmen have done is change what a body is.

@chapter Why it matters for obedience
@art falling-stone
Heavy bodies fall, say the schools, because of heaviness, an endeavour to go to the centre of the earth. That's as much as to say bodies descend because they do. Why does it matter? So men aren't frightened from the laws with empty names, as birds are frightened from corn with an empty doublet, a hat and a crooked stick.

@words text="Men, not laws, govern?"
In a commonwealth, the measure of good and evil is the law, not private appetite. And what man with his natural senses doesn't find himself governed by those he fears? Aristotelians answered that terms like essence answer real questions. That's the quarrel the lab can show, but not decide.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Insignificant means false@The first|Hobbes rejects all philosophy@The second|The lab proves incorporeal is meaningless@And the third"
The first slip is taking insignificant for false. An insignificant claim has no truth value at all. The second is thinking Hobbes rejects philosophy, or all of Aristotle. And the third is reading the lab as proof. Define incorporeal otherwise, and the verdicts change.
