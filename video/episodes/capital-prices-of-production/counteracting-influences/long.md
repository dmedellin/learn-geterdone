@chapter Why so slowly?
@title text="Counteracting Influences" sub="Capital, Volume III, Chapter 14" art=thumb
Marx turns the question round. Looking at the mass of machinery industry had added in thirty years, the puzzle isn't why the rate of profit falls. It's why it falls so slowly.

@quote text="There must be some counteracting influences at work, which thwart and annul the effects of this general law, leaving to it merely the character of a tendency." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 14"
That's why he calls it a tendency to fall.

@art six-checks
His answer is a list of six. Raising the intensity of exploitation. Pushing wages below their value. Cheapening the elements of constant capital. Relative overpopulation. Foreign trade. And the increase of stock capital.

@chapter Raising exploitation
@art longer-day
The first is the one the lab computes. A longer or more intense working day raises the rate of surplus value, and lengthening the day does it without adding constant capital.

@math heading="300c + 100v at 100% → 500c + 100v" items="start:  100 / 400 = 1/4@a quarter;;s′ still 100%:  100 / 600 = 1/6@a sixth;;s′ at 150%:  150 / 600 = 1/4@still a quarter"
Start at three hundred c and a hundred v at a hundred per cent: a quarter. After four periods c is five hundred. At the old rate of surplus value, the rate of profit would be a sixth. But let s prime rise to a hundred and fifty per cent, and it's still a quarter. All of the fall is offset.

@words text="Checked, retarded, weakened"
Marx is careful about what this proves. A rise in the rate of surplus value doesn't suspend the law. It makes it more of a tendency, a law whose enforcement is checked, retarded, weakened. And he argues it can't offset the fall without limit, because fewer workers' surplus labour is bounded by their working day.

@chapter Cheaper constant capital
@art cheaper-cotton
The third check is the lab's second case. The same productivity that multiplies the cotton a spinner works up also cheapens the cotton. In exceptional cases, Marx says, the mass of constant capital may grow while its value stays the same or even falls.

@math heading="c cheapens: rises 25 a period, not 50" items="end:  400c + 100v;  p′ = 1/5@a fifth;;offset:  10/3 of 25/3 points@ten thirds"
Let c's value rise by twenty five a period instead of fifty, because its elements cheapen. It ends at four hundred c and a rate of a fifth. That offsets ten thirds of a point, out of a fall of twenty five thirds: two fifths of the fall.

@art held-rate
Combine them, with s prime rising half as fast, to a hundred and twenty five per cent, and c cheapening as before. Neither alone holds the rate. Together they hold it at twenty five per cent.

@chapter The other checks
@art two-edges
The others the lab doesn't separate. Overpopulation keeps labour cheap enough for old labour intensive trades to survive. Foreign trade cheapens necessities and materials. Stock capital takes only interest and stays out of the average. And several cut both ways: foreign trade also speeds accumulation. The law is not suspended, but its effect is weakened.

@words text="Because labour becomes more productive"
Near the chapter's end comes a sentence that sums it up. The rate of profit doesn't fall because labour becomes less productive, but because it becomes more productive.

@chapter Okishio's theorem
@art okishio-grid
Here the strongest modern objection belongs. In nineteen sixty one, Nobuo Okishio proved that if capitalists adopt a technique only when it cuts costs at current prices, and the real wage stays the same, the general rate of profit can't fall. If it does fall, he concluded, it's because real wages rose.

@words text="The fall comes later" sub="when competition generalises the method"
There are replies. Marx himself says no capitalist adopts a method that lowers his rate; the fall comes later, when competition generalises it.

@words text="Shaikh · Kliman · Freeman"
Shaikh argued competition with fixed capital pushes firms to cut unit costs even at a lower rate. The temporal single system school argued Okishio's result depends on valuing inputs and outputs at the same prices. The lab can't say which sequence an economy follows.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="The checks refute the law, or don't matter@The first|They're separate from the causes@The second|More exploitation lowers the rate@And the third"
The first slip is treating the counteracting influences as either a refutation or mere exceptions. They make the fall a tendency. The second is thinking they're separate from the causes of the fall. They come from the same development. And the third is reading a higher rate of exploitation as a lower rate of profit. By itself, a rise in s prime raises it.
