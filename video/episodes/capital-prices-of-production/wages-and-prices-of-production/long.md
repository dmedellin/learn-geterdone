@chapter A common first thought
@title text="Wage Changes and Prices of Production" sub="Capital, Volume III, Chapter 11" art=thumb
When wages rise across an economy, a common first thought is that every price must rise with them.

@art all-prices-up
It has a distinguished source. Adam Smith wrote that wages, profit and rent are the component parts of price, so raising one raises the whole. Ricardo disagreed: a rise in wages lowers profits, and changes relative prices. This short chapter is Marx's version of Ricardo's point, with prices of production.

@chapter The average sphere
@quote text="Let wages rise by 25%. Then the same quantity of labor, which was formerly set in motion with 20, costs 25." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 11"
Start from the average capital, eighty c and twenty v, at a general rate of twenty per cent.

@art same-forty
The labour bought is the same, so it still adds forty of new value. Wages take twenty five of it instead of twenty, and fifteen is left.

@math heading="80c + 20v, wages up 25%" items="80c + 25v + 15p = 120@a hundred and twenty;;new rate = 15 / 105 = 1/7 = 14 2/7%@one seventh"
So the average sphere's price is still eighty plus twenty five plus fifteen, a hundred and twenty, its value. The new rate is reckoned on the capital as it now stands, a hundred and five. Fifteen over a hundred and five is one seventh, fourteen and two sevenths per cent.

@chapter Above and below
@art low-composition
Now a sphere below the average, fifty c and fifty v, which sold at a hundred and twenty before. Its wages rise to sixty two and a half, and its cost price to a hundred and twelve and a half. It must now earn a seventh on that.

@math heading="New rate 1/7: multiply cost price by 8/7" items="50c + 62½v:  112½ · 8/7 = 900/7 = 128 4/7@up;;92c + 10v:  102 · 8/7 = 816/7 = 116 4/7@down"
Adding a seventh is multiplying by eight sevenths. The low composition sphere now sells at a hundred and twenty eight and four sevenths, up. A sphere above the average, ninety two c and eight v, has wages rise to ten, a cost price of a hundred and two, and sells at a hundred and sixteen and four sevenths, down.

@art high-composition
The low composition's price rises, but not in the same proportion as the profit falls. The high composition's price falls, but not as much as the profit.

@chapter Why prices move
@art three-directions
Why do prices move at all? Because the rate must be equal everywhere. At the old price, the low composition sphere would earn seven and a half on a hundred and twelve and a half, six and two thirds per cent, far below the new average. Prices move until each earns a seventh again.

@art totals-hold
And the totals still hold. Take two spheres that together have the average composition, seventy c and thirty v, and ninety c and ten v. After the rise, their prices move up and down by exactly the same amount, and still sum to two hundred and forty.

@chapter Wages falling
@math heading="Wages fall by a quarter" items="average:  80c + 15v + 25p;  rate 25/95 = 26 6/19%@twenty five on ninety five;;50c + 37½v → 2,100/19 = 110 10/19@low composition falls;;92c + 6v → 2,352/19 = 123 15/19@high composition rises"
Marx then reverses the case, which he notes Ricardo didn't analyse. Let wages fall by a quarter. The rate rises to twenty five on ninety five. The low composition falls to a hundred and ten and ten nineteenths. The high composition rises to a hundred and twenty three and fifteen nineteenths. Every direction is reversed.

@words text="Paid out of profits, not passed on"
The lesson for a reader of the news is narrow but sharp. On this accounting, a general wage rise is paid out of profits. It shifts relative prices without raising the price level. The lab knows nothing of money or monetary policy, which is where most modern arguments about wages and prices live.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="A wage rise raises all prices@The first|The new rate on the old capital@The second|The printed fractions are exact@And the third"
The first slip is thinking a general wage rise raises all prices. The second is reckoning the new rate on the old capital. It's fifteen on a hundred and five, not on a hundred. And the third is taking the text's rounded figures as exact. The lab prints nine hundred sevenths.
