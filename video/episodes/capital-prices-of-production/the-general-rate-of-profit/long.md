@chapter A stock company
@title text="The General Rate of Profit and Prices of Production" sub="Capital, Volume III, Chapter 9" art=thumb
The last lesson ended with equal capitals earning unequal rates at value, which no real economy shows. This chapter is Marx's answer, and the book's most argued over step.

@art stock-company
Treat the whole social capital as one capital, and its parts as shares. The various capitalists, so far as profits are concerned, are so many stockholders in a stock company, each drawing profit in proportion to the shares held.

@art pooled-surplus
The total dividend is the total surplus value. What changes is who receives it.

@chapter Five spheres
@art five-spheres
Marx's table has five spheres of a hundred each, all at a hundred per cent: eighty c and twenty v, seventy and thirty, sixty and forty, eighty five and fifteen, and ninety five and five.

@math heading="Five spheres of £100 · s′ = 100%" items="390c + 110v = 500@five hundred;;s = 110@a hundred and ten;;general rate = 110 / 500 = 22%@twenty two"
Together that's three hundred and ninety c and a hundred and ten v. A hundred and ten of surplus value on five hundred gives a general rate of twenty two per cent.

@words text="Price of production = cost price + 22% of the whole capital"
Each sphere now sells at its cost price, plus twenty two per cent on the whole capital advanced, used up or not. That's its price of production.

@chapter With fixed capital
@math heading="Used-up c: 50, 51, 51, 40, 10" items="cost prices:  70, 81, 91, 55, 15@cost prices are"
Only the worn part of fixed capital enters this year's product. The spheres use up fifty, fifty one, fifty one, forty and ten of their c. So cost prices are seventy, eighty one, ninety one, fifty five and fifteen.

@math heading="Values and prices" items="values:  90, 111, 131, 70, 20@Values are;;prices:  92, 103, 113, 77, 37@Prices are"
Values are ninety, a hundred and eleven, a hundred and thirty one, seventy and twenty. Prices are ninety two, a hundred and three, a hundred and thirteen, seventy seven and thirty seven.

@art above-below
Sphere five, with a large capital and a small cost price, gains most: thirty seven against a value of twenty. It produced five of surplus value and receives twenty two of profit. Spheres two and three sell eight and eighteen below value.

@words text="Above average composition → above value"
The pattern is plain. Capital of higher composition sells above value, lower composition below, and average composition at value.

@chapter The totals
@art balance-totals
So does the law of value still hold? Marx's claim is that it holds for the whole. Values total four hundred and twenty two, and so do prices. Surplus value totals a hundred and ten, and so does profit. The deviations sum to nothing.

@words text="A never ascertainable average" sub="of ceaseless fluctuations"
Under capitalism, Marx writes, the law of value enforces itself as a never ascertainable average of ceaseless fluctuations.

@chapter The sentence to find
@art inputs-loop
There's one sentence every reader should find. A price of production becomes the buyer's cost price, so each sphere's inputs should themselves be at prices of production, not values.

@quote text="The foregoing statements are indeed a modification of our original assumption concerning the determination of the cost-price of commodities." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 9"
He grants the possibility of an error, says the totals must come out right across the country, and doesn't redo the table. The table, and the lab, value inputs at value.

@chapter The transformation problem
@words text="Böhm-Bawerk, 1896 · Bortkiewicz, 1906–07"
That's the transformation problem, at full strength. Bohm-Bawerk argued volume three contradicts volume one: if nothing exchanges at its value, value explains no actual price. Bortkiewicz transformed the inputs too, and found Marx's two totals can't in general both hold.

@words text="Samuelson · Steedman · New Interpretation · temporal single-system"
Samuelson and Steedman argued prices can be computed without values at all. The New Interpretation, and the temporal single system reading, redefine terms so that totals hold. This Subject doesn't take a side, and the lab does only what the chapter does.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Prices abolish the law of value@The first|Profit on the cost price@The second|The lab solved the problem@And the third"
The first slip is thinking prices of production abolish the law of value. Marx's claim is about the totals. The second is computing profit on the cost price instead of the capital advanced. Sphere five would get three point three, not twenty two. And the third is believing the lab has solved the transformation problem. It transforms outputs only.
