@chapter The question
@title text="Flashing Words Without Looking Back" sub="Reading Faster · The Inner Voice and Flashing Words" art=thumb
A word stream never lets you go back to a word. Is that just waste removed, or does it cost you something? This drill holds the rate fixed and changes only whether you can look back.

@art pacer-vs-stream
Both readings run at one rate, your baseline median. One story is read behind the pacer, a highlight moving line by line with the whole story on screen. A matched story is read in the stream, one word at a time. Each has six questions.

@art gone-word
The pacer keeps the page: your eyes can go back to a word whenever they need to. In the stream, each word replaces the last. A sentence you misread can't be checked, and the next words arrive whether you've repaired it or not.

@chapter A sentence that needs a second look
@words text="The dog walked past the gate barked."
Each story also hides one sentence built to be misread on the first pass. Linguists call it a garden path. In the dog walked past the gate barked, you take walked as what the dog did, until barked arrives.

@words text="the dog that was walked past the gate barked"
Then the sentence only makes sense as the dog that was walked past the gate barked. On a page, you can repair a sentence like that with a glance back. One question on each story depends on getting its sentence right.

@art garden-path
In the stream, there's no glance back. If you got that question right with the pacer and wrong in the stream, you may remember the moment: the sentence stopped making sense, and the next words were already coming.

@chapter Two readers
@math heading="Stream-costs preset, 300 wpm" items="pacer: 300 × 6/6 = 300@six of six;;stream: 300 × 4/6 = 200@four of six;;difference = −100@minus a hundred"
Here's a reader at three hundred words a minute both ways. With the pacer, six of six right: an effective rate of three hundred. In the stream, four of six: two hundred. The difference is minus a hundred, two questions, at the same rate. So the whole difference is in what was understood.

@words text="5 of 6 both ways · no difference" sub="even preset"
The even preset is a reader with five of six both ways: two fifty and two fifty. That's a real possibility. Two stories aren't a laboratory, and a reader who misreads neither garden path, or both, sees no gap.

@chapter What going back is for
@art x-mask
Schotter, Tran and Rayner, in twenty fourteen, tested what going back is for. As people read, each word was replaced by x's as soon as the eyes left it. A reader could still look back, but would find nothing there.

@list heading="Schotter, Tran and Rayner (2014)" items="Lower comprehension with the mask@lower with the mask|For ordinary sentences too@ordinary sentences too|Normal regressions: understood about equally@about equally well"
Comprehension was lower with the mask, and not only for the ambiguous sentences. It was lower for ordinary sentences too. And in normal reading, sentences read with and without regressions were understood about equally well. That's what you'd see if readers go back when they need to.

@art small-repairs
So the garden path isn't where the cost is confined. It's where you can feel it. A stream removes every chance to repair, including the small, unnoticed ones. And Acklin and Papesh, in twenty seventeen, found the apps that use it lowered comprehension at speed.

@chapter Reading your own
@art one-pair
If your stream came out as good as the pacer, or better, you haven't refuted the study, and it hasn't refuted you. You read two stories once each. Their readers read many sentences, under careful control.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="RSVP is reading with the waste removed@The first|Every look back is a mistake@The second|Reading the garden-path question alone@And the third"
The first slip is thinking a word stream is reading with the waste removed. The looks back aren't waste. The second is thinking every look back is a mistake. And the third is reading the garden path question alone. One question is about sixteen point seven points, not a result.

@chapter Try it
@words text="300 wpm both · pacer 5 of 6 · stream 3 of 6" sub="stream minus pacer?"
Your turn. Both readings run at three hundred words a minute. With the pacer, five of six. In the stream, three of six. What's the difference in effective rate, stream minus pacer? You should get minus a hundred: two fifty against one fifty.
