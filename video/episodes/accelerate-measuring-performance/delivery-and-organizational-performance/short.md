title: What twice as likely really tells you
---

@art same-ratio
Twice as likely sounds huge. It might be a ten point difference.

@math items="60% vs 30%: ratio 2, difference 30 points@sixty percent;;20% vs 10%: ratio 2, difference 10 points@twenty percent"
Sixty percent against thirty is twice as likely, thirty points apart. Twenty percent against ten is also twice as likely, but only ten points apart.

@words text="Ask for the two shares"
A ratio alone can't tell you how big the gap is. You need the shares behind it.
