title: The half per cent that became eight per cent
---

@art a-and-b-bills
A half per cent commission sounds cheap. Charged six times a year, it isn't.

@math items="draws:  12 / 2 = 6@six times;;commission:  6 · ½ = 3%@three per cent;;with 5% interest:  8% a year@eight per cent"
In Adam Smith's account, traders kept renewing two month bills, drawn six times a year. A half per cent commission each time is three per cent. Add five per cent interest, and the money cost eight per cent a year.

@words text="Ordinary profit: 6% to 10%"
Against ordinary profits of six to ten, few projects could pay that.
