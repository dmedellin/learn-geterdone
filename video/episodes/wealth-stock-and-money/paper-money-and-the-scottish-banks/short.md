title: Why extra bank notes come straight back
---

@art returning-notes
A bank prints four thousand pounds of extra notes. Where do they go? Straight back.

@math items="absorbed:  £40,000@forty thousand;;issued:  £44,000@forty four;;reserve needed:  £14,000, not £11,000@fourteen thousand"
In Adam Smith's example, the country can absorb a bank's notes up to forty thousand pounds. Issue forty four, and the extra four thousand return for coin almost at once. So the bank needs fourteen thousand in its coffers, not eleven.

@words text="Paper can't exceed the gold it replaces"
Notes beyond the circulation earn nothing, and cost the bank coin.
