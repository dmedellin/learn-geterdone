@chapter The question
@title text="Choosing a Target Pace" sub="Reading Faster · Guided Practice" art=thumb
Practice with a pacer needs a target. And the useful one isn't the fastest you can follow. It's the fastest pace at which you still understand. This lesson finds it with a ladder.

@art ladder
The ladder is three passages, each read with the pacer, each at a faster target than the last. Your baseline rate, then a fifth above it, then a half above it. Each one is followed by six questions.

@math heading="Three rungs" items="base × 1, × 6/5, × 3/2@times six fifths;;240 → 240, 288, 360@two forty;;200 → 200, 240, 300@two hundred"
So the rungs are your baseline times one, times six fifths, and times three halves. From a baseline of two forty, they're two forty, two eighty eight and three sixty. From two hundred, they're two hundred, two forty and three hundred.

@chapter Testing the advice to push
@art push-arrow
The speed reading tradition's advice is to push. Set the guide faster than is comfortable, and keep raising it, on the view that understanding catches up with the pace. The ladder puts that to a test you can score.

@chapter Two figures
@list heading="Two figures from three rungs" items="Hold target: fastest rung with 4 of 6 or more@The hold target|Best run: highest effective rate@The best run"
Two figures come out of three rungs. The hold target is the fastest rung at which you answered at least four of six. It's the edge of the pace you still understand at. The best run is the rung with the highest effective rate. They answer different questions, and they can name different rungs.

@chapter Holds at 288
@math heading="Holds at 288" items="240 × 6/6 = 240@six of six;;288 × 4/6 = 192@four of six;;360 × 2/6 = 120@two of six;;hold: 288 · best run: 240@the hold target"
Here's a reader whose comprehension gives way as the pace rises. Six of six at two forty, four of six at two eighty eight, two of six at three sixty. The effective rates are two forty, one ninety two and one twenty. So the hold target is two eighty eight, and the best run is two forty, the slowest.

@art fewer-understood
At three sixty, this reader moved through the words half again as fast as at two forty, and took away half as many understood words a minute. Beyond the hold, the effective rate falls.

@chapter Holds at 360
@math heading="Holds at 360" items="240 × 5/6 = 200@two forty;;288 × 5/6 = 240@two eighty eight;;360 × 4/6 = 240@three sixty;;hold: 360 · best run: 240 at 288@a tie"
Now a reader for whom the pace costs little. Five of six at two forty and two eighty eight, and four of six at three sixty. The effective rates are two hundred, two forty and two forty. The hold is three sixty. The best is a tie, and the lab gives a tie to the slower rung.

@art tie-rungs
So the best run is two forty, at two eighty eight. The same understood words a minute, for less effort, is the better run. And once again, the hold and the best run are different rungs.

@chapter Why practise at the hold
@art edge
So why train at the hold, not the best run? They serve different purposes. For a reading you need to understand today, read at the best run's pace. For practice, the idea is to work at the edge: faster than comfortable, but not so fast that the words go by unread.

@words text="No promise" sub="your log will answer"
Does practice at that edge raise your natural rate? That's a question your log will answer. This course doesn't promise that it will.

@chapter Reading your own ladder
@art two-ends
If you got four or more on every rung, the ladder didn't find your edge, so next time start at your top rung. If you got fewer than four even at your baseline, take the hold as your baseline rate for now. And one question is about sixteen point seven points, so a rung with three may have been one hard question away.

@words text="Find the hold again on harder text"
The hold also depends on the text. On easy passages it'll be higher, on hard ones lower. When the Daily Drill's pool reaches the hard passages, find the hold again.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Setting the pacer as fast as you can follow@The first|Training at the best run@The second|Treating the hold as fixed@And the third"
The first slip is setting the pacer as fast as you can follow it. Following a highlight is not the same as reading behind it. The second is taking the best run as the training target. And the third is treating the hold as fixed.
