@chapter One box, two sides
@title text="Intentionality and Rationality" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Preface" art=thumb
Picture a cereal box on the table between you and someone sitting opposite.

@art cereal-box
You see the front, with its picture. They see the back, with the ingredients. You both see the top. Neither of you has seen the box whole, and neither of you doubts it's one box. If they read out the ingredients, you don't check against a view from nowhere. There's no such view.

@chapter Consciousness of something
@words text="All consciousness is consciousness of something"
The Preface gets there by way of intentionality, and it starts by deflating the idea. All consciousness is consciousness of something. There's nothing new in that. It came to Husserl from Brentano, in eighteen seventy four, and further back from Kant.

@words text="The world's unity is lived as already there"
What's new is this. The world's unity is lived as ready made, already there, before knowledge sets it down as an object in a specific act.

@chapter Two intentionalities
@list heading="Two kinds" items="Of acts: judgements and positions we deliberately take@judgements|Operative: the world and our life one, before any judgement@operative intentionality"
From there Husserl draws the distinction the whole book uses. There's the intentionality of acts: our judgements, and the positions we deliberately take. And there's operative intentionality, which makes the world and our life one before any judgement.

@art landscape
It shows itself in our desires, our evaluations, and the landscape we see. It furnishes the text that our knowledge tries to translate into precise language. Knowledge translates it. It doesn't produce it.

@chapter Where views cross
@words text="History does not think with its feet"
The Preface widens this to history with a borrowed joke. As Marx says, history doesn't walk on its head. It's also true that it doesn't think with its feet. What matters is its body. Every view of an event is true, provided it isn't isolated. Followed deep enough, the views reach one core of meaning.

@quote text="To say that there exists rationality is to say that perspectives blend, perceptions confirm each other, a meaning emerges." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Preface, p. xxii"
Now the definition. Three verbs: blend, confirm, emerge. None of them is a verdict from outside. There's exactly as much rationality as there is agreement among the experiences.

@art paths-intersect
The sense isn't in a realm apart, not in Hegel's absolute Spirit, and not in a world in itself behind every view. It's where the paths of my experiences intersect.

@art meshing-gears
And where mine and other people's intersect too, engaging each other like gears.

@chapter The idealist's reply
@words text="Who checks the views?"
The idealist has a strong reply. If every view is only a view, nothing among them can tell true from false. So a consciousness that holds them all together must be the condition of their agreeing.

@words text="An act of initiative which has no guarantee in being"
The Preface's answer is that the philosopher, the impartial spectator, doesn't rediscover a rationality already given. It establishes it, by an act of initiative which has no guarantee in being.

@chapter Three viewers
@art cube-viewers
The lab puts the views on a cube. One viewer looks from front, right and above, along one, two, two. A second looks from back, left and above. A third looks straight at the front.

@math heading="Three views" items="1 + 4 + 4 = 9, so length 3@one plus four;;first: right 1/3, above 2/3, front 2/3@first view;;second: left 2/3, above 1/3, back 2/3@second view;;third: front 1@third view;;together: 5 of 6 faces@together"
One plus four plus four is nine, so the first direction's length is three. The first view sees the right at a third of its true area, the top and front at two thirds. The second view sees left at two thirds, top at a third, back at two thirds. The third view sees the front whole. Together: five of six faces.

@art unseen-bottom
The top is seen twice, drawn differently, and it's one top. Nobody sees the bottom, and the cube is no less a cube. But when the lab flags a report that sees below from above, it checks against a cube it holds complete, from nowhere. A perceiver never has that.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="Rationality needs a view above all views@above all views|Operative intentionality is a quick judgement@quick judgement|Unfinished means failed@failed"
Don't say rationality needs a view above all views. Don't say operative intentionality is a quick judgement: it comes before judgement. And don't say unfinished means failed. True philosophy consists in relearning to look at the world.
