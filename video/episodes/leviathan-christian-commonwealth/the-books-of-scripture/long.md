@chapter The letter and the bridge
@title text="The Books of Holy Scripture" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 33" art=thumb
A friend hands you a letter dated nineteen hundred, which says the new bridge still stands to this day. You'd look up when the bridge was built. Hobbes does the same with the Bible.

@list heading="Three starting points" items="History is the only proof of fact@History|Reason proves consequence, not fact@Reason|Titles mark the subject as often as the writer@titles"
No other history tells us who wrote these books, and history is the only proof of matter of fact. Reason won't do it either, since it proves consequences, not facts. And titles prove little, because a title marks the subject as often as the writer. The Book of Ruth isn't by Ruth.

@chapter The grave of Moses
@quote text="no man knoweth of his sepulcher to this day, that is, to the day wherein those words were written." cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 33"
So the books themselves are left. The last chapter of Deuteronomy says no man knoweth of his sepulchre to this day, that is, to the day those words were written. So they were written after his burial.

@art grave-unknown
It would be a strange reading, Hobbes says, to have Moses speak of his own grave by prophecy. And if someone says only the last chapter is later, Genesis says the Canaanite was then in the land, which is written by someone for whom the Canaanite was gone. Moses died before entering.

@art stone-tablets
His conclusion is moderate. The five books were written after Moses's time, though how long after isn't clear. Moses did write what the books say he wrote, the volume of the law in Deuteronomy, read every seventh year, laid in the ark, lost, and found again by Hilkiah.

@art scroll-and-later-hand
What Hobbes denies is only that Moses compiled the five books in the form we have them. There was a first hand, and a later one.

@chapter Unto this day
@art twelve-stones
The phrase unto this day signifies a time past, beyond the memory of man. He finds it in Joshua, for the twelve stones set up in the Jordan. In Judges, and in Samuel. And Kings and Chronicles run down to the captivity in Babylon, and the facts registered are always older than the register.

@chapter The lab's test
@words text="Earliest date written = latest event mentioned"
The lab makes this a calculation. Give each book a traditional author and his death, and the events it mentions. A book can't be written before the latest thing it mentions.

@art timeline-gap
If that's after the author's death, the book as it stands isn't wholly his. The years are the lab's round figures. Hobbes gives none.

@math heading="Moses and Joshua" items="Pentateuch: latest event 30; Moses died year 0@thirty;;30 > 0: written after@written after;;Joshua: latest event 200; Joshua died year 30@two hundred;;gap: 200 − 30 = 170@a hundred and seventy"
Count years from Moses's death. The Pentateuch mentions an event at year thirty, the Canaanite gone. Moses died at year zero, so it's written after. Joshua mentions the stones unto this day at year two hundred. Joshua died at year thirty, so that's a gap of a hundred and seventy years.

@math heading="Samuel and the Psalms" items="Samuel: reaches 700; died 400: gap 300@seven hundred;;Psalms: return from Babylon 868; David died 440: gap 428@eight sixty eight;;Job: nothing dated: no test@Job"
Samuel reaches year seven hundred, three hundred years after his death. The Psalms include a psalm of the return from Babylon, year eight sixty eight, four hundred and twenty eight years after David died. And Job mentions nothing dated, so there's no test. That's not a finding that Job is early.

@chapter By what authority?
@art sovereign-seal-on-book
The second half turns to authority. God is to be obeyed, Hobbes agrees. But the question is what God has said, and a subject without his own revelation can know that only by obeying his commonwealth. So it isn't the writer, but the authority of the church, that makes a book canonical.

@words text="By what authority they are made law"
And the church, if it's one person, is the commonwealth of Christians. The question truly stated is by what authority the Scriptures are made law. Answer the Pope, or the Spirit in me, and you're making a claim about who is sovereign. Many readers accept his dating and reject all of this.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="The test shows who wrote it@The first|Hobbes says Moses wrote nothing@The second|The authority argument is about dates@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the test shows who did write the book. It only gives a date. The second is thinking Hobbes denies Moses wrote anything. And the third is reading the authority argument as another dating argument. It's about law.
