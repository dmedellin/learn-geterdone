title: The pound that never leaves the country

@art local-garbs
When a country pays its debts abroad, its pounds stay at home.

@math items="£1,000 − £800 = £200@two hundred pounds;;+ £100 interest = £300 in gold@three hundred"
Import a thousand pounds of goods, export eight hundred, and two hundred pounds is owed abroad. Add a hundred of interest, and three hundred must be shipped.

@art bullion-by-weight
But it isn't shipped as pounds. Abroad, Marx says, money strips off its local names and returns to gold, counted by weight.
