@chapter Two letters, four sounds
@title text="C and G Before E, I and Y" sub="Spelling to Sound" art=thumb
Cat and city. Go and gentle. The letters c and g each have two sounds. Here's the rule that chooses between them, and how often it's right.

@art letter-fork
For c, the two sounds are k, as in cat, and s, as in city. For g, they are the hard sound in go, and the sound at the start of gentle. That's the same sound that starts the word judge.

@chapter The rule
@art next-tile
The rule you've probably been given looks at one letter: the letter after the c or the g. Before e, i or y, the letter is soft. Anywhere else, it's hard. And if the c or the g is the last letter, nothing comes after it, so it's hard.

@math heading="The next letter chooses" items="city, cell  ·  c before i, e  →  s@city and cell;;cat, cold, cup  ·  c before a, o, u  →  k@Cat, cold;;gentle, giant  ·  g before e, i  →  j  /dʒ/@Gentle and giant;;go, good  ·  g before o  →  hard g  /ɡ/@go and good"
So city and cell begin with s. Cat, cold and cup begin with k. Gentle and giant begin with the sound of judge. And go and good begin with the hard g.

@chapter What the lab counts
@art pair-tiles
The lab takes every word in the list of two thousand eight hundred that has a c or a g in it, and reads how it's said from a dictionary. But it only scores a word when that one letter is the only place the sound could come from.

@art set-aside
So it sets some words aside. A word with two c letters. A word where the c is part of a pair, like chair, or back. A word where another letter could make the sound. That leaves three hundred and forty-eight words for c, and a hundred and eighty-seven for g.

@chapter The c rule is perfect
@art full-bar
On the three hundred and forty-eight words, the c rule is right three hundred and forty-eight times. That's a hundred percent. Center, certain, face and price are said with s. Call, come, cut and cold are said with k.

@words text="ancient · ocean · official · racial …" sub="c said sh: set aside"
Eight words were set aside because the dictionary gives them both sounds, or neither. They include ancient, ocean, official and racial. In most of them, the c is said sh. That's a different rule, and a later lesson in this course takes it up.

@chapter The g rule fails ten times
@art bar-with-ten
For g, the rule is right a hundred and seventy-seven times out of a hundred and eighty-seven. That's ninety-four point seven percent. Gentle, giant, age, large and energy all follow it.

@list heading="The ten that break it" items="get · give · girl · gift · begin@are get|forget · gear · target · together · altogether@And forget"
The ten that don't are get, give, girl, gift and begin. And forget, gear, target, together and altogether. In every one, the g comes before e or i, and it's said hard. All ten are common words that you'll use every day.

@chapter Two rules, not one
@words text="c: apply the rule and stop" sub="g: apply the rule, then check ten words"
So the two rules look the same, but they're not the same kind of rule. With c, you apply the rule and stop. With g, you apply it, and then ask whether the word is one of the ten. Nothing goes wrong the other way: a g before any other letter is never said like judge.

@words text="sign · foreign" sub="set aside for g"
Two limits. The dictionary is American, and the lab reads only its first entry. And nine g words were set aside because the dictionary gives them both sounds or neither, sign and foreign among them.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="thinking the two rules are equally good@The first|scoring every word with a c or a g@The second|forgetting the y  ·  policy, energy@The third"
The first slip is thinking the two rules are equally good. The second is scoring every word that has a c or a g, even back, chair or sign, which don't test the rule. And the third is leaving out the y. The rule names three letters, and y counts like e and i: policy, agency, energy.

@chapter Try it
@words text="cell · get · giant · cold" sub="soft or hard?"
Your turn. Say the first sound of cell, get, giant and cold. Read the next letter each time. Cell has an e, so s. Get has an e too, but it's one of the ten, so it's hard. Giant is soft, and cold is k.
