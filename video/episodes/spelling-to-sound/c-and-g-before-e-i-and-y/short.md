One spelling rule is right every time. Its twin fails on ten common words.

@words text="c before e, i, y  →  s" sub="348 of 348 words"
Before e, i or y, c says s. City. Cell. In the lab, that's right on all three hundred and forty-eight words it can be tried on.

@words text="get · give · girl" sub="g before e, i: 177 of 187"
The same rule for g says get should start like judge. It doesn't. Get, give, girl and seven more common words break it.
