@chapter Letters you don't say
@title text="Letters You Do Not Say" sub="Spelling to Sound" art=thumb
Say knee. You don't say the k. Say write, and you don't say the w. English is famous for letters like these, and they can feel like one more thing to learn, word by word.

@art faded-tile
But the lab asks a plainer question. For each group of silent letters, how many words in the list follow a rule? Most groups follow rules with no misses at all. And one group has no rule. Let's take them one group at a time.

@chapter Never g
@art gh-fork
Take every word with a vowel followed by g-h. There are forty-two. The rule is simple: g-h is never said as g. And it's right on all forty-two.

@math heading="gh after a vowel: 42 of 42" items="silent  ·  35  ·  night, right, high, daughter, eight, through@In thirty-five;;f  ·  7  ·  cough, enough, laugh, laughter, rough, roughly, tough@In seven"
Read the rows, and two groups appear. In thirty-five words, g-h is silent: night, right, high, daughter, eight, through. In seven, it's said as f: cough, enough, laugh, laughter, rough, roughly and tough.

@words text="igh  →  always silent" sub="ough, augh  →  silent or f"
So a perfect score hides a choice. The rule stops you saying g, but it doesn't choose between nothing and f. After i, it's always silent. After o-u or a-u, check the word. Though, through and daughter are silent. Enough, laugh and tough say f.

@chapter Six places
@art six-places
The second rule covers six fixed places, and in each one, a letter drops out.

@list heading="One letter drops: 19 of 19" items="kn  ·  knee, know, knife, knock, knowledge@K-n at|wr  ·  wrap, write, writer, wrong@W-r is|mb  ·  bomb, climb@m-b drops|mn  ·  autumn, column, damn@M-n drops|l  ·  folk, talk, walk, calm, half@And the l"
K-n at the start is said n: knee, know, knife. W-r is said r: wrap, write, wrong. At the end of a word, m-b drops the b, in bomb and climb. M-n drops the n, in autumn and column. And the l is silent in folk, talk, walk, calm and half.

@art ghost-path
That's nineteen words, and the rule is right on all nineteen. Most of these letters were said once, long ago. The speech lost them, and the spelling kept them. But you don't need the history. You need the six places. Learn them, and you can find every silent letter in this group.

@chapter The letter h
@art h-row
The third rule is about an h at the start of a word, before a vowel. It's said. On eighty words, that's right seventy-seven times, ninety-six point three percent. Have, hair, hand and happy all say their h.

@words text="hour · honest · honor" sub="the three where h is silent"
The three misses are hour, honest and honor. They're three words to learn, and the other seventy-seven need no note. Outside the list, heir drops its h too, and in American speech, so does herb.

@chapter Ten words, five sounds
@art ough-five
The last group has no rule to score. Ten words in the list have o-u-g-h, and the dictionary gives them five different sounds.

@list heading="ough: 5 sounds in 10 words" items="though, although  ·  oh@Though and although|through, throughout  ·  oo@Through and throughout|enough, rough, roughly, tough@Enough, rough|cough@Cough has|ought@so does ought"
Though and although end with oh. Through and throughout end with oo. Enough, rough, roughly and tough share one sound. Cough has its own, and so does ought. No rule would help here. The honest answer is to count the sounds, and learn the words.

@chapter What is left to learn
@list heading="What is left to learn" items="gh  ·  the seven words that say f@For g-h|h  ·  hour, honest, honor@For h|ough  ·  ten words, five sounds@And for o-u-g-h"
So after the rules, what's left to learn? For g-h, which seven words say f. For h, just three words: hour, honest and honor. And for o-u-g-h, ten words and their five sounds. Everything else, the rules handle for you.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="thinking silent letters have no rules@The first|reading 42 of 42 as a full rule@The second|saying every ough one way@The third"
The first slip is believing English has no rules for silent letters. Most groups follow a rule every time. The second is reading a perfect score as a full rule. The g-h rule tells you what it isn't, not which of two sounds it is. And the third is saying every o-u-g-h the same way.

@chapter Try it
@words text="climb · hour · knife · enough" sub="which letter is not said?"
Your turn. Find the letter you don't say in climb, hour, knife and enough. Pause and try. Climb drops the b. Hour drops the h. Knife drops the k. And enough is a trick. Its g-h is said, as f.
