@chapter Endings said as one
@title text="-tion, -sion and -ture" sub="Spelling to Sound" art=thumb
Nation. Vision. Picture. Special. Many English words end in the same few groups of letters, and each group is said one way. Learn four endings, and you can say hundreds of words.

@art letters-merge
Here's the key idea. None of these endings is said the way its letters read. Each one is said as a unit, and the unit is the same in nearly every word.

@art ending-block
The lab scores each ending on the words in the list that have it. It checks every word against the first way an American dictionary says it, and it prints the words the rule gets wrong.

@chapter tion says shun
@words text="nation  ·  -tion = shun  /ʃən/" sub="123 of 127"
Start with t-i-o-n. It has four letters, and it's said shun. No t is said. A hundred and twenty-seven words in the list end this way, and the rule is right on a hundred and twenty-three. That's ninety-six point nine percent. Nation, station, action, attention.

@list heading="The four misses" items="question · suggestion  ·  the sound of church@Question and suggestion have|equation  ·  zhun@Equation has|intention  ·  the church sound, in the first entry@For intention"
The four that don't follow it are question, suggestion, equation and intention. Question and suggestion have an s before the t, and the dictionary gives them the sound at the start of church. Equation has zhun. For intention, the dictionary's first entry also has the church sound, though you may hear it said another way.

@words text="question · suggestion · digestion" sub="after s: the church sound"
Notice what question and suggestion share. After an s, t-i-o-n takes the church sound, and digestion, outside the list, does the same. Sort the misses, and a reason appears.

@chapter sion has two sounds
@art two-paths
Now s-i-o-n. It has two sounds, and the letter before it chooses. After a vowel letter, it's zhun. After a consonant, it's shun.

@math heading="The letter before -sion" items="decision, vision, television  ·  after a vowel  →  zhun  /ʒən/@After a vowel: decision;;tension, mission, session  ·  after a consonant  →  shun  /ʃən/@After a consonant: tension;;version  ·  r before it  →  zhun, the one miss@is version"
After a vowel: decision, vision, television, conclusion. After a consonant: tension, mission, session, permission. The rule is right on twenty-four of twenty-five words. The one miss is version. It has an r before the ending, and it's said with zhun.

@art before-letter
So the rule is one glance at the letter before the ending, plus one word to learn. American speech does the same in conversion, which isn't in the list. British speech says shun in both.

@chapter ture says chur
@words text="picture  ·  -ture = chur  /tʃər/" sub="19 of 20"
T-u-r-e is said chur. Of twenty words, nineteen follow it. That's ninety-five percent. Nature, picture, future, culture, temperature.

@art strong-end
The one miss is mature. Its last part is the strong part, said harder than the rest, so its vowel keeps a full sound, and the ending is choor. In the other nineteen, the ending is weak. The next course, Word Stress, is all about which part is strong.

@chapter cial and tial say shul
@words text="social · special · official · essential · potential" sub="-cial, -tial = shul  /ʃəl/  ·  12 of 12"
Twelve words end in c-i-a-l or t-i-a-l, like social, special, official, essential and potential. All twelve are said with shul. That's a perfect score, twelve of twelve. Three of them, financial, official and racial, were set aside in the first lesson of this course, because their c is said sh. Here, they fit the rule.

@chapter Four sounds, six words
@list heading="Four rules" items="tion  ·  shun  ·  123 of 127@T-i-o-n says|sion  ·  zhun or shun  ·  24 of 25@S-i-o-n says|ture  ·  chur  ·  19 of 20@T-u-r-e says|cial, tial  ·  shul  ·  12 of 12@c-i-a-l and"
So, T-i-o-n says shun. S-i-o-n says zhun or shun, by the letter before it. T-u-r-e says chur. And c-i-a-l and t-i-a-l say shul.

@art stacks-misses
That's a smaller job than it looks. You learn four sounds, and six words that break a rule: question, suggestion, equation, intention, version and mature. Then you can say a long list of words you've never heard.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="saying the ending as it is spelt@The first|using one sound for -sion@The second|thinking a miss means the rule is wrong@The third"
The first slip is saying the ending the way it's spelt: t, i, on. It's shun. The second is using one sound for s-i-o-n. Vision and tension look alike, and end differently. And the third is thinking one miss means the rule is wrong. A rule that's right ninety-five percent of the time is worth keeping.

@chapter Try it
@words text="station · division · culture · mission" sub="say each ending"
Your turn. Say the endings of station, division, culture and mission. Pause and try. Station: shun. Division has a vowel before s-i-o-n, so zhun. Culture: chur. And mission has a consonant before it, so shun.
