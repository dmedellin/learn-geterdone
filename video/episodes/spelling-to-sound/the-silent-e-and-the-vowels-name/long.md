@chapter Hop and hope
@title text="The Silent E and the Vowel's Name" sub="Spelling to Sound" art=thumb
Say hop. Now say hope. The second word has one more letter, and you don't say it. But something changed. In hope, the o says its own name.

@art add-e
That's the silent e rule. It's about words of one part, said in one beat. When a word like that ends in a vowel, a consonant and an e, the vowel says its name. Bit becomes bite. Tub becomes tube. Mat becomes mate.

@list heading="The vowel's name" items="a → ay  ·  make@A says ay|i → eye  ·  time@I says the word|o → oh  ·  hope@O says oh|u → oo or you  ·  tube, huge@U says oo|e → ee@for e"
The vowel's name is what the letter is called. A says ay, as in make. I says the word eye, as in time. O says oh, as in hope. U says oo or you, as in tube and huge. And for e, it says ee.

@art silent-tile
And the e itself? You never say it. Make has one sound after the m: ay, and then k. The last letter is only a mark for the vowel before it.

@chapter How often it's right
@art score-field
The lab takes every word of one part in the list that ends in a vowel, a consonant and an e. It leaves out words where the consonant is w, x, y or r. That gives a hundred and forty words. The rule is right on a hundred and thirty-one. That's ninety-three point six percent.

@words text="time · make · hope · tube · huge · wide" sub="131 of 140"
Time, make, hope, tube, huge and wide all follow it.

@chapter The nine that break it
@math heading="Nine misses, three groups" items="have, give  ·  a short vowel@Have and give;;come, some, love, none  ·  the vowel of cup@Come, some;;lose, move, prove  ·  oo@Lose, move"
Now the nine misses. They aren't rare words. They're words you need in your first week, and they sort into three groups. Have and give keep a short vowel. Come, some, love and none are said with the vowel of cup. Lose, move and prove are said with oo.

@art three-groups
Each group is small enough to say out loud in a few seconds. And notice that seven of the nine have an o as the vowel. That's the letter to be careful with.

@words text="live" sub="live music  ·  to live"
There's really a tenth. The dictionary's first entry for live is the word in live music, said with the sound of eye, so the table counts it as right. But the verb, to live, has the short vowel of give.

@chapter Why have and give are different
@art v-end
Have and give have a reason you can use. English words almost never end in v. When the last sound of a word is v, an e is written after it. So the e in have isn't a mark for a long vowel. It's only there so the word doesn't end in v.

@words text="save · wave" sub="the same e, and a long vowel"
Save and wave have the same e, and they do follow the rule, because there the vowel is long too. The letters show you the pattern. The dictionary tells you which job the e is doing.

@chapter Before r
@art r-blocker
Now put an r before the e. The rule says the a in care should say its name, as it does in make. It doesn't. The lab scores nineteen words like this apart from the rest, and the rule is right only once, for here.

@list heading="Before r: 1 of 19" items="care, rare, share, there, where  ·  the vowel of bed@the vowel of bed|bore, core, more, score, shore, store  ·  the vowel of or@the vowel of or|pure, sure  ·  the vowel of book@the vowel of book|mere  ·  the vowel of sit@the vowel of sit"
Nine, like care, share and where, are said with the vowel of bed. Six, like more and store, have the vowel of or. Pure and sure have the vowel of book, and mere has the vowel of sit. An r changes the vowel before it.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="thinking the exceptions are rare words@The first|saying the final e@The second|using the rule before r@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the exceptions are rare words. They're have, give, come and some. The second is saying the final e, as if hope had two parts. And the third is using the rule before r, on care, more or sure.

@chapter Try it
@words text="make · move · hope · time" sub="which one breaks the rule?"
Your turn. Which of these breaks the rule: make, move, hope or time? Pause and say them. Move is said with oo, not oh. It's one of the nine.
