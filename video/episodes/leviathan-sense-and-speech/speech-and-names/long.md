@chapter The noblest invention
@title text="Of Speech" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 4" art=thumb
Hobbes says printing, clever as it is, is no great matter next to letters. And letters only write down something greater still: speech.

@quote text="without which, there had been amongst men, neither Common-wealth, nor Society, nor Contract, nor Peace, no more than amongst Lyons, Bears, and Wolves." cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 4"
Speech is names and their connexion, and without it there had been amongst men neither commonwealth, nor society, nor contract, nor peace, no more than amongst lions, bears, and wolves.

@art lions-and-city
Look at that list: commonwealth, society, contract, peace. They're the subjects of the whole book, and Hobbes has set them down as consequences of the power to name. The first author of speech was God, teaching Adam names. After that, need did the rest.

@chapter What speech is for
@art marks-and-signs
Speech turns a train of thoughts into a train of words, for two benefits. First, it registers the consequences of our thoughts, so we don't have to work them out again. Names serve as marks of remembrance. Second, when many use the same words, they signify to each other. Then names are signs.

@list heading="Four uses, four abuses" items="Acquire arts · inconstant words@acquiring arts|Counsel and teach · deceive by metaphor@counsel and teach|Make wills known · declare a false will@our wills|Please · grieve one another@to please"
Out of those come four uses: acquiring arts, to counsel and teach, to make known our wills and purposes, and to please, playing with words innocently. Each has its abuse: inconstant meanings, metaphors used to deceive, declaring a will you don't have, and words used to grieve one another.

@chapter Proper and common names
@art one-and-many
Some names are proper, belonging to one thing, like Peter, or this tree. Others are common to many, like man or horse. Those are universals. And Hobbes says there's nothing in the world universal but names. Everything named is individual and singular.

@words text="A likeness is not a further thing"
That's his side of an old dispute in the schools. A realist would answer that the likeness among things is itself real, and the name picks it out. Hobbes would reply that what they share is a likeness, and a likeness isn't one more thing.

@chapter Bigger and smaller names
@art nested-names
Names differ in breadth. Body is a larger name than man, and comprehends it. And a name needn't be one word. He that in his actions observeth the laws of his country makes one name, equal to one word, just.

@art triangle-rule
That's what lets reasoning become a reckoning of names. A deaf man who sees that one triangle has angles equal to two right angles must check the next triangle again. A man with words notes what mattered, straight sides and three angles, and writes it once for every triangle.

@chapter Reckoning with definitions
@math heading="The lesson's vocabulary" items="living-creature = body + living + sensible@living creature;;man = living-creature + rational@man is;;Peter = man + peter-self@Peter is;;just = man + observes-laws@just is"
The lab makes that reckoning checkable, with a vocabulary the lesson wrote. Living creature is body, living and sensible. Man is living creature and rational. Peter is man and peter self, a part only he has. Just is man and observes laws.

@art parts-bundle
Now expand every name down to its simple parts. Peter has every part man has, plus one more. That gives a consequence Hobbes doesn't state: a name with more parts covers fewer things.

@math heading="A is B: every part of B is in A" items="man is living-creature: true@true;;man is Peter: false, no peter-self@no peter self;;just is rational: true@just is rational;;Peter is just: false, no observes-laws@Peter is just"
An affirmation A is B is true when every simple part of B is in A. Man is a living creature: true. Man is Peter: false, there's no peter self. Just is rational: true. Peter is just: false, no observes laws. In all, of seven affirmations, three come out true and four false.

@words text="man is body ✓ · body is man ✗"
Reverse a pair and the verdict can change. Man is body is true, body is man is false. The larger name has fewer parts. One limit: Hobbes calls man and rational of equal extent, and a model of bundled parts can't show that.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Reading A is B as symmetrical@The first|A universal name names a universal thing@The second|The lab's test is Hobbes's own words@And the third"
The first slip is reading A is B as if it ran both ways. The second is thinking a universal name names a universal thing. And the third is taking the lab's test for Hobbes's words. His sentence is short, and this is one reading of it.
