@chapter Nouns that never take -s
@title text="Nouns With No Plural" sub="Nouns and Articles" art=thumb
Some nouns have no plural. Advice is one. You can give some advice, or a piece of advice, but never advices. And yet learners write informations and advices all the time.

@art mass-vs-count
In the word list this course uses, there are six hundred and eighty-three words that are only nouns. Seventy-five of them have no plural that the list records and a dictionary confirms.

@chapter Things you don't count
@math heading="Things you do not count" items="advice · assistance · clothing · equipment · furniture@Advice, assistance;;information · knowledge · money · software · wealth@information, knowledge;;happiness · darkness · poverty · violence · tennis@Happiness, darkness"
Most of the list is things you don't count. Advice, assistance, clothing, equipment, furniture. Information, knowledge, money, software, wealth. And happiness, darkness, poverty, violence and tennis.

@art piece-of
Each one names a mass of something. You have a lot of it, or a little. You don't have one of them and two of them. To count it, you use a phrase like a piece of, or some.

@chapter Three things they never take
@list heading="A noun with no plural never takes" items="-s: advices ✗@never takes s|a or an: an advice ✗@It doesn't take a|many, a few: many informations ✗@not many"
A noun like this never takes s. It doesn't take a or an. And it goes with much and a little, not many and a few. So it's much information, not many informations.

@math heading="Say it this way" items="some advice@some advice;;a piece of advice@a piece of advice;;a piece of news  ·  a piece of furniture@a piece of news"
So say some advice, or a piece of advice. A piece of news. A piece of furniture.

@words text="one chair, two chairs ✓ · one advice, two advices ✗" sub="does it sound wrong? it's probably a mass noun"
Here's a quick test. Can you say one of them, two of them? One chair, two chairs: yes, so it has a plural. One advice, two advices: no. If the words sound wrong, the noun is probably a mass noun.

@chapter Plural already, or the same
@words text="news · series · sheep · species · data" sub="also: aircraft, politics, mathematics, personnel"
A smaller group of nine already looks plural, or doesn't change: news, series, sheep, species, data, aircraft, politics, mathematics, and personnel. News takes a singular verb. And one sheep, two sheep: it's the same for one and for many.

@art same-for-many
Series and species work the same way: one series, two series. And personnel names a group of people, so it's used as a plural. These nine are worth learning one by one.

@chapter Limits of the list
@math heading="Four kinds in the 75" items="47  ·  things you do not count@forty-seven;;9  ·  plural already, or the same for one and many@nine;;16  ·  days and months@sixteen;;3  ·  left over: basis, hell, sake@three left over"
So the seventy-five sort into four kinds. Forty-seven are things you don't count. Nine look plural already, or are the same. Sixteen are days and months. And there are three left over: basis, hell and sake.

@art four-trays
A learner needs the first two kinds. They're true of English. The other two kinds are limits of the list itself, and they need a little care.

@art two-mondays
The days and months are a limit of the list. The word list records only the singular name, but two Mondays is correct English. So for them, the rule never takes s is false. Don't carry it over.

@words text="basis → bases" sub="hell · sake: fixed phrases"
Basis has the plural bases, which the word list doesn't record. Hell and sake live in fixed phrases, like for the sake of. So read the list as nouns where the word list records no plural, not nouns that can't have one.

@chapter One word, two senses
@math heading="Time in the novel, quoted" items="a time  ·  10 times@a time ten times;;times  ·  19 times@times nineteen times"
Some nouns count in one sense and not in another. Time that you measure has no plural. But a time you remember does. In one novel, counted once, it's written a time ten times, and times nineteen times.

@art one-word-two-senses
Hope is the same: hopes is written twenty-three times. No list can mark which sense a writer means. And ten words from the list, like advice, news, money and health, are never written with a or an, and never as a plural, in the whole novel.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="informations ✗  advices ✗@The first|an advice ✗@The second|Thinking a noun is always counted or never@the third"
The first slip is believing every noun has a plural. Informations and advices are two of the commonest errors. The second is putting a or an in front: an advice. And the third is thinking a noun is always counted, or never counted. Remember time.

@chapter Try it
@words text="I need some ___." sub="advices · advice · an advice · many advices"
Your turn. Which is right? I need some advices. I need some advice. I need an advice. I need many advices. Advice has no plural, and doesn't take an. So it's I need some advice.
