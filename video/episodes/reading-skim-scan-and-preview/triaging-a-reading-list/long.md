@chapter The problem
@title text="Triaging a Reading List" sub="Reading Faster · Skimming, Scanning and Previewing" art=thumb
Eight documents land on your desk. Reading them all at two hundred and fifty words a minute takes an hour, and you have forty five minutes. What do you do?

@art pile
Here's the purpose. Tomorrow you help decide whether your team moves to a new building. Eight documents arrived, fifteen thousand words in all. Each has a value for that decision, from one for the neighbourhood newsletter to ten for the landlord's lease proposal. Thirty six points in all.

@chapter The model
@art three-choices
For each document, you choose read, skim or skip. A document read costs its words over a reading rate of two hundred and fifty, and yields its whole value. One skimmed costs its words over a skimming rate of seven hundred and fifty, and is credited with half its value. One skipped costs nothing, and yields nothing.

@words text="A skim keeps ½ the value" sub="an assumption you can change"
That half is an assumption, in the spirit of the last lesson, where a skim kept the gist better than the detail. The values are judgements too. Choosing them is half of triage.

@chapter Three plans
@math heading="Read everything" items="15,000 / 250 = 60 min@fifteen thousand over;;budget 45 → 15 over@fifteen over"
The plan most piles get is to read everything. Fifteen thousand over two hundred and fifty is sixty minutes, fifteen over the budget. So in practice, the last documents are never reached.

@art time-budget
A plan first has to fit the budget. Among the plans that fit, the better one covers more of the value. And reading in the order the pile arrived is a plan too, usually a poor one.

@art arrival-order
That's the skip wrong preset: read in the order they came until time runs out. It gets through the staff survey and stops, at forty three point two minutes. It covers twenty three of the thirty six, about sixty three point nine percent, and never opens the facilities manager's summary, the second most valuable document in the pile.

@math heading="Skim-the-rest preset" items="read 7,500 / 250 = 30 min@thirty minutes;;skim 7,500 / 750 = 10 min@ten minutes;;10 + 6 + 8 + 4 + (1 + 3 + 3 + 1) / 2 = 32@thirty two;;32/36 = 8/9 ≈ 88.9% in 40 min@eight ninths"
Now triage. Read the four documents worth four or more, seventy five hundred words, in thirty minutes. Skim the other four, seventy five hundred words, in ten minutes. The value is the four read in full, plus half of the four skimmed: thirty two. That's eight ninths, about eighty eight point nine percent, in forty minutes.

@art triaged-plan
Half the words were read and half skimmed, and the skimmed half carried only eight of the thirty six points. That asymmetry is what got the pile under the budget, not a faster rate.

@chapter Or read faster?
@math heading="Reading it all in 45 minutes" items="15,000 / 45 ≈ 333.3 words a minute@three hundred and thirty three;;a third faster than 250@a third faster"
Compare that with reading faster. To read the whole pile in forty five minutes, you'd need fifteen thousand over forty five, about three hundred and thirty three point three words a minute. That's a third faster than two fifty, with the same understanding, and no lesson in this Subject promises that.

@art faster-vs-fewer
And skipping the four low documents instead of skimming them? Thirty minutes, covering seven ninths, about seventy seven point eight percent.

@chapter Choosing where to look
@words text="Choose where to look" sub="Duggan and Payne (2009)"
Duggan and Payne, in two thousand nine, gave readers more text than time. Those who skimmed all of it remembered more important ideas than those who read half, because they chose where to look. A reading list asks for the same choice one level up, between documents instead of within one.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Reading faster to get through a pile@The first|Working in the order it arrived@The second|Forgetting the values are yours@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the fastest way through a pile is to read faster. The second is working through it in the order it arrived, which is nobody's judgement of value. And the third is forgetting the values are yours. A wrong value produces a confident wrong plan.

@chapter Try it
@words text="Read 10% faster, or skip two documents?" sub="15,000 words · 250 wpm · newsletter + building history"
Your turn. On the same pile, which saves more: reading ten percent faster, or skipping the newsletter and the building history? Reading faster saves about five point five minutes. Skipping those thirty nine hundred words saves fifteen point six minutes, and loses two of thirty six points.
