@chapter The question
@title text="Shifting Gears" sub="Reading Faster · Skimming, Scanning and Previewing" art=thumb
When a text suddenly gets harder, do you slow down? And if you do, was that your choice, or did the text just drag you down? This drill tells the two apart.

@chapter The drill
@art two-sections
It's one passage in two sections. The first is a story: a retired woman, her grandson, and a vegetable plot that won't grow. The second is the soil laboratory's report on that plot, written the way such reports are. The lab times each section, then asks six questions, three on each.

@words text="Change speed if the text asks you to" sub="logged as a test of a mixed passage"
Change speed between the sections if the text asks you to. That's the exercise. The reading is logged as a test of a mixed passage, so it stays out of the medians set against your standard baselines.

@chapter Two readers
@math heading="Flexible preset" items="story: 292 × 60 / 60 = 292@two ninety two;;report: 286 × 60 / 104 = 165@one sixty five;;whole ≈ 211.5 · effective ≈ 176.2 (5 of 6)@five of six"
The flexible reader took the two ninety two word story at two ninety two words a minute, then slowed to one sixty five for the two eighty six word report. With five of six right, that's about two eleven point five overall, and an effective rate of about one seventy six point two.

@math heading="Rigid preset" items="story: 292 × 60 / 73 = 240@two forty;;report: 286 × 60 / 72 ≈ 238.3@two thirty eight point three;;whole ≈ 239.2 · effective ≈ 159.4 (4 of 6)@four of six"
The rigid reader held one speed: two forty, and about two thirty eight point three. They got all three story questions, but only one of the three on the report. Four of six: about two thirty nine point two overall, and an effective rate of about one fifty nine point four.

@art whole-hides
The rigid reader finished sooner and understood less, and the whole loss was in the dense section. The whole passage rate alone would have hidden both the shift and what it bought. That's why the lab gives each section its own rate.

@chapter Chosen or forced
@art shift-vs-collapse
But a slower second section isn't by itself a shift of gear. If your rate fell to one sixty five and your report questions fell too, you didn't choose to slow down. The text slowed you, and still beat you. A shift is a choice when the rate falls and the questions on the hard section hold.

@art attention-drift
That's why the drill asks about both sections, and why it asks whether your attention left the text. A slow section with your mind elsewhere is a lapse, not a gear.

@chapter Carver's gears
@list heading="Carver's five gears (1990)" items="Scanning, about 600@Scanning|Skimming, about 450@skimming|Rauding, about 300@rauding|Learning, about 200@learning|Memorising, about 138@memorising"
Carver, in nineteen ninety, put five gears at about six hundred, four fifty, three hundred, two hundred and one thirty eight standard length words a minute for the college students he studied: scanning, skimming, rauding, learning and memorising.

@art constant-speed
And he found most readers seldom use them. They hold a nearly constant rate, their rauding rate, across ordinary texts. When a text is harder, their understanding tends to give way rather than their speed. His advice, in nineteen ninety two, was to shift deliberately.

@art gear-gate
Down into the learning gear for what must be learned. Up into skimming for what need only be skimmed. So name the gear for each part before you start: skim it, read it, or learn it.

@words text="The rigid reader is the usual one"
So the rigid preset isn't a poor reader. By Carver's account, it's the usual one. This lesson asks you to do the unusual thing on purpose, and the drill shows whether you did.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="A good reader holds one fast speed@The first|Any slowdown counts as a shift@The second|Judging by the whole-passage rate@And the third"
The first slip is believing a good reader holds one fast speed. The second is counting any slowdown as a shift. And the third is judging by the whole passage rate, where the rigid reader's two thirty nine point two beats two eleven point five, but its effective rate doesn't.

@chapter Try it
@words text="300 easy words in 60 s · 300 dense in 100 s" sub="the two section rates?"
Your turn. A reader takes sixty seconds over a three hundred word easy section, and a hundred seconds over a three hundred word dense one. What are the two section rates? Three hundred, and one eighty. The two twenty five for the whole passage hides the shift.
