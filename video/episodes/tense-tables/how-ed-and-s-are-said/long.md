@chapter One spelling, three sounds
@title text="How -ed and -s Are Said" sub="Tense Tables" art=thumb
Walked, played and wanted all end in e-d. But say them, and the endings aren't the same.

@math heading="-ed" items="walked  /t/@Walked ends;;played  /d/@played ends;;wanted  /ɪd/@wanted has"
Walked ends in a t sound. Played ends in a d sound. And wanted has an extra part. The s ending does the same thing in walks, plays and passes.

@art three-paths
You don't have to learn which sound each verb takes. The last sound of the verb decides, and there's a rule for it.

@chapter Voiced and voiceless
@art throat-wave
Put a finger on your throat. Say s, then z. With s, there's no shake. With z, your throat shakes. A sound with no shake is voiceless. A sound with a shake is voiced.

@list heading="Voiceless sounds" items="p  k  f  s  t@p, k, f, s and t|/θ/  think@start of think|/ʃ/  ship@start of ship|/tʃ/  church@start of church"
The voiceless sounds are p, k, f, s and t, the sound at the start of think, the sound at the start of ship, and the sound at the start of church. Every vowel is voiced, and so are b, d, g, l, m, n, r, v and z, among others.

@chapter The rule for -ed
@list heading="-ed" items="after t or d → /ɪd/: wanted, needed@After t or d|after other voiceless → /t/: walked, hoped, laughed@any other voiceless|after anything else → /d/: played, loved, opened@any other sound"
After t or d, the ending is a part of its own, id: wanted, needed. After any other voiceless sound, the ending is t: walked, hoped, laughed. After any other sound, a vowel included, the ending is d: played, loved, opened.

@art pair-bars
So most of the time, the ending joins the last part of the verb. Walked is one part. Only after t or d does it need a part of its own, and wanted has two parts.

@chapter The rule for -s
@list heading="-s" items="after a hiss → /ɪz/: passes, wishes, watches@After a hiss|after other voiceless → /s/: walks, hopes@other voiceless sound|after anything else → /z/: plays, needs, loves@after anything else"
The s rule has the same shape. After a hiss, the ending is a part of its own, iz: passes, wishes, watches. After any other voiceless sound, it's s: walks, hopes. And after anything else, it's z: plays, needs, loves.

@words text="judges · faces" sub="silent e, still /ɪz/"
The spelling doesn't always show the hiss. Judges and faces end in a silent e, so the spelling adds only s. They still take the extra part.

@chapter Three verbs, step by step
@math heading="Read the last sound" items="walk  →  k, voiceless  →  walked /t/@Walk ends;;play  →  a vowel, voiced  →  played /d/@Play ends;;want  →  t  →  wanted /ɪd/@Want ends"
Walk ends in k, a voiceless sound, so the ending is t. Play ends in a vowel, and every vowel is voiced, so the ending is d. Want ends in t, so wanted gets a part of its own, id.

@art sound-not-letter
Listen to the sound, not the letter. Love ends in the letter e, but its last sound is v, so loved ends in d. Laugh ends in g h, but its last sound is f, so laughed ends in t.

@chapter How often it's right
@math heading="Checked against a dictionary of sounds" items="-ed  ·  1111 of 1118  ·  99.4%@one thousand one hundred and eleven;;-s  ·  1187 of 1189  ·  99.8%@one thousand one hundred and eighty-seven"
The rule was checked against a dictionary of sounds. The e-d rule is right for one thousand one hundred and eleven of one thousand one hundred and eighteen verbs. The s rule is right for one thousand one hundred and eighty-seven of one thousand one hundred and eighty-nine.

@art two-readings
Not one of the seven misses breaks the rule. In each one, the dictionary answered about a different word. The noun house ends in s, but the verb house ends in z, so housed ends in d. Give the rule the verb's sound, and it's right.

@math heading="The -s rule on nouns" items="1820 of 1823  ·  99.8%@eighteen hundred and twenty;;misses: mouths, paths, youths@mouths, paths and youths"
The same s rule works on nouns that mean more than one. It's right for eighteen hundred and twenty of eighteen hundred and twenty-three nouns. The misses are mouths, paths and youths, where the last sound turns voiced.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="walk-ed as two parts ✗@The first|Reading the last letter, not the last sound@The second|Saying -s as /s/ every time@the third"
The first slip is saying e-d as a part of its own every time. Walked has one part, not two. The second is reading the last letter instead of the last sound. And the third is saying s as s every time. Plays, needs and loves end in z.

@chapter Try it
@art finger-check
Your turn. Take a verb, say it, and listen to its last sound. Is it t or d, or a hiss? Then add a part. If not, put a finger on your throat. A shake means d or z. No shake means t or s.
