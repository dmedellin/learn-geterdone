@chapter Twelve boxes
@title text="Five Forms Make Twelve" sub="Tense Tables" art=thumb
Open any book for learners of English and you'll find a page with twelve boxes on it. The book asks you to learn every box. You don't have to.

@art twelve-grid
The table has three times down the side: now, before, and later. And it has four shapes across the top. Three times four is twelve boxes. But twelve boxes are not twelve things to learn.

@chapter Five forms
@math heading="The five forms of walk" items="walk  ·  the base@walk, the base form;;walks  ·  he, she, it@walks, for he;;walking  ·  the -ing form@walking, the ing form;;walked  ·  the past@walked, the past;;walked  ·  the form after have@the form after have"
A verb in English has five forms. For walk, they are: walk, the base form. Walks, for he, she and it. Walking, the ing form. Walked, the past. And the form after have, which for this verb is also walked.

@art five-forms
Only one verb has more. Be has eight forms, and it gets its own place in the course. The small helping words, like will, have fewer, and they only ever stand in front of another verb.

@chapter The whole table
@art small-word-front
Here's the whole trick. Two of the twelve boxes are a form on its own: walk and walked. The other ten are one of the five forms with a small word in front of it.

@list heading="Three small words" items="have · looks back from now@Have gives you|be + -ing · still going on@Be with the ing form|will · moves it later@Will moves"
Have gives you the shape that looks back from now. Be with the ing form gives you the shape that is still going on. Will moves the whole thing later. That's the entire table.

@math heading="Four shapes" items="She walks. She walked. She will walk.@She walks;;She is walking. She was walking. She will be walking.@She is walking;;She has walked. She had walked. She will have walked.@She has walked;;She has been walking. She had been walking. She will have been walking.@She has been walking"
Listen to the four shapes. She walks, she walked, she will walk. She is walking, she was walking, she will be walking. She has walked, she had walked, she will have walked. And she has been walking, she had been walking, she will have been walking.

@chapter Four rules
@art base-branches
You learn the base form when you learn the word. The other four forms come from rules. Each rule is short, with a small change for certain endings.

@list heading="The rules" items="-s: passes, carries, goes@For he, she or it|-ing: hope → hoping, lie → lying, see → seeing@For the ing form|-ed: hope → hoped, carry → carried@For the past|double: stop → stopping, stopped@last letter doubles"
For he, she or it, add s. After a hissing sound, add e-s: passes. After a consonant and y, carries. For the ing form, drop a silent e: hope, hoping. Lie becomes lying, but see keeps its e: seeing. For the past, add e-d: hoped, carried. And sometimes the last letter doubles: stop, stopping, stopped.

@words text="stop → stopping · listen → listening" sub="double when the last part is the strong part"
That last rule is the only one that needs the sound of the word. Stop has one part, so the last part is the strong part, and it doubles. Listen has two parts, and the strong one is the first, so nothing doubles. And nothing ever doubles before s.

@chapter Building a box
@math heading="Four of walk's twelve boxes" items="she walks  =  a form on its own@a form on its own;;she is walking  =  be + the -ing form@she is walking;;she has walked  =  have + the form after have@she has walked;;she has been walking  =  have + been + the -ing form@she has been walking"
Let's build four boxes. She walks is a form on its own. She is walking is be, plus the ing form. She has walked is have, plus the form after have. And she has been walking is have, plus been, plus the ing form.

@art two-words-front
That last box has two small words in front. Has been is have and be together, and walking comes behind them. Nothing new was learned. Walking was already one of the five forms.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Learning twelve boxes as twelve things@The first|Doubling when the strong part is early@The second|she will walks ✗  she will walk ✓@The third"
The first slip is treating the twelve boxes as twelve things to learn. The second is doubling the last letter when the strong part is early. Listen does not become listenning. The third is putting s or e-d on the verb after will. It's she will walk, not she will walks.

@art three-times
The small word decides which form follows it. After will, use the base form. After have, use the form after have: she has walked. And have, be and will have forms of their own, like has, was and been. The course on helping verbs takes those one at a time.

@chapter Try it
@words text="stop · carry · hope" sub="stopping · carries · hoping"
Your turn. Take stop, carry and hope. Make their five forms: stopping, carries, hoping, and the rest. Then notice that the twelve boxes don't change at all. Only the five forms do.
