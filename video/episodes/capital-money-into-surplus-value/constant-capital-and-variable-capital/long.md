@chapter One act, two results
@title text="Constant Capital and Variable Capital" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 8" art=thumb
Here's the puzzle. The spinner doesn't do two jobs, one to add value and another to keep the cotton's value in the yarn. He just spins. Yet two results come out: new value added, and old value preserved.

@art spinner-two-sides
The explanation is the twofold character of labour. As concrete spinning, the work turns this cotton into this yarn, so the cotton's value goes with it. As labour in the abstract, so many hours, it adds new value. Make the same man a joiner, and he'd still add value, but he couldn't carry the cotton's value into anything.

@chapter Testing it
@art sixfold-cotton
Marx changes one thing at a time. Suppose an invention lets the spinner spin as much in six hours as he did in thirty six. Six hours now spin thirty six pounds of cotton instead of six. The new value is the same, spread over six times as many pounds. But the value carried over from the cotton is six times as great.

@words text="Productivity moves what is preserved" sub="cotton's price moves what is transferred · neither moves what is added"
Now let the cotton's value rise sixfold while the spinning stays the same. The value transferred from each pound changes in proportion, and the value added doesn't change at all. Two causes, two separate effects.

@chapter Wear
@art machine-corpse
Instruments behave differently from materials. Cotton disappears into the yarn. A machine keeps its shape, day after day, until it's worn out, and then its corpse is left behind. Over its life it passes its whole value into its products, a fraction each day. You know the fraction the way a life insurance office knows your lifespan: by averages.

@quote text="Suppose a machine to be worth £1000, and to wear out in 1000 days. Then one thousandth part of the value of the machine is daily transferred to the day's product." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 8"
Suppose a machine to be worth a thousand pounds, and to wear out in a thousand days. Then one thousandth part of the value of the machine is daily transferred to the day's product.

@art land-wind-water
One rule holds throughout. Means of production never transfer more value than they lose. Normal waste, the devil's dust, still counts, because it's a condition of the product. But land, wind and water, which no labour made, transfer no value at all.

@chapter The two names
@art c-and-v
So Marx names the two parts. Capital laid out on means of production doesn't change its value in production. That's constant capital, c. Capital laid out on labour-power reproduces its own value and produces an excess, surplus value. That's variable capital, v.

@words text="Dearer cotton is still constant capital"
Two cautions. If cotton bought at sixpence rises to a shilling after a bad harvest, each pound passes a shilling into the yarn. That doesn't make cotton variable: the change came from the fields. And swapping ten cheap tools for one costly machine changes the proportion of c to v, not their functions.

@chapter A day in the mill
@math heading="A day in the mill" items="machine: £1,000 / 1,000 days = £1 a day@a pound a day;;transferred: £1 + £10 = £11@eleven pounds;;new value: 12 h at £1 = £12@twelve hours;;product: £11 + £12 = £23@twenty three"
The lab values one day. The machine passes a pound a day. Add ten pounds of cotton, and eleven pounds is transferred. Twelve hours add twelve pounds of new value. The day's product is worth twenty three.

@math heading="What replaces v" items="wages: £6@six pounds;;new value: £12@replaces;;sixfold cotton: transferred £61 · new £12@sixty one"
Wages were six pounds. The twelve pounds of new value replaces the six of variable capital, and adds six more. Spin six times the cotton in the same day, and the transfer is sixty one pounds, while the new value stays at twelve.

@art mill-day
The strongest objection is that machines are productive, and their owners are paid for that. Marx grants half: a machine helps make use-values, and can multiply the yarn. What he denies is that it adds value, which on his definition only living labour does. The dispute is over what value is.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Constant capital is fixed capital@The first|A productive machine passes on more@The second|v is constant because the wage is fixed@And the third"
The first slip is taking constant capital for fixed capital. The cotton passes its whole value in a day and is still constant. The second is thinking a productive machine passes on more than it cost. And the third is calling v constant because the wage is a fixed sum. What varies is what replaces it.
