@chapter Shape or history?
@title text="Entitlement, Patterns and the Gini Coefficient" sub="Justice and Collective Choice" art=thumb
You can judge a distribution by its shape, or by how it came about. Nozick argued that justice depends on the history, not the pattern. And a single free exchange can show you why the two come apart.

@art final-list
His challenge to the last two lessons is that they judge only the final list of holdings. Suppose the list is the one you think just. Then people spend what they have, freely, and the list changes. If nothing wrong happened in the exchanges, how can the new list be unjust?

@chapter Wilt Chamberlain
@art basketball-payments
His example is a basketball player. Four people hold ten units each. Three of them each pay one to watch Wilt Chamberlain play, and he keeps what they pay. Afterwards, three hold nine, and one holds thirteen. The total is still forty, but the shares are no longer level.

@chapter The Gini
@words text="Gini = sum of all ordered gaps ÷ (2 · n² · m)" sub="0 when all holdings are equal"
The Gini coefficient measures that shape. Add up the gap between every ordered pair of people, then divide by two, times n squared, times the mean. It's zero when all holdings are equal.

@math heading="After one round" items="before:  10, 10, 10, 10:  Gini 0@every gap;;after:  9, 9, 9, 13@after the game;;gaps:  3 pairs × 4 × 2 orders = 24@three such pairs;;divisor:  2 · 16 · 10 = 320@The divisor;;Gini = 24/320 = 3/40@three fortieths"
Before, every gap is zero, so the Gini is zero. After the game, each of the three at nine is four away from the person at thirteen. Three such pairs, counted in both orders, gives twenty four. The divisor is two times sixteen times ten, three hundred and twenty. So the Gini is three fortieths.

@art gini-drift
That's Nozick's point as a number. One round of voluntary payments moved the Gini from zero to three fortieths, and no one was wronged. To keep it at zero, the state would have to stop the payments, or take the money back. Liberty upsets patterns, he concluded, and a theory that wants a pattern must keep interfering.

@art two-rounds
And it keeps drifting.

@math heading="Two rounds" items="8, 8, 8, 16@they hold eight;;gaps:  48@forty eight;;Gini = 48/320 = 3/20@three twentieths"
If the same three pay again, they hold eight each, and Wilt holds sixteen. The gaps add to forty eight, and the Gini is three twentieths, twice the single round figure. A pattern isn't stable under free exchange.

@chapter Three replies
@list heading="Three replies" items="Was the start just? Were the choices free?@The first|Rawls: rules of the basic structure@The second|Not every pattern is about the Gini@The third"
Three replies deserve their strength. The first asks what made the starting list just, and whether a choice made under need is as free as the story imagines. The second is Rawls's own. The difference principle governs the rules people trade under, like taxation, so a tax on Chamberlain's receipts isn't an interference after the fact. The third: a theory that cares only about a floor is untouched by payments above it.

@art redistribution
Nozick's rejoinder to the second, that a tax on earnings is on a par with forced labour, is where the dispute stands.

@chapter Redistribution
@math heading="The second table" items="2, 4, 6, 28:  Gini 1/2@Start with;;6, 8, 10, 16:  Gini 1/5@End with;;total unchanged@the total"
Redistribution moves the number back. Start with two, four, six and twenty eight, with a Gini of one half. End with six, eight, ten and sixteen, with a Gini of one fifth. The total is unchanged, so the total rule calls them equal. Whether the move was just is the dispute, and the number is the same under both views.

@art same-list-two-histories
The lab measures shape. It doesn't know who paid whom. And a history is what the entitlement view is made of, so no number here can pass a verdict on it.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Thinking a just distribution has a just shape@The first|Treating the Gini as a measure of injustice@The second|Reading it as ruling out all redistribution@And the third"
The first slip is thinking a just distribution is one with a just shape. Nine, nine, nine, thirteen has the same Gini whether the fans paid freely or were robbed. The second is treating the Gini as a measure of injustice. It measures spread, nothing else. And the third is reading Nozick as ruling out all redistribution. It leaves room to rectify past injustice.
