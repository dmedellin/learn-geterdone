title: The court that accepts both premises and rejects the conclusion
---

@art three-judges
A court can accept each premise by majority, and reject the conclusion they imply.

@math items="p:  2 to 1,  yes@contract;;q:  2 to 1,  yes@was breached;;p ∧ q:  1 to 2,  no@liable"
Three judges. A majority says there was a contract. A majority says it was breached. But only one judge says liable.

@art broken-triangle
Every judge is consistent, accepting liability exactly when they accept both premises. The court isn't. Its majority says yes, yes, and no. That's the discursive dilemma.
