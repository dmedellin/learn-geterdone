@art two-down-one-up
Can an outcome be better when two out of three people lose?

@math items="A = 10, 10, 10:  total 30@ten, ten, ten;;B = 1, 30, 2:  total 33@one, thirty, two"
By total welfare, yes. Ten, ten, ten adds to thirty. One, thirty, two adds to thirty three. So B wins.

@words text="The sum can't see who has what"
The rule counts the sum, and nothing else. It doesn't care who has which number. Swap the numbers around, and it wouldn't notice.
