title: Can doing nothing be a cause?
---

@art lifeguard-pool
A lifeguard watches a swimmer drown. Did the lifeguard cause the death?

@math items="R = L and W,  D = not R@the rescue;;flip R to 1:  D = 0@Flip the rescue"
Model it. The rescue needs the lifeguard in reach and willing, and no rescue means drowning. Flip the rescue on, and the drowning goes away.

@words text="The omission is a but-for cause"
So by the same test as a struck match, the omission is a cause. If letting die differs from killing, the reason lies elsewhere.
