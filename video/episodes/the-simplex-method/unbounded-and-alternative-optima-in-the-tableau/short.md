title: When the best answer is a whole edge

@art optimal-edge
Sometimes the optimum isn't one point. It's a whole edge.

@math items="stop at (3, 3/2),  z = 12@three halves;;nonbasic s₂ has reduced cost 0@reduced cost zero;;pivot → (2/3, 5),  z = 12@two thirds"
Maximise three x one plus two x two, and the method stops at three, three halves, with z twelve. But a nonbasic column has reduced cost zero. Pivot it in, and you reach two thirds, five, also twelve.

@words text="Every point between them scores 12"
And every point on the edge between them scores twelve too.
