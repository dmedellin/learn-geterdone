@chapter The question
@title text="Time and Sales" sub="Volume and Order Flow" art=thumb
Every trade that happens gets reported, one line at a time. That stream is called time and sales, or the tape. It's fast, it's busy, and it's very easy to read too much into. So what does it actually show?

@art tape
Each line is a print, a reported transaction with a time, a price, and a size. Reading the tape means watching those prints by price, size, side, pace, and what price does in response.

@words text="Fast buying matters only when it moves or holds price higher"
The lesson's central idea is this. Fast buying matters only when it moves or holds price higher. Speed and size are not the result. Price is. Keep that in mind, and the tape becomes much easier to read.

@chapter Which side?
@art ask-bid-prints
Trades near the ask are commonly classified as buyer initiated. Trades near the bid are classified as seller initiated. That's aggressor classification. A print at the ask means a buyer crossed the spread to get filled.

@words text="An inference, not exchange truth"
But it's an inference from the quote, and it can be wrong on any print. Classification depends on the quote data and the method. That's why trade colour on a tape isn't exchange provided truth.

@chapter Aggression that works
@art prints-rising
Many trades execute at the ask, and price rises. What's happening? Aggressive buyers are making upward progress. They're accepting offers and successfully moving price higher. Sellers are being lifted, not controlling.

@chapter Aggression that doesn't
@art absorption
Now the same tape, but price doesn't rise. Many trades at the ask, and nothing happens. That suggests possible sell side absorption. Passive sellers may be replenishing enough liquidity to stop upward progress.

@words text="Not 'no sellers' · not a guaranteed breakout"
It's tempting to say there are no sellers. But every print at the ask was a seller filling. The sellers are there, and they're replenishing. Nor is it a guaranteed breakout. Nothing in the tape says the resting side will run out first. The same logic applies to selling at the bid.

@chapter Pace
@art pace
Pace is the speed of the tape. Is it accelerating or slowing? But pace has to be read together with price response, because flow intensity alone doesn't show whether it moves the market. Fast tape is a change in activity. The lab's absorption tapes are fast, and go nowhere.

@chapter Count it
@math heading="Ten prints" items="6 at the ask: 1,800 shares@six at the ask;;4 at the bid: 1,500 shares@four at the bid;;1,800 − 1,500 = 300@three hundred more"
Here's the lesson's example. Ten prints. Six at the ask totalling eighteen hundred shares, and four at the bid totalling fifteen hundred. Eighteen hundred minus fifteen hundred is three hundred more ask classified than bid classified.

@words text="Ask-classified leads by 300" sub="direction still has to be read from price"
So ask classified volume leads by three hundred. That's an estimate of which side crossed the spread. Only price shows whether it worked.

@list heading="Not these" items="Buyers outnumber sellers 6 to 4@outnumber|Price must be rising@must be rising"
It doesn't mean buyers outnumber sellers six to four. Every print had a buyer and a seller. And it doesn't mean price must be rising. The lab's absorption tape has a two point seven to one ask side imbalance, and moves four cents. Imbalance is aggression. Price is the result.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common mistakes" items="Every large print is a directional bet@The first|Ignoring hidden or replenishing liquidity@The second|Trade colour as exchange truth@the third"
Three mistakes. The first is treating every large print as a directional bet. The second is ignoring hidden or replenishing liquidity. And the third is assuming trade colour is exchange provided truth.

@art big-print
So read sequences, not isolated prints. One big print is one trade, with a buyer and a seller like every other. And remember aggression without progress can indicate absorption.

@chapter Try it
@list heading="Reading steps" items="Observe side@observe side|Observe pace@observe pace|Observe response@observe response"
The lab gives three steps. Observe side: are trades repeatedly at the ask, or the bid? Observe pace: is the tape accelerating or slowing? Then observe response. Does price move with the aggressive flow, or fail to progress?

@words text="Synthetic educational data" sub="no live market connection"
The lab runs on synthetic educational data, with no live market connection. Run a tape where buyers win and one where they're absorbed, and describe each with those three steps before reading the lab's explanation.
