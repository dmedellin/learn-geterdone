@chapter How a commonwealth lives
@title text="The Nutrition and Procreation of a Commonwealth" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 24" art=thumb
So far Hobbes has shown how a commonwealth is made and what its parts are. Now he asks how it lives. And he answers with the body he began with. A body eats.

@art fruits-of-the-earth
The artificial man's food is animals, vegetables and minerals, which God has laid near the surface of the earth. All they need is the labour and industry of receiving them. So plenty depends, next to God's favour, on labour alone.

@chapter Property is made
@quote text="The Distribution of the Materials of this Nourishment, is the constitution of Mine, and Thine, and His; that is to say, in one word Propriety, and belongeth in all kinds of Common-wealth to the Soveraign Power." cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 24"
The distribution of all this is the constitution of mine, and thine, and his. In one word, propriety, or property. And it belongs, in every kind of commonwealth, to the sovereign power.

@words text="Neither propriety, nor community, but uncertainty"
Constituted means made by an act, not found in nature. Without a commonwealth, everything is his that gets it and keeps it by force. That's neither property nor common ownership. It's uncertainty. The Greeks, Hobbes adds, called law nomos: distribution.

@chapter The division of Canaan
@art twelve-portions
His example is Canaan. Israel's land was divided not by the people's own discretion, but by Eleazar the priest and Joshua their general. There were thirteen tribes once Joseph's was split, yet Joshua made only twelve portions. The tribe of Levi got no land, but the tenth part of the whole fruits.

@words text="Arbitrary = by the sovereign's discretion" sub="not: unjust"
Hobbes calls that division arbitrary. He means it followed the discretion of those who held the sovereignty, not that it was unjust. He reads William the Conqueror's division of England the same way.

@chapter The harvest sum
@art tenth-set-apart
The lab does the sum, on a sample harvest of twelve hundred measures, since Hobbes gives no quantities. Levi's tenth comes off first. Only what's left is divided.

@math heading="Canaan preset" items="set apart: 1200 · 1/10 = 120@a tenth;;remainder: 1200 − 120 = 1080@leaves;;each of 12: 1080 ÷ 12 = 90@ninety"
A tenth of twelve hundred is a hundred and twenty. That leaves ten eighty. Shared among twelve equal portions, each gets ninety. With nothing set apart, each would get a hundred.

@math heading="Unequal preset: Judah weighs 2" items="weights: 11 · 1 + 1 · 2 = 13@thirteen;;ordinary portion: 1080/13@ten eighty over thirteen;;Judah: 2160/13@twenty one sixty;;check: 120 + 11 · 1080/13 + 2160/13 = 1200@add back"
Now give Judah's portion weight two. The weights total thirteen. An ordinary portion gets ten eighty over thirteen, and Judah gets twenty one sixty over thirteen. They aren't whole numbers, so the lab keeps the fractions. And the shares add back to twelve hundred, because nothing was rounded.

@chapter Against the sovereign, no claim
@words text="Exclude all other subjects, not the sovereign"
Here's the hard consequence. A subject's property is a right to exclude all other subjects from it, not to exclude the sovereign. And giving the commonwealth public land to live on is in vain, because commonwealths can endure no diet. Their costs are set by neighbours and accidents.

@art labour-and-fence
Locke, a generation later, said property is made by labour before any government, and a government that takes it without consent breaks its trust. Hobbes's reply is the uncertainty sentence. Your labour is unprotected until someone has the power to say whose it is.

@chapter Blood and children
@art money-as-blood
Foreign trade and the rules of contract belong to the sovereign too. And money does what digestion does. It turns what isn't eaten now into something portable and of equal value. Hobbes calls it the sanguification of the commonwealth, its blood, with the treasurers as veins and arteries.

@art colony-ship
Last, procreation. Colonies are the commonwealth's children. One may become a commonwealth of itself, owing only honour and friendship, like an emancipated child. Or it stays united, as Roman colonies did, and is a province. Its letters from the sovereign decide which.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Separate pots for sovereign and subjects@The first|Arbitrary means unjust@The second|Rounding a share@And the third"
The first slip is treating the sovereign's share and the subjects' as separate pots. It's one distribution, by one will. The second is reading arbitrary as unjust. And the third is rounding a share that doesn't divide, which makes the total wrong.
