@chapter Three ways to say do this
@title text="Of Counsel" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 25" art=thumb
Think of asking three friends what to do, one at a time, and then asking them all at once in a group chat. Hobbes thinks you got more out of the first. To see why, he starts with a small phrase: do this.

@art three-speakers
Do this is said by someone who commands, someone who advises, and someone who exhorts. People mistake one for another according to what they want to conclude. So Hobbes fixes the words.

@list heading="Command and counsel" items="Command: the reason is the speaker's will@Command is|Counsel: the reason is your benefit@Counsel is|You can be obliged to obey, not to follow advice@obliged"
Command is where a man says do this, with no reason but his own will. Counsel is where he says do this, and draws his reasons from the benefit to you. So command serves the commander, and counsel the one counselled. You can be obliged to obey a command, but not to follow advice. Covenant to follow it, and it's turned into command.

@words text="Kill not · Sell all thou hast" sub="a command · a counsel"
His examples are from Scripture. Kill not is a command, because the reason is the will of God our King. Sell all thou hast and give it to the poor is counsel, because the reason given is your own treasure in heaven.

@chapter The good counsellor
@art memory-of-the-state
Counsel is experience made known to another. So to the artificial man, counsellors serve in the place of memory and reasoning. But unlike the objects that teach a natural man, a counsellor has ends of his own. So the first condition is that his interest isn't inconsistent with yours.

@list heading="A good counsellor" items="His ends agree with yours@his ends|He reasons clearly@reason clearly|He knows the business@experienced|He knows foreign affairs@foreign affairs"
So his ends must agree with yours. After that, he must reason clearly, without metaphor, be long experienced in the business, and know foreign affairs when they're in question.

@chapter Apart, not in an assembly
@quote text="Supposing the number of Counsellors equall, a man is better Counselled by hearing them apart, then in an Assembly" cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 25"
And the fifth condition. Supposing the number of counsellors equal, a man is better counselled by hearing them apart than in an assembly.

@art brands-enflame
In an assembly, many vote yes or no moved by another's eloquence, or by fear of displeasing the room. Passion, made eloquent, spreads, as many brands enflame one another. Apart, you can interrupt and examine a man's reasons. And many counsellors are dangerous where secrecy matters.

@art wheelbarrow-tennis
His image is tennis. You do best with able seconds, each in his proper place, and worst of all carried in a frame heavy of itself, like a man taken to the ball in a wheelbarrow.

@chapter What Condorcet showed
@art counsellors-apart
There's a famous argument on the other side, which Hobbes couldn't have known. In seventeen eighty five, Condorcet showed that a majority of independent voters, each right more than half the time, is right more often than any one of them.

@math heading="3 counsellors, each right 3/5" items="two right: 3 · (3/5)² · (2/5) = 54/125@exactly two;;all three: (3/5)³ = 27/125@all three;;majority right: 81/125@eighty one;;one alone: 3/5 = 75/125@one counsellor"
Take three counsellors, each right three fifths of the time. Exactly two right is fifty four over a hundred and twenty five. All three is twenty seven. So the majority's right eighty one times in a hundred and twenty five, against seventy five for one counsellor alone.

@words text="11 counsellors: about 75%"
That's about sixty five percent. With eleven counsellors apart, it rises to about seventy five percent. Hearing more counsellors apart helps.

@chapter What the sum assumes
@art one-voice-echo
But the figure assumes the votes are independent, and every one of Hobbes's reasons says they aren't. If ten of eleven just vote as the first speaker did, the assembly is one man's opinion eleven times over. The chance is three fifths, no better than the orator alone.

@words text="Each right 2/5 → eleven right about 25%" sub="a bigger body is wrong more often"
It's also about competence. If each counsellor is right only two fifths of the time, eleven of them are right about twenty five percent of the time. A bigger body is wrong more often.

@words text="The final resolution in one man"
And Hobbes doesn't tally votes. Many eyes see more than one, he says, but only when the final resolution is in one man. Reading his advice as a call for independent evidence is a reading, not his claim.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="More counsellors are always better@The first|The theorem is Hobbes's argument@The second|Command and counsel differ in tone@And the third"
The first slip is thinking more counsellors are always better. The second is taking Condorcet's theorem for Hobbes's argument. And the third is thinking command and counsel differ in tone. A kindly worded command is still a command.
