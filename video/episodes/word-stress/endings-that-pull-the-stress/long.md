@chapter An ending can move the stress
@title text="Endings That Pull the Stress" sub="Word Stress" art=thumb
Educate is strong on its first part. Add t-i-o-n, and you get education, strong on the third. The ending moved the push. Some endings decide where the strong part goes, whatever the word was before.

@art pull-magnet
The first lesson of this course placed the strong part by the class of the word. A word with an ending is different. The ending itself says where the strong part goes, and it can move the push away from where it was in the word you started with.

@chapter Just before the ending
@art before-ending
Two of the rules put the strong part on the part just before the ending. Before t-i-o-n and s-i-o-n: station, education. And before i-c: basic, economic. In each, the push sits right up against the ending.

@chapter The third from the end
@art count-back
Four more rules count back from the end. Before i-c-a-l, i-t-y, a-t-e and i-z-e, the strong part is the third part from the end, with two parts after it. Chemical, political. Ability, community. Celebrate, operate. Realize, organize.

@math heading="Counting back" items="a · bil · i · ty  →  third from the end: bil@Ability has four parts;;e · du · ca · tion  →  just before -tion: ca@Education has four;;cel · e · brate  →  third from the end: cel@Celebrate has three"
Ability has four parts: a, bil, i, ty. Count back three, and you land on bil. Education has four parts too, and the part just before the ending is the third, ca. Celebrate has three, so the third from the end is the first part.

@art long-short
That's why the count is from the end. The same rule gives a different part in a word of a different length. Chemical has three parts and biological has five, and in both, the strong part is the third from the end.

@chapter How often each rule is right
@list heading="Scored against a dictionary" items="-tion, -sion  ·  151 of 152@the rule for t-i-o-n|-ic  ·  28 of 28   ·   -ical  ·  16 of 16@I-c, twenty-eight|-ity  ·  27 of 27   ·   -ate  ·  36 of 36@I-t-y|-ize  ·  13 of 14@I-z-e is"
Counted against the dictionary, the rule for t-i-o-n and s-i-o-n is right for a hundred and fifty-one of a hundred and fifty-two words. I-c, twenty-eight of twenty-eight. I-c-a-l, sixteen of sixteen. I-t-y, twenty-seven of twenty-seven, and a-t-e, thirty-six of thirty-six. I-z-e is right for thirteen of fourteen.

@words text="-ate: verbs of 3 or more parts" sub="set aside: accurate, candidate · create, date, private"
Each rule applies only where it makes sense. The a-t-e rule is for verbs of three or more parts. So accurate and candidate, which aren't verbs, are set aside, and so are short words like create, date and private.

@chapter The two misses
@art base-keeps
There are only two misses. Television has four parts and is strong on the first, though the rule asks for the part before s-i-o-n. Some British speakers do say it there. And characterize is strong on the first, though the rule asks for the second. It's built on character, and it keeps the push of its base.

@chapter The weaker rule
@art ending-itself
The last rule is different. For e-e, and for e-e-r, e-s-e and e-t-t-e, the push goes on the ending itself. Agree. Degree. Engineer. But this rule is weaker. It's right for eight of twelve words, about two in three.

@words text="coffee · committee · employee · refugee" sub="the ending takes no push"
The four misses are coffee, committee, employee and refugee. In the dictionary's first reading, the ending is said like the end of happy, with no push. Many speakers do stress the end of employee and refugee, but the lab scores the first reading.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="PHO·to·graph  ·  pho·TOG·ra·phy@The first|counting from the front@The second slip|trusting -ee like the others@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the stress of photograph stays in photography. It doesn't. Photograph is strong on its first part, photography on its second. The second slip is counting from the front of the word. Count from the end, where the ending is. And the third is trusting the e-e rule like the others. It's the weakest.

@chapter Try it
@words text="community · economic · operate" sub="where is the strong part?"
Your turn. Find the strong part of community, economic and operate. Pause and count back. Community ends in i-t-y, so the third from the end: mu. Economic ends in i-c, so the part just before: nom. And operate: the third from the end, which is the first part, op.
