title: Why the corn bounty cost the public six pounds four

@art two-taxes
Adam Smith said every export bounty makes the public pay twice.

@words text="Bounty 5s a quarter · home price up 4s"
First, taxes pay a bounty of five shillings on each quarter of corn sent abroad. Then, on his supposition, the home price rises four shillings a quarter.

@math items="31 · 4 = 124s = £6 4s@thirty one"
And thirty one quarters are eaten at home for each one exported. So for every five shillings of bounty, buyers pay six pounds four at home.
