@chapter This moment of work
@title text="The Field of Presence" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part Three, chapter 2" art=thumb
Where is time met? In my field of presence: this moment I spend working, with the horizon of the day behind it, and the evening and night ahead.

@art day-weighs
I don't form a picture of my day. It weighs on me. I recall no detail of it, and I still have it in hand. The evening is there too, like the back of a house of which I see only the front.

@words text="retentions and protentions"
Husserl calls these holds on past and future retentions and protentions. One small note: Smith's translation prints protections on these pages, but Husserl's word is protentions, which Smith uses a few pages later.

@chapter Husserl's diagram
@art husserl-diagram
The pages turn on a diagram from Husserl's lectures on internal time. A horizontal line is the series of present moments. From each moment, an oblique line runs down: that moment as it's seen, shaded off, from each later present. The vertical through a present is what that present holds.

@quote text="With the arrival of every moment, its predecessor undergoes a change: I still have it in hand and it is still there, but already it is sinking away below the level of presents; in order to retain it, I need to reach through a thin layer of time." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part Three, chapter 2, p. 484"
Now the passage. A moment arrives, and its predecessor changes. I still have it in hand, but already it's sinking away below the level of presents. To retain it, I reach through a thin layer of time.

@art thin-layer
When a third moment comes, the second changes again. From being a retention, it becomes the retention of a retention, and the layer between it and me thickens.

@words text="Time is not a line, but a network of intentionalities"
So the chapter concludes from the diagram: time is not a line, but a network of intentionalities. Each present is directed at every moment it still holds.

@chapter The lab's counts
@math heading="Three preset: C present" items="B at depth 1@B is at depth one;;A at depth 2@A is at depth two;;column: C, B′, A′′@the column"
The lab counts it. Moments A, B and C, with C present. B is at depth one, held directly. A is at depth two, held through B. The column through C reads C, then B prime, then A double prime.

@math heading="Later preset: D present" items="C at 1, B at 2, A at 3@C is at one;;column: D, C′, B′′, A′′′@reads D"
Let D arrive. C is at one, B at two, A at three. The column reads D, C prime, B double prime, A triple prime. Nothing's added to the past from outside. Every moment already held sinks one layer.

@math heading="Span preset" items="protention span 2 at C@span of two;;protended: D, E@D and E"
Give C a protention span of two, over moments A to E, and the moments held ahead are D and E. The lab adds that as a count. It can't add the chapter's caution: protention traces only the style of what's to come.

@chapter A pebble through water
@words text="one A, identified by a synthesis?"
Now the objection. For A prime to count as a shading of A, mustn't a synthesis identify them as one A, as Kant requires? The chapter says that would abolish time. A would be identifiable for ever, and a sequence would be just a spatial array.

@art pebble-water
What's given isn't A prime first, from which I infer A. It's A itself, seen through A prime, and both through A double prime, as I see a pebble through the mass of water which moves over it.

@art suit-armistice
Identifying syntheses do exist, in express memory. Unsure when a scene took place, I reason that I had this suit made before the armistice, since no English cloth was available after. That reaches a date, not the past itself.

@words text="passive synthesis" sub="clearly not a solution, but merely a pointer to the problem"
Husserl names this unity passive synthesis, and the chapter takes it with a reservation: clearly not a solution, but a pointer to the problem. How faithful this reading of Husserl is, specialists still argue.

@chapter One column
@art column-slice
And the diagram is only an instantaneous cross section of time. What a single column can't show is the passage from one column to the next. That's the next lesson.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="Retention is short-term memory@short term memory|The diagram shows time as a line@as a line|Depth is how faint the past feels@how faint"
Don't say retention is short term memory, a stored copy; a copy would be present, like an ink stain. Don't say the diagram shows time as a line. And don't take a depth for how faint the past feels. It's a count of steps.

@words text="moments A to E, E present" sub="at what depth is B?"
Your turn. Moments A to E, with E present. At what depth is B retained? Then say why retention isn't memory. Next lesson: time as subject, and who lives the passage.
