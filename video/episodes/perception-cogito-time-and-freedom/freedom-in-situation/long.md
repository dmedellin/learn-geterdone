@chapter Twenty years
@title text="Freedom in Situation" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part Three, chapter 3" art=thumb
Having built our life on an inferiority complex for twenty years, it isn't probable that we'll change. A summary rationalism replies: either the free act is possible or it isn't. Probable is a word for statistics.

@art weighted-past
The chapter answers that generality and probability are phenomena. To say the change is improbable means I've made inferiority my abode. This past, though not a fate, has at least a specific weight.

@chapter Two forms of one mistake
@list heading="Two accounts" items="Determinism: conditions cause conduct@Determinism|Absolute freedom: the decision gives conditions their sense@absolute freedom"
The lesson codes the chapter's own pair. Determinism derives class consciousness from the worker's objective condition. Absolute freedom reduces that condition to the awareness the worker arrives at. The chapter calls them two aspects of the same mistake.

@art exploitation
The idealist says: there was exploitation long before there were revolutionaries, so revolt comes from the worker's decision. The chapter keeps the fact, it is not the greatest poverty which produces the most clear sighted revolutionaries, but refuses the conclusion.

@chapter The labourer and the prisoner
@art day-labourer
The day labourer goes from farm to farm at harvest, with no farm and no tools, feeling an anonymous power making a nomad of him. That life can go on without class consciousness. If it appears, it's not because he decided, but because he's perceived that his life is synchronized with the life of the town labourers.

@art prisoner-comrades
And a prisoner under torture who refuses to give the names. Not by a solitary decision. What withstands pain is not, in short, a bare consciousness, but the prisoner with his comrades, or a proudly willed solitude that's still a way of being with others.

@chapter The lab's table
@list heading="Four cases" items="Inferiority complex: y, n@inferiority complex|Hardship without revolutionaries: n, y@hardship|The day labourer: n, n@labourer|The prisoner: n, n@prisoner"
The lab codes four cases, determinism first. The inferiority complex: determinism absorbs it, absolute freedom can't say why change is improbable. Hardship without revolutionaries: the reverse. The day labourer: both fail. The prisoner: both fail.

@math heading="All preset" items="each: 1 of 4 absorbed, 3 failed@each account;;survivors: none@survivors;;split: the complex and the hardship@split;;both fail: labourer, prisoner@both fail"
So each account absorbs one of four and fails three. Survivors: none. The split is the complex and the hardship. Both fail the labourer and the prisoner, the two cases where conduct comes from coexistence with others.

@chapter Never a thing
@quote text="There is, therefore, never determinism and never absolute choice, I am never a thing and never bare consciousness." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part Three, chapter 3, p. 527"
Freedom in situation isn't a column. In the exchange between a situation and the person who takes it up, it's impossible to determine the share of each. There is never determinism and never absolute choice. I'm never a thing, and never bare consciousness.

@words text="We choose our world and the world chooses us."
We choose our world and the world chooses us. Freedom has a field. It shrinks, without ever disappearing, as the room left by the body and by institutions narrows.

@quote text="The fact remains that I am free, not in spite of, or on the hither side of, these motivations, but by means of them." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part Three, chapter 3, p. 529"
So it isn't a compromise, partly free and partly determined. Every explanation of my conduct by my past is true, as a moment of my whole being. The fact remains that I am free, not in spite of these motivations, but by means of them.

@chapter The last page
@art three-questions
The book's last page asks three questions. Shall I make this promise? Shall I risk my life for so little? Shall I give up my liberty to save liberty? There's no theoretical reply. But there's this person you love, and these men around you who live as slaves.

@words text="to teach us to see them clearly once more"
Philosophy's only function, before things or historical situations, is to teach us to see them clearly once more. Here what's needed is silence, since only the hero lives out his relation to men and the world.

@art network
So the book gives the last word to Saint Exupery, who flew over Arras in May nineteen forty. It closes on his passage about a son caught in a fire: man is but a network of relationships, and these alone matter to him.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="Freedom in situation is a compromise@a compromise|The chapter sides with determinism@sides with|Both-fail cases are Merleau-Ponty's score@score"
Don't call freedom in situation a compromise. Don't think the chapter sides with determinism because it denies absolute freedom; it denies both. And don't read the both fail cases as Merleau-Ponty's score.
