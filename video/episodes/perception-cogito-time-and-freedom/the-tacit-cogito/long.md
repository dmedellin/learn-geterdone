@chapter Reading Descartes
@title text="The Tacit Cogito" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part Three, chapter 1" art=thumb
Picture yourself reading Descartes's Second Meditation. By following the words and the argument, you reach the conclusion: because I think, I am.

@art reading-lines
But you've grasped your thought and existence only through language, and the conclusion is about a me in idea, anyone's who reflects. So the true formula of this cogito, the chapter says, would be: one thinks, therefore one is. Language makes us forget it. My eyes follow the lines and lose sight of them.

@chapter Speech is not clothing
@words text="speech cannot be regarded as a mere clothing for thought"
The chapter has prepared this with an account of speech. Speech cannot be regarded as a mere clothing for thought. If it were, language could teach us nothing.

@art speaker-surplus
A good book imposes its meaning on us. And the speaker too must be able to find in his own words more than the thought he put into them. Secondary speech renders a thought already acquired. Originating speech brings one into existence.

@art ninth-symphony
So ideas are cultural objects, like a church or the Ninth Symphony. Once the quadratic equation has appeared, it lives on among us, but it has its historical area, and would need fresh acts of expression if its tradition were destroyed. The non temporal is the acquired.

@chapter Beneath the spoken cogito
@quote text="Behind the spoken cogito, the one which is converted into discourse and into essential truth, there lies a tacit cogito, myself experienced by myself." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part Three, chapter 1, pp. 469–470"
Now the passage. Behind the spoken cogito, the one converted into discourse, there lies a tacit cogito: myself experienced by myself.

@words text="The tacit cogito is a cogito only when it has found expression for itself."
And a page later. The tacit cogito is a cogito only when it has found expression for itself.

@chapter Two accounts
@list heading="Two accounts" items="Thought before language: words are clothing@Thought before language|Language before thought: thought is only words@language before thought"
The lab sets two opponent accounts against the chapter's cases. Thought before language says thought is complete and words are its clothing. And language before thought says thought is nothing but words. The chapter says that would be to forget half the truth.

@list heading="Three cases" items="Descartes's book: y, n@Descartes's book|The speaker's surplus: n, y@speaker's surplus|The illusory love: n, −@illusory love"
The cases, thought first then language first. Descartes's book: I couldn't read it unless I were already in contact with my own life. Thought first absorbs that, language first can't. The speaker's surplus: the reverse. And the illusory love, lived and not known: thought first fails it, language first is silent.

@math heading="All preset" items="thought first: 1 of 3, fails 2@thought first;;language first: 1 of 3, fails 1, silent 1@language first;;survivors: none · split: cases 1 and 2@split"
So thought first absorbs one of three and fails two. Language first absorbs one, fails one, and is silent on one. Survivors: none. The split is the first two cases. The third is n against silent, which isn't a split.

@chapter Singing when happy
@art singing
Neither account is the tacit cogito, and it isn't a column. It's not a silent voice saying I think before I say it aloud. It doesn't constitute the world or the word. It speaks as we sing when we are happy.

@art sleet
The word sleet, I caught on to as one imitates a gesture, a modulation of my body, never inspected as an idea. The tacit cogito knows itself only in extreme situations, in the dread of death or of another's gaze on me, like the infant's grasp on the world at its first breath.

@art field-experience
So what am I, beneath my particular thoughts? I am a field, an experience. With my birth something began that can no longer cease to see or not to see. The cogito retrieves that event.

@chapter Disputed
@words text="the tacit cogito impossible?" sub="a late working note, The Visible and the Invisible, 1964"
This is the chapter's most disputed idea, and among the first to dispute it was Merleau-Ponty himself. A late working note says the tacit cogito is impossible, since even the idea of thinking needs words. Readers disagree whether that retracts the idea, or only the way it's stated here.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="It's a silent inner voice@inner voice|The chapter puts language before thought@puts language|No survivors is the chapter's verdict@verdict"
Don't call the tacit cogito a silent inner voice; that would already be a spoken cogito. Don't say the chapter puts language before thought. And don't take the table's result for the chapter's verdict.
