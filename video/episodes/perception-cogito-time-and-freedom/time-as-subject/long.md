@chapter Only a cross-section
@title text="Time as Subject" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part Three, chapter 2" art=thumb
The last lesson ended on a warning. Husserl's diagram is only an instantaneous cross section. So what is there really?

@art running-off
Not a past, a present and a future, nor separate instants A, B and C. There's one single phenomenon of running off. When the present passes from B to C, B bursts into B prime, and C, which announced its coming, begins at once to lose its substance. Heidegger called this movement an ek stase.

@chapter The columns side by side
@math heading="Walk preset: A to D" items="B: A 1@at B;;C: A 2, B 1@at C;;D: A 3, B 2, C 1@at D"
The lab sets the columns side by side. Moments A to D, with presents B, C and D in turn. At B, A is at depth one. At C, A is at two, B at one. At D, A is at three, B at two, C at one.

@math heading="Read across" items="A: 1, 2, 3@A at;;B: 1, 2@B at;;C: 1@C at;;order A, B, C in every column@the order"
Read it the other way. A at one, two, three. B at one, two. C at one. And the order, A, B, C, is the same in every column.

@art depth-stack
One moment, held at a different depth under each present, and its place among the others never changes. That's how time can pass without the moments losing their place.

@art approach-pass
So C doesn't lose its being when D pushes it into the past. It had been approaching, and its passing is the reverse side of its arriving. Bergson was wrong to explain time's unity by continuity, as if the moments blurred, and right to keep continuity. The moments are distinct, and they run into each other.

@chapter Time is someone
@art fountain
I don't watch the passage as an onlooker. I effect it, as my gesture is already at its goal. We say there is time, as we say there is a fountain, whose water changes while its form remains. The river image is right, not because the river flows, but because it is one with itself.

@quote text="We are not saying that time is for someone, which would once more be a case of arraying it out, and immobilizing it. We are saying that time is someone … We must understand time as the subject and the subject as time." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part Three, chapter 2, p. 490"
Now the passage. Time isn't for someone, which would array it out and immobilize it. Time is someone. We must understand time as the subject, and the subject as time.

@words text="What does not pass in time is the passing of time itself"
This needs a consciousness with no consciousness behind it to watch it, or the regress never ends. It isn't eternity. What does not pass in time is the passing of time itself. The feeling of eternity feeds on time, as the fountain feeds on the water's pressure.

@chapter What no column holds
@math heading="Long and one presets" items="long, A to F: A sinks to depth 5, order kept@A sinks;;one, D present: A 3, B 2, C 1@single present"
The long preset runs A to F, and A sinks to depth five, the order kept. The one preset takes a single present, D: A at three, B at two, C at one. That's the cross section the chapter warns about. It holds every depth, and no passing.

@art columns-gap
The lab can lay columns side by side, but it can't pass from one to the next. And that passing, on the chapter's thesis, is what the subject is.

@chapter Active and passive
@words text="the affecting of self by self"
Heidegger transferred Kant's phrase to time: time is the affecting of self by self. What affects is time as a thrust towards the future. What's affected is time as a series of presents. They're one, because the thrust is just the transition.

@art heartbeat
I'm not the creator of time any more than of my heart beats. I didn't choose to be born, and time flows through me whatever I do. Yet a decision that binds me holds me back from what I was about to become. We're wholly active and wholly passive, because we are the upsurge of time.

@words text="It is always in the present that we are centered"
Here the chapter parts from Heidegger, for whom the future is primary. If time is an ek stase, we can never cease to see it from the present. It is always in the present that we are centred. Whether that's fair to Heidegger is disputed.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="The subject is in time, or outside looking on@outside looking|Time as subject is idealism@idealism|It's a new kind of eternity@eternity"
Don't say the subject is in time like a thing, or outside looking on. Don't hear time as subject as idealism, time placed in the mind. And don't take it for a new kind of eternity. Only the passing doesn't pass.
