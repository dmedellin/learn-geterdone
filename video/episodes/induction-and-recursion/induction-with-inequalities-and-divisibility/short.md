title: Why this induction proof can't start at one
---

@art crossing-curves
Two to the n beats n squared. Except when it doesn't.

@list items="n = 2:  4 vs 4@At two|n = 3:  8 vs 9@at three|n = 4:  16 vs 16@at four|n = 5:  32 > 25@At five"
At two, they tie at four. At three, it's eight against nine. At four, they tie at sixteen. At five, thirty two beats twenty five.

@art starting-cell
So the base case is five. Start at one out of habit, and the theorem you've stated is false.
