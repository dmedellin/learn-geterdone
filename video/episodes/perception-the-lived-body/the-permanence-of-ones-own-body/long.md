@chapter Always there
@title text="The Permanence of One's Own Body" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part One, Chapter 2" art=thumb
Classical psychology noticed that your body doesn't behave like an object. You can turn away from the table or the lamp. Your body is constantly perceived.

@words text="Its presence entails a possible absence"
But does that leave it an object at all? An object is an object because it can be moved away, and in the end disappear from view. Its presence entails a possible absence. So the body's permanence isn't a permanence in the world. It's a permanence on my part.

@chapter The church from the window
@art church-window
From my window only the church tower is visible. That limit is a promise: from elsewhere, the whole church could be seen. Even a prisoner's view is a fact about where he is.

@words text="I should need the use of a second body which itself would be unobservable"
The body's fixed perspective isn't that kind of fact, because every such fact presupposes it. I observe objects with my body. To observe the body itself, I should need the use of a second body, which itself would be unobservable.

@quote text="To say that it is always near me, always there for me, is to say that it is never really in front of me, that I cannot array it before my eyes, that it remains marginal to all my perceptions, that it is with me." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part One, Chapter 2, p. 104"
Here's the passage. Always near me means never in front of me. It's with me.

@chapter Counting faces
@art box-walked-round
The lab builds a model the chapter doesn't: the body as a rigid box. Walk round an ordinary box, one view from each of six sides, and you reach all six faces. Every side can be brought in front of you.

@math heading="Faces reached" items="box walked round: 6 of 6, unseen none@walk round;;own body: front, right, left = 3 of 6@your own eyes;;unseen: back, above, below@unseen;;with two mirrors: 4 of 6@two mirrors"
Walk round the box: six of six, none unseen. From your own eyes, looking down the front and each side, you reach front, right and left, three of six. Unseen: the back, the top of the head, the soles. With two mirrors, the back is added: four of six.

@art own-view-box
And walking doesn't change it, because the eyes go with the body. The box is crude. Its front includes a face no one sees, and a foot can be lifted into view.

@chapter What a mirror shows
@art two-mirrors
The model counts the mirror as a view. It can't tell a reflection from a look. The chapter can. The back in the mirror moves when you move and turns when you turn. It follows your intentions like a shadow. It imitates your actions instead of answering them with new sides.

@words text="That by which there are objects"
So the chapter's claim isn't about the number of faces. The body isn't an object, because it's that by which there are objects. Objects show one side at a time because I'm in a place I can't see. Things are understood through the body, not the other way round.

@chapter Four characteristics
@art alternating-touch
The second mark was the double sensation. When I touch my right hand with my left, can the touched hand feel too? The chapter says the two hands are never touched and touching at the same time. The roles alternate. The body catches itself at work, and starts a kind of reflection.

@list heading="And two more" items="Affective: my foot has a pain@my foot|Kinaesthetic: I move my body directly@move my body"
Third, the body is affective. When my foot hurts, the pain comes from my foot, my foot has a pain, not the way a nail causes one. Fourth, I move outside things by taking hold of them, and I move my body directly, without finding it first. Those relations are, in movement, magic ones.

@chapter Why the psychologists stopped
@art second-body
Why did the psychologists draw no conclusion? They took up the impersonal standpoint of science, and turned the experience of the body into a representation, a psychic fact. Yet the psychologist was himself the fact he studied. That's where psychology could have found its way back to experience.

@list heading="Don't say" items="Touching and touched at once@at once|The body is an object that never leaves@never leaves|A mirror lets you observe your body@mirror"
So don't say the hands are touching and touched at once. Don't say the body is an object that never leaves. And don't take a mirror for a way of observing your body.
