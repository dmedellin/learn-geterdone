@chapter The house next door
@title text="Experience and Objective Thought" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part One, introduction" art=thumb
Part One opens with the house next door. You see it from one angle. It would look different from the right bank of the Seine, from inside, or from an aeroplane.

@words text="The house seen from nowhere?"
So the house itself, it seems, is none of these appearances. In Leibniz's words, it's the projection of all possible perspectives, the position without perspective: the house seen from nowhere.

@words text="Is not to see always to see from somewhere?"
Merleau-Ponty asks one question back. Is not to see always to see from somewhere? A house seen from nowhere would be an invisible house.

@chapter How seeing works
@art film-ashtray
So he describes the seeing. To look at one thing is to let the rest go dim at the edges, without its ceasing to be there. Compare a film. The camera moves in on an ashtray, and you don't see it as the same one, because the screen has no horizons.

@art lamp-mirror
Take the lamp on his table. He credits it with the sides he can see, and with those the chimney, the walls and the table could see. The back of the lamp is just the face it shows to the chimney. Every object is the mirror of all others.

@chapter Seen from everywhere
@quote text="the house itself is not the house seen from nowhere, but the house seen from everywhere. The completed object is translucent, being shot through from all sides by an infinite number of present scrutinies which intersect in its depths leaving nothing hidden." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part One, introduction, p. 79"
So the formula is modified: not seen from nowhere, but from everywhere, so many views crossing that nothing's left hidden.

@words text="a harmonious and indefinite set of views of the object, but not the object in its plenitude"
But read on, because that's not the last word. A human gaze only ever posits one facet. Gather as many gazes as you like, and you still have a harmonious and indefinite set of views, not the object in its plenitude. The synthesis is only presumptive.

@art hidden-pipes
The house has water pipes and a floor, perhaps cracks spreading in its ceilings, and no view ever reaches them.

@chapter Counting views
@art box-views
The lab makes the views countable, with parallel projection, ordinary geometry nobody disputes. The house is a box four wide, three deep, two high. A view is three whole numbers: right, up, and towards the street.

@math heading="Street preset" items="view 0 3 4: length 5@nought, three, four;;front 4/5, above 3/5@front"
The window across the street is at nought, three, four, a direction of length five. It reaches two faces, the front at four fifths and the roof at three fifths. Those fractions are foreshortening in a drawing, not how big the front looks to anyone.

@math heading="Everywhere preset" items="street, garden, east 1 2 2, west −1 2 −2@four views;;union: 5 of 6 faces@union;;most by one view: 3@more than three;;cover: east and west, 2 views@cover"
Now four views: street, garden, and two corners, east and west. The union is five of six faces. No view reaches more than three. And the cover is east and west, two views. The street and the garden add nothing.

@art house-views
The face the house stands on, no view reaches. And the pipes inside aren't faces at all. The figures are exact. But what they count is views, and a set of views, however well they agree, is still a set of views.

@chapter Objective thought
@words text="Objective thought"
To have the house as an absolute object would take infinite perspectives compressed into one act of seeing. So positing the object goes beyond experience. Then experience turns on itself. My body becomes one more object, my eyes bits of matter, and the perspective I see a projection on my retina.

@art crystal
It's not a mistake that could have been avoided. Consciousness tends to posit objects. And yet the absolute positing of a single object is the death of consciousness, congealing existence as a crystal dropped into a solution crystallizes it.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="The house seen from nowhere is the house itself@from nowhere|Seen from everywhere is the last word@last word|The fractions are what is seen@fractions"
Don't take the house seen from nowhere for the house itself. Don't read seen from everywhere as the last word: the synthesis is presumptive. And don't take the lab's fractions for what's seen. Treating perspective as projection on the retina is objective thought's own step.
