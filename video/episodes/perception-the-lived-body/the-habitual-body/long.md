@chapter Present and absent
@title text="The Habitual Body" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part One, Chapter 1" art=thumb
The amputee knows the arm is gone. And he still counts on it. How can both be true?

@words text="No middle term between presence and absence"
Both classical explanations stay inside the objective world. For physiology, the phantom is a fragment of the body's representation that shouldn't be there. For psychology, it's a memory or a judgement. Either way, there's no middle term between presence and absence.

@chapter Knowing where not to look
@art empty-chair
The middle term is in the reports. The anosognosic, who ignores his paralysed arm, can avoid meeting his disability only because he knows where he'd meet it. Think of a friend who has died. We avoid the places where we'd meet the silence, which means we know where they are.

@words text="The phantom arm is not a representation of the arm, but the ambivalent presence of an arm."
The amputee tries to walk on the phantom leg, and a fall doesn't discourage him. Yet he can describe the leg's peculiarities quite well. The phantom arm is not a representation of the arm, but the ambivalent presence of an arm.

@chapter Open to the arm's actions
@quote text="To have a phantom arm is to remain open to all the actions of which the arm alone is capable; it is to retain the practical field which one enjoyed before mutilation." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part One, Chapter 1, p. 94"
What refuses the mutilation is an I committed to a world, still tending towards it. The refusal isn't a deliberate decision. It's not of the kind, I think that.

@art piano-and-cup
The world still holds the objects to be handled and the piano to be played. And the same world gives the loss away. Things that arouse habitual intentions present themselves as usable, and so they appeal to a hand which I no longer have.

@chapter Two layers
@list heading="The body has two layers" items="The habit-body@habit body|The body at this moment@at this moment"
So the chapter's resolution is that the body has two distinct layers: the habit body, and the body at this moment. The handling movements that have gone from the second are still there in the first.

@words text="something one can handle"
For the habit body to stand guarantee for the present one, a thing must stop being something I'm handling and become something one can handle. My body is grasped in a general, impersonal way.

@chapter The cup model
@art reach-arcs
The lab is the lesson's model, crude on purpose. Each layer has a reach on each side. The habit body reaches seventy centimetres on both sides. The body at this moment reaches seventy on the left and nothing on the right. A cup sits on the right at sixty.

@math heading="Cup preset" items="habitual reach, right: 70 cm@seventy on the right;;60 ≤ 70: within the habit-body's reach@sixty is less;;present reach, right: 0 cm@present reach;;60 > 0: outside the present reach@more than nought;;appeal: the cup@appeal"
The habitual reach is seventy on the right, and sixty is less than seventy, so the habit body reaches the cup. The present reach on the right is nought, and sixty is more than nought, so the body at this moment doesn't. What's in the first and not the second is the appeal: the cup.

@art reach-left
Move the cup to the left, and both layers reach it. The appeal is none. But remember: the cup doesn't appeal by being sixty centimetres away. It appeals by being a cup. The habit body isn't a second set of measurements.

@chapter A present that won't recede
@art stuck-clock
The chapter then compares repression. You start a course of action, meet an obstacle you can neither overcome nor give up, and stay caught. Impersonal time goes on, and personal time stops. The phantom is a former present which can't decide to recede into the past.

@art open-slot
That's why memories and emotions can call it up. And the cut nerves? The impulses from the stump keep the limb's place open in the circuit of existence, for the patient's history to fill. The union of soul and body is enacted at every instant.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="He doesn't know the arm is gone@doesn't know|The refusal is a decision@decision|The habit-body is a stored map of reaches@stored map"
Don't say he doesn't know the arm is gone. He knows it as a fact, and counts on it as a power. Don't hear the refusal as a decision. And don't take the habit body for a stored map of reaches. The lab's number is an illustration.
