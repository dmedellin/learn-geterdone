title: He could swat a mosquito, but not point to his nose

@art mosquito-spot
He could find a mosquito bite instantly, eyes shut. He couldn't point to his nose.

@words text="Grasping is not pointing"
That was Schneider, as Gelb and Goldstein reported him. His habitual movements were quick and precise. Movements to order, answering no situation, failed.

@art centripetal-centrifugal
Merleau-Ponty's reading: grasping goes to a place given by the situation. Pointing needs a background built up, a body placed among places.
