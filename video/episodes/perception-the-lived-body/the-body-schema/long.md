@chapter Beside the ashtray?
@title text="The Body Schema" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part One, Chapter 3" art=thumb
Rest your arm on the table. You'd never say it's beside the ashtray, the way the ashtray is beside the telephone.

@art enveloped-parts
The outline of your body is a frontier ordinary spatial relations don't cross. Its parts aren't spread side by side. They're enveloped in each other. In allocheiria, a touch on one hand is felt on the other, and the whole hand is displaced at once, not point by point.

@chapter An ambiguous notion
@words text="the notion of body image is ambiguous"
How do you know where each limb is? Through what Smith translates as the body image. This course says body schema, because image suggests a picture, and the chapter shows it isn't one. As it says itself, the notion of body image is ambiguous.

@list heading="Three definitions" items="A summary of associated images@summary|A Gestalt: a total awareness of posture@Gestalt|An attitude towards a task@attitude"
First, Henry Head and others meant a summary of bodily experience, many associated images. Then a Gestalt, a total awareness of posture, the whole before its parts. And that's overtaken too. The schema takes up the parts in proportion to their value for what you're doing. It's an attitude directed towards a task.

@chapter Situation, not position
@art desk-comet
Lean on your desk with both hands. Only the hands are stressed, and the rest of you trails behind, like the tail of a comet. That's a spatiality of situation, not of position.

@art pipe
Hold your pipe, and you know where your hand is without adding up angles, hand to forearm, forearm to upper arm, arm to trunk. You know where the pipe is, and from that, where your hand is.

@quote text="The word ‘here’ applied to my body does not refer to a determinate position in relation to other positions or to external co-ordinates, but the laying down of the first co-ordinates" cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part One, Chapter 3, p. 115"
Here's the passage. Here, said of your body, isn't one position among others. It's the point from which positions could be counted at all, fixed by the body's hold on what it's doing.

@art theatre-dark
The chapter's image is a theatre. The body's space is like the darkness needed to show up the performance, the background against which a gesture and its aim stand out.

@chapter Two frames
@art room-grid
The lab computes two coordinate frames. The room's, fixed to the floor, east and north. And the body's: how far ahead a thing is, and how far left or right. The body stands at the origin, facing east, and a cup is two metres east and one north.

@math heading="Cup at (2, 1)" items="facing east: ahead 2, left 1@facing east;;facing north: ahead 1, right 2@facing north;;at (1, 0), facing east: ahead 1, left 1@one metre east;;room position throughout: (2, 1)@room position"
Facing east, the cup is ahead two, left one. Turn so you're facing north, and it's ahead one, right two. Walk one metre east, facing east, and it's ahead one, left one. And the room position throughout stays two, one.

@art body-turn
The room figures never move. The body figures change with every turn and step. That's ordinary geometry, and nothing in the chapter disputes it.

@chapter What the lab is not
@words text="Both frames are maps"
What the chapter disputes is that either frame is the body schema. The body frame is still a grid with the body at its origin. The chapter's here is what such a grid presupposes. Your hand on the pipe doesn't consult ahead two, left one.

@words text="there would be no space at all for me if I had no body"
An objector says figure and ground already presuppose objective space. The reply: against means nothing for a subject not set face to face with the world by a body. Homogeneous space borrows its meaning from oriented space. There would be no space at all for me if I had no body.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="The schema is a map the brain consults@map|The Gestalt is the last word@Gestalt|Here is the origin of the body's frame@origin"
Don't say the schema is a map the brain consults. Don't take the Gestalt definition as the last word. And don't read here as the origin of the body's frame. The origin of a grid is still a point in the grid.

@chapter Try it
@words text="body at (0, 0) facing north · cup at (2, 1)" sub="ahead? left or right?"
Your turn. The body stands at the origin facing north, and the cup is at two, one. Where's the cup relative to the body? Then say which figures stayed fixed, and what neither frame is.
