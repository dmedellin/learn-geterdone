title: The one-letter mistake in a proof
---

@art two-tokens
Use one letter where you needed two, and your proof shrinks.

@math items="m = 2k + 1,  n = 2k + 1@two k plus one;;⟹  m = n@forces m equals n"
Say you're proving something about two odd integers, and you write both as two k plus one. That forces m equals n. Now you've only proved the case where they're equal.

@words text="m = 2j + 1,  n = 2k + 1"
Different numbers need different letters. Use j and k.
