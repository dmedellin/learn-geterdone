@chapter Paid for what you make?
@title text="Piece-Wages" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 21" art=thumb
If you've been paid per delivery, per item packed, or per article written, you know the feeling. You're paid for what you made, and working harder pays more. Marx grants the appearance is strong.

@quote text="Wages by the piece are nothing else than a converted form of wages by time, just as wages by time are a converted form of the value or price of labour-power." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 21"
But his claim is that wages by the piece are nothing else than a converted form of wages by time, just as wages by time are a converted form of the value or price of labour-power.

@art two-forms
His first shock is simple. Both forms exist side by side. London compositors worked by the piece and country ones by the day. In the same London saddlery shops, the French were paid by the piece and the English by time, for the same work.

@chapter Twenty-four pieces
@art twenty-four-pieces
Now Marx's figures. The day is twelve hours, six of them paid, and it creates six shillings of new value. An average worker turns out twenty four pieces a day. So a piece is worth threepence.

@math heading="Marx's piece-rate" items="rate: 36d / 24 = 1½d a piece@thirty six pence over twenty four;;value of a piece: 6s / 24 = 3d@threepence;;day's wage: 24 · 1½d = 36d = 3s@three shillings"
The piece rate is the daily value of labour-power over the pieces of a normal day: thirty six pence over twenty four, a penny ha'penny a piece. Each piece is worth threepence and paid a penny ha'penny. The day pays three shillings, half paid and half unpaid, exactly like each hour of a time wage.

@chapter The measure inside the wage
@art waistcoat-hour
What piece wages do is put the measure inside the wage. A piece below average quality isn't paid in full. In London tailoring, a waistcoat was called an hour or half an hour, and master and worker argued over whether a new fashion was an hour's work. A worker who couldn't reach the minimum was dismissed.

@art middleman
Supervision becomes largely unnecessary. And the form suits middlemen, in what England called the sweating system, where a sub contractor lives on the gap between the rate the capitalist pays and the part he lets reach the worker.

@words text="Individual wages up, the average down"
Now it's the worker's own interest to work harder and longer. Earnings spread with skill and stamina. But the competition this sets up tends to raise individual wages above the average, while lowering the average itself.

@chapter Forty-eight pieces
@quote text="then the piece-wage falls from 1½d. to ¾d., as every piece now only represents ¼, instead of ½ of a working hour." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 21"
Here's where appearance and reality part. If productivity doubles and the day supplies forty eight pieces, then the piece wage falls from a penny ha'penny to three farthings, as every piece now only represents a quarter, instead of a half of a working hour.

@math heading="Twice the pieces" items="worker's reading: 48 · 1½d = 6s@six shillings;;capitalist's: 36d / 48 = ¾d a piece@three farthings;;day's wage: 48 · ¾d = 3s@the same three shillings"
The worker's reading is forty eight pieces at the old rate, six shillings. The capitalist's is that the normal day now yields forty eight, so the rate is thirty six pence over forty eight, three farthings. And the day pays the same three shillings.

@art rate-cut
Marx calls that change purely nominal so far. It's where the fights start. Employers use it as a pretext for real cuts, or the higher productivity comes with more intensity. In the lab's speed up case, a worker pushes from twenty four pieces to thirty, the rate is cut to one and a fifth pence, and the day still pays three shillings.

@chapter The ratchet
@art ratchet
Modern labour economics reads piece rates more kindly. Edward Lazear's study of windscreen installers, published in two thousand, found piece rates raised output and pay. The same literature knows Marx's problem as the ratchet effect: workers hold back output when they expect the rate to be cut.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Piece-wages pay for actual effort@The first|The piece-rate as a piece's value@The second|A fast worker is less exploited@And the third"
The first slip is believing piece wages pay for actual effort. Twice the pieces, the same three shillings. The second is reading the rate as the value of a piece. And the third is thinking a fast worker's higher pay changes the rate of exploitation. Each of his pieces is half paid like anyone's.
