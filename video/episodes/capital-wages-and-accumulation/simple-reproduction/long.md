@chapter Run it again
@title text="Simple Reproduction" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 23" art=thumb
A society can no more stop producing than stop consuming. So each year it must turn part of its product back into means of production. That's reproduction, and if production is capitalist, so is reproduction.

@art cycle
If a hundred pounds advanced brings in twenty of surplus value, it must do the same next year and the year after. The surplus value becomes a regular revenue. When all of it is spent on the capitalist's consumption, the scale stays the same. Marx calls that simple reproduction.

@chapter Last week's labour
@words text="Last week's labour pays this week's wage"
The first thing repetition changes is the look of wages. The worker isn't paid until he's worked, so before his wage reaches him he's already produced the fund it comes from. It's his labour of last week, or last year, that pays for his labour-power this week.

@art serf-to-wages
Marx makes it vivid with a serf. A peasant works three days a week on his own land and three on the lord's. Nobody advances him anything. Then one fine morning the lord takes the land, the cattle and the seed. The peasant still works three days for himself and three for the lord. But now his three days come back to him as wages, apparently advanced by the lord.

@chapter Five years
@math heading="£1,000, all surplus consumed" items="years = capital / yearly surplus consumed@divided by;;£1,000 / £200 = 5 years@five years;;5 · £200 = £1,000 consumed@a thousand pounds;;half consumed: £1,000 / £100 = 10 years@ten years"
The second change concerns the whole capital, and it's a single division. A capital of a thousand pounds brings in two hundred of surplus value a year, all consumed. The capital divided by the surplus value consumed each year gives five years. In five years he's consumed a thousand pounds, the sum he advanced. Consume only half, and it takes ten years.

@quote text="After the lapse of a certain number of years the capital value he then possesses is equal to the sum total of the surplus-value appropriated by him during those years, and the total value he has consumed is equal to that of his original capital." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 23"
The capitalist thinks he's consuming the surplus and keeping his capital intact. But what he thinks can't alter facts. After the lapse of a certain number of years the capital value he then possesses is equal to the sum total of the surplus value appropriated by him during those years, and the total value he has consumed is equal to that of his original capital.

@art debtor
Marx compares a man who runs through his property by borrowing against it. He still holds the property, but its value now represents only his debts. Likewise the capitalist still holds a thousand pound capital, but not a single atom of the value of his old capital continues to exist.

@chapter The accountant's reply
@words text="Money has no label"
This is a way of reckoning, and the objection deserves its full weight. Money has no label. An accountant could say just as truly that he spent his profits and kept his capital. Marx's answer is that the question is what the capital's value is now the equivalent of. Readers who find the accountant equally good aren't making an arithmetic mistake.

@chapter Invisible threads
@art cleaning-machinery
The third change is in the worker. He consumes productively at work, and individually at home. Seen as a class, even his eating and sleeping reproduce labour-power for capital, like the cleaning of machinery.

@quote text="The Roman slave was held by fetters: the wage-labourer is bound to his owner by invisible threads." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 23"
The Roman slave was held by fetters: the wage labourer is bound to his owner by invisible threads.

@art cotton-famine
Marx's example is the cotton famine. When the American Civil War cut off cotton and Lancashire's spinners were idle, there were calls to help them emigrate. Edmund Potter wrote to the Times in eighteen sixty three against it: the workers were the trade's trained power. Marx reads him as counting workers among the machinery. The emigration was prevented.

@art invisible-threads
So repetition reproduces more than products and surplus value. It keeps the worker without means of production, and returns him to the market. Capitalist production produces and reproduces the capitalist relation: on one side the capitalist, on the other the wage labourer.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="The capital stays his original property@The first|Wages come from the capitalist's own funds@The second|The worker's private life is outside production@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the capital stays the capitalist's original property however long it runs. The second is thinking wages are advanced from the capitalist's own funds. And the third is treating the worker's private life as outside production.
