@chapter Two jobs, same pay
@title text="Time-Wages" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 20" art=thumb
Two jobs are offered at the same weekly wage. Before you choose, you want to know the hours, because the same money for more hours is less money for each one.

@art nominal-vs-price
That's Marx's first distinction. The nominal wage is the sum received. The price of labour is the sum for a given quantity of labour. To find it, divide the daily value of labour-power by the hours of the normal working day.

@chapter The same 3s
@quote text="If, e.g., the habitual working-day is 10 hours and the daily value of the labour-power 3s., the price of the working hour is 3 3/5d." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 20"
If the habitual working day is ten hours and the daily value of the labour-power three shillings, the price of the working hour is three and three fifths pence.

@math heading="Same 3s, three days" items="10 h: 36d / 10 = 3⅗d an hour@over ten hours;;12 h: 36d / 12 = 3d an hour@over twelve;;15 h: 36d / 15 = 2⅖d an hour@over fifteen"
Three shillings is thirty six pence, and it doesn't move. Over ten hours it's three and three fifths pence an hour. Over twelve, threepence. Over fifteen, two and two fifths. Every day pays three shillings.

@art same-pay-longer-day
So a worker whose day goes from ten hours to fifteen takes home the same money, and the price of his labour has fallen by a third. Nothing on the payslip records it.

@chapter Overtime
@math heading="The price held, the hours raised" items="12 h at 3⅗d = 43⅕d = 3s 7⅕d@three shillings and seven"
The same fraction works the other way. Keep the price at three and three fifths pence, and let him work twelve hours because trade is brisk. His day's wage rises to three shillings and seven and a fifth pence, and the price of labour hasn't changed at all.

@words text="Given the hours, the wage follows the price · given the price, it follows the hours"
That's the general law. Given the hours, the wage depends on the price of labour. Given the price of labour, the wage depends on the hours.

@art overtime-hour
In trades with no legal limit, hours beyond the normal day were paid a little better. Marx does the sum. If a normal hour is paid threepence and creates sixpence, the employer keeps half. Pay an overtime hour fourpence, and he still keeps a third. Better paid overtime is still partly unpaid.

@chapter Short time
@art short-time
Now the reverse: short time, when there isn't enough work, and the worker is employed for only part of the normal day.

@math heading="12-hour normal day, 3d an hour" items="8 h worked: 2s@eight hours;;6 h worked: 1s 6d@six hours"
Take a twelve hour normal day at three shillings, threepence an hour. Employ the worker only eight hours, and he gets two shillings. Six hours, and he gets one and six. He works half of every hour for himself, so to earn six hours' value, he must be employed twelve.

@art idle-and-overworked
Earlier chapters showed the damage of over work. Here Marx finds the sufferings of insufficient employment. And if the employer pays only so much an hour for hours he chooses, there's no normal day at all. He can alternate over work with idleness. The London builders' revolt of eighteen sixty against the hour system was, Marx says, perfectly rational.

@chapter The underselling bakers
@quote text="getting 18 hours' work out of their men for 12 hours' wages." cite="London master bakers, quoted by Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 20"
Competition finishes the movement. A low price of labour pushes the day longer, and a longer day lowers the price. London's full price bakers complained that undersellers were getting eighteen hours' work out of their men for twelve hours' wages.

@art bakers
Marx finds their complaint revealing. They think the normal day is fully paid, and only the extra hours are stolen. Surplus labour inside the normal day doesn't exist for them, because they believe the wage paid for it.

@words text="Robbins, 1930: income or leisure"
Later labour economics treats the hourly wage as a price, and hours as the worker's choice. Lionel Robbins showed in nineteen thirty that a higher hourly wage can lead to more hours or fewer. Marx assumes the opposite: the employer, custom and law fix the normal day, and the worker takes it or leaves.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="A higher hourly rate means better paid@The first|Better-paid overtime is fully paid@The second|Dividing by the hours actually worked@And the third"
The first slip is taking a higher hourly rate for a better paid worker. The day's wage depends on the hours too. The second is thinking better paid overtime is fully paid. And the third is computing the hourly price from the hours worked, instead of the normal day.
