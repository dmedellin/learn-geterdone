@chapter The lot of the working class
@title text="Accumulation with Constant Composition" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 25" art=thumb
Chapter twenty five's subject is stated in its first line: the influence of the growth of capital on the lot of the working class. And the most important factor, Marx says, is the composition of capital.

@art two-compositions
He defines it twice. In value, it's the ratio of constant to variable capital, c to v. In material, it's the mass of means of production against the labour needed to work them. The value composition, so far as it mirrors the material one, he calls the organic composition.

@quote text="Accumulation of capital is, therefore, increase of the proletariat." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 25, §1"
Hold the composition fixed, and part of each year's surplus value becomes additional capital, part of it variable. So the demand for labour grows in proportion with capital. More capitalists, or larger, at one pole. More wage workers at the other. Accumulation of capital is, therefore, increase of the proletariat.

@chapter The steady case
@math heading="c/v = 1 · s/v = 100% · half capitalised" items="growth a period: ½ · 100% · ½ = ¼@a quarter;;capital: 5,120 · 6,400 · 8,000 · 10,000 · 12,500@fifty one twenty;;workers wanted: 256 · 320 · 400 · 500 · 625@two hundred and fifty six;;supply: 300 · 400 · 500 · 600 · 700@three hundred"
The lab starts half constant, half variable, with illustrative figures. Capital of fifty one twenty pounds pays twenty five sixty in wages, so at ten pounds a head it wants two hundred and fifty six workers. Capital grows by a quarter each period, and so does demand: three twenty, four hundred, five hundred, six twenty five. In the steady case, the supply starts at three hundred and grows by a hundred, always ahead.

@art demand-vs-supply
But sooner or later, Marx says, accumulation outruns the customary supply of labour, and wages rise. The complaint was heard in England through the fifteenth century and the first half of the eighteenth.

@chapter The labouring poor
@art mines-of-the-rich
The earlier economists saw the link clearly. John Bellers wrote in sixteen ninety six of the labour of the poor being the mines of the rich. Mandeville thought the poor should be kept from starving, but receive nothing worth saving. What Mandeville hadn't seen, Marx says, is that accumulation itself produces the labouring poor along with the capital.

@art golden-chain
Under these conditions, the most favourable to the worker, he can buy a little more and put something by. Marx grants it with an image. A rise in wages means only that the length and weight of the golden chain the wage worker has already forged for himself allow a relaxation of its tension.

@chapter The alternative
@list heading="Two outcomes" items="Wages rise without checking accumulation@Either|Accumulation slackens, wages fall back@Or"
Why can't the rise go on for ever? Marx states an alternative. Either wages go on rising, because the rise doesn't interfere with accumulation. Or accumulation slackens, because the rise blunts the stimulus of gain. Then the gap between capital and labour-power closes, and wages fall back.

@quote text="the rate of accumulation is the independent not the dependent, variable; the rate of wages, the dependent, not the independent, variable." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 25, §1"
To put it mathematically, he says: the rate of accumulation is the independent not the dependent, variable; the rate of wages, the dependent, not the independent, variable.

@math heading="When demand passes supply" items="tight, period 3: 400 wanted · 380 available@three eighty;;s: £3,600 instead of £4,000@thirty six hundred;;supply stuck at 300: s from £2,800 to about £1,181@eleven eighty one"
In the tight case, supply grows by only forty. In period three capital wants four hundred and only three eighty are available. All are employed at a higher wage, and the extra comes out of surplus value: thirty six hundred pounds, not four thousand. Stop the supply at three hundred, and surplus value falls from twenty eight hundred to about eleven eighty one by period five. The rise in wages eats its own cause.

@art spreading-wage
That's the lab's own rule, not a formula of Marx's: the variable capital is spread over the workers there are, as a higher wage. The chapter states the direction and the limit.

@chapter Population, or capital?
@art arrow-reversed
Marx's target is the economists who read it backwards, as a law of population: few workers, high wages, the poor multiply, wages fall. He reverses the arrow. The movement of wages reflects the movement of capital, a relation between unpaid and paid labour of the same working population.

@words text="Solow, 1956"
A modern version of the view he opposed is standard. In Robert Solow's growth model of nineteen fifty six, capital growing faster than labour raises the real wage, braked only by diminishing returns. Marx's reaction depends on the rise coming out of unpaid labour. The lab builds that in, so it can't test it.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Rising wages benefit the worker without limit@The first|Population as the independent variable@The second|Value and technical composition confused@And the third"
The first slip is taking rising wages as proof that accumulation benefits the worker without limit. The chain is relaxed, not removed. The second is making population the independent variable. And the third is confusing the value composition with the technical one.
