@chapter The payslip
@title text="The Value of Labour-Power Becomes Wages" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 19" art=thumb
If you've ever been paid by the hour, you know this from the inside. The payslip says so many hours at so much an hour. It's hard to read that as anything but a price for the work you did.

@quote text="On the surface of bourgeois society the wage of the labourer appears as the price of labour, a certain quantity of money that is paid for a certain quantity of labour." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 19"
Marx doesn't call the payslip a fraud. He calls it a surface. On the surface of bourgeois society the wage of the labourer appears as the price of labour, a certain quantity of money that is paid for a certain quantity of labour.

@chapter A phrase that defeats itself
@art tautology
His first objection: the value of a commodity is measured by the labour in it. So the value of a twelve hour day would be the twelve hours it contains. An absurd tautology.

@art two-horns
His second is sharper. Suppose twelve hours are embodied in six shillings. Pay the worker six, and equivalents are exchanged, nothing's left over, and money never becomes capital. Pay him less, and twelve hours exchange for fewer, breaking the law of equal exchange in the very act it should govern.

@quote text="Labour is the substance, and the immanent measure of value, but has itself no value." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 19"
What meets the buyer on the market is not labour but the labourer, who sells his labour-power. Once his labour begins, it already belongs to the buyer. Labour is the substance, and the immanent measure of value, but has itself no value.

@art payslip
The classical economists half found this out. Looking for the natural price of labour, they ended up asking what it costs to produce the labourer. That's the value of labour-power, as different from labour as a machine is from the work it performs. They found the right category and kept the wrong name.

@chapter Twelve hours for three shillings
@math heading="Marx's figures" items="day's wage: 3s = 36d@three shillings;;apparent price of an hour: 36d / 12 = 3d@threepence;;value created in an hour: 6d@sixpence;;day: 12 · 6d = 72d = 6s@six shillings;;unpaid: 6s − 3s = 3s@three shillings unpaid"
The day is twelve hours. Labour-power is worth three shillings, the money form of six hours. Written as a price of labour, an hour costs three shillings over twelve, threepence. But an hour creates sixpence of new value, so the day creates six shillings. That leaves three shillings unpaid.

@art half-paid
Labour that creates six shillings seems to have a value of three. Marx calls it absurd at first sight. The three shillings pays for six hours, and it appears as the price of all twelve.

@chapter Three ways to divide a day
@art corvee-slave-wage
Compare three ways of dividing a day. Under the corvee, the serf works for himself some days and for the lord on others, in plain sight. Under slavery, even the hours replacing the slave's keep look like work for the master. In wage labour, on the contrary, even surplus labour, or unpaid labour, appears as paid.

@art machine-hire
Marx gives the appearance its due. Money is handed over for something not money, as in every sale. The worker is paid afterwards, like a debt. And the buyer receives tailoring or spinning, a definite useful labour. Wages rise for a longer day, but hiring a machine for a week costs more than for a day, too.

@math heading="Smith's error" items="labour-power 4s: 4d an hour · day creates 6s@four shillings;;labour-power 2s: 2d an hour · day creates 6s@worth two"
The worker always gives twelve hours. So when his labour-power's value moves, he sees the price of his twelve hours move. That misled Adam Smith. In the lab, labour-power worth four shillings gives fourpence an hour, and worth two gives twopence. The twelve hours create six shillings throughout.

@chapter The marginal product
@words text="J. B. Clark, 1899: paid what you add"
The strongest objection came later. John Bates Clark argued in eighteen ninety nine that under competition the wage equals what the last worker hired adds to the product. Then nothing is unpaid. Marx's six against three counts all new value as labour's creation. The lab computes on Marx's definition, and can't decide between them.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="The wage as the price of work done@The first|The value of labour is fixed@The second|Higher pay for longer days proves labour is paid@And the third"
The first slip is taking the wage as the price of the work done. The second is Smith's: thinking the value of labour is fixed and only money varies. And the third is reading higher pay for a longer day as proof that labour is paid.
