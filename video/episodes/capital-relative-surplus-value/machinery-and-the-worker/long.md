@chapter Machinery and the family
@title text="Machinery and the Intensity of Labour" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 15" art=thumb
When the law shortened the working day, many manufacturers said surplus value would shrink with it. Marx says capital found another dimension. But first, two other effects of machinery.

@art whole-family
Machinery needs no strength, so the first labour capital sought for it was women's and children's. The value of labour-power was the cost of keeping a worker and his family. Once the whole family works, that value is spread over the whole family, and four people give surplus labour where one did.

@chapter The long day
@art idle-mill
The second effect is the long day. A machine is an industrial perpetual motion, stopped only by the bodies that tend it. The longer it runs, the sooner its value comes back, and the less it risks being made obsolete first. A cotton master told Senior that a man who lays down his spade idles eighteenpence of capital, but one who leaves the mill idles a hundred thousand pounds.

@words text="You can't squeeze as much from 2 as from 24"
Behind this lies a contradiction. Machinery raises the rate of surplus value by cutting the number of workers. But it's impossible to squeeze as much surplus value out of two labourers as out of twenty four. Lengthening the day is how capital tries to make up the numbers.

@chapter The denser hour
@art legal-limit
The long day provoked the legal limit, twelve hours from eighteen thirty three, and ten from eighteen forty seven. From then on, Marx says, a point must be reached where the length of the day and the intensity of labour exclude each other. With length fixed, capital turned to intensity.

@quote text="The denser hour of the ten hours' working-day contains more labour, i.e., expended labour-power, than the more porous hour of the twelve hours' working-day." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 15, §3(c)"
The denser hour of the ten hours' working day contains more labour, that is, expended labour-power, than the more porous hour of the twelve hours' working day.

@art pores-in-the-hour
A denser hour has fewer pores: fewer moments of rest, more motions, more attention. So it counts for more value. If one hour of the ten hour day holds as much labour as one and a fifth hours of the old day, then ten such hours hold as much as twelve.

@math heading="Ten denser hours for twelve" items="labour spent: 10 h · 6/5 = 12 h@ten hours times six fifths;;surplus: 12 − 8 = 4 h · rate 4/8 = 50%@fifty percent;;necessary in clock time: 8 / (6/5) = 6⅔ h@six and two thirds;;surplus in clock time: 4 / (6/5) = 3⅓ h@three and a third"
The old day was eight hours necessary and four surplus. Now, ten hours times six fifths is twelve hours of labour of normal intensity. Take away the eight necessary, and four hours are surplus: fifty percent, just as before. In clock time, that's six and two thirds hours necessary and three and a third surplus.

@chapter Four days compared
@math heading="8 normal hours necessary" items="12 h at normal intensity: 4 / 8 = 50%@twelve hours at normal;;10 h, no change: 2 / 8 = 25%@no change in intensity;;10 h at 6/5: 4 / 8 = 50%@six fifths;;12 h at 4/3: 8 / 8 = 100%@a third"
In the lab, twelve hours at normal intensity give fifty percent. Ten hours with no change in intensity, the case the manufacturers argued from, give twenty five. Ten hours at six fifths are back at fifty. And twelve hours with intensity up by a third give a hundred percent, exactly the combination Marx says can't last.

@art more-looms
How was the hour made denser? First by the worker, who can exert more in a shorter day. When Robert Gardner cut his Preston mills from twelve hours to eleven, a year later he got the same product for the same cost. Then by machinery: faster spindles, more looms to each weaver. By eighteen sixty three, twelve hours' work was compressed into less than ten.

@chapter The Pindar of the factory
@quote text="when capital enlists science into her service, the refractory hand of labour will always be taught docility." cite="Andrew Ure, The Philosophy of Manufactures, 1835, quoted by Marx"
Andrew Ure, in eighteen thirty five, praised the automatic factory for relieving the heaviest toil, and wrote that when capital enlists science into her service, the refractory hand of labour will always be taught docility.

@art appendage
Marx calls him the Pindar of the automatic factory. The worker becomes the machine's mere living appendage, and the lightening of the labour, even, becomes a sort of torture. And the inspectors who praised the shorter day reported that the new pace was itself injuring health.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="A shorter day always means less surplus@The first|Measuring labour by the clock alone@The second|Any day can be both long and intense@And the third"
The first slip is thinking a shorter day always means less surplus value. The second is measuring labour by the clock alone, when intensity varies. And the third is assuming any day can be both long and intense.
