title: How cheaper bread raises the rate of surplus value

@art fixed-day
The working day can't get any longer. So how does the surplus grow?

@math items="2 / 10 = 20%@twenty percent;;3 / 9 = ⅓@a third"
In a twelve hour day, ten hours replace the wage and two are surplus: twenty percent. Make the worker's necessaries cheaper, so nine hours replace the wage. Now it's three over nine, a third.

@words text="Same day, same wage goods, less necessary time"
The worker buys exactly what he bought before. Marx calls it relative surplus value.
