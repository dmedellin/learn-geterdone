@chapter Three circumstances
@title text="Changes in the Price of Labour-Power and in Surplus Value" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 17" art=thumb
Think of a pay rise in a year when prices rose too. Are you better off? Chapter seventeen asks that kind of question about the working day. A day's labour creates a value. Labour-power takes part, and the capitalist the rest.

@art three-dials
Three things can change. The length of the working day. The intensity of labour, how much is spent in a given time. And the productiveness of labour, how much product a given quantity of labour yields. Marx changes them one at a time.

@chapter Productivity
@quote text="A working day of given length always creates the same amount of value, no matter how the productiveness of labour, and, with it, the mass of the product, and the price of each single commodity produced, may vary." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 17"
First, productivity, with length and intensity fixed. Marx states three laws, and the first carries the others. A working day of given length always creates the same amount of value, no matter how the productiveness of labour may vary.

@art fixed-pie
If a twelve hour day creates six shillings, that's all there is to divide. So labour-power and surplus value move in opposite directions, by the same amount. And the movement starts with the value of labour-power, which falls only when the necessaries cheapen.

@math heading="Productivity rises, 12-hour day" items="before: labour-power 4s, 8 h · surplus 2s, 4 h · 50%@four shillings;;after: labour-power 3s, 6 h · surplus 3s, 6 h · 100%@three shillings"
Marx's figures. Labour-power worth four shillings, eight hours, and surplus value two shillings, four hours: fifty percent. Productivity rises, and labour-power falls to three shillings, six hours. Surplus value rises to three shillings. A hundred percent.

@words text="−¼ for labour-power · +½ for surplus value"
The same shilling moved each way, but labour-power fell by a quarter while surplus value rose by half. Ricardo, who first stated these laws, missed that. Worse, Marx says, he knew no change in length or intensity, and confused the rate of surplus value with the rate of profit.

@chapter Intensity
@art denser-day
Second, intensity, with length and productivity fixed. A more intense day is embodied in more value. The twelve hours that created six shillings may create eight. Now both parts can grow at once, from three shillings each to four each.

@words text="A higher wage, below value?"
So a rise in wages needn't mean the worker is paid above the value of labour-power, since he's also wearing it out faster. In the lab, the intensity case holds labour-power at six hours, so all the extra goes to surplus value: sixteen normal hours on six necessary.

@chapter Length
@art longer-day
Third, length. A longer day creates more value, and with labour-power still worth three shillings, the extra is all surplus. Twelve hours become fourteen, and surplus labour goes from six hours to eight. Past a point, Marx adds, the extra wear grows geometrically.

@chapter All together
@math heading="Dearer bread, a longer day" items="new necessary time: 6 h / (3/4) = 8 h@six hours to eight;;12-hour day: 4 / 8 = 50%@fifty percent;;14-hour day: 6 / 8 = 75%@seventy five;;16-hour day: 8 / 8 = 100%@sixteen"
Now combine them. The soil grows less fertile, food grows dearer, and labour-power rises from three shillings to four, six hours to eight. On a twelve hour day surplus is four hours, fifty percent. At fourteen hours it's six over eight, seventy five percent. At sixteen, the rate is back at a hundred percent, and surplus labour has grown by a third.

@art dear-bread
Marx uses this against West and Ricardo, who read the dear bread of seventeen ninety nine to eighteen fifteen as proof that the rate of surplus value fell. In fact, he says, the day was lengthened and intensified, and the rate rose.

@quote text="In capitalist society spare time is acquired for one class by converting the whole life-time of the masses into labour-time." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 17"
The chapter ends the other way round: higher productivity and intensity with a shorter day. Marx puts it in terms of class. In capitalist society spare time is acquired for one class by converting the whole life time of the masses into labour time.

@art spare-time
The necessary time sets the lower limit of the day. Only the end of capitalist production, Marx says, would let the working day shrink toward it, and turn the time saved into free time for everyone.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Productivity raises the day's value@The first|Wages and surplus always move opposite@The second|A wage rise must be above value@And the third"
The first slip is thinking higher productivity raises the value a worker produces each day. It raises the number of things, not their value. The second is expecting wages and surplus value always to move in opposite directions. And the third is reading every wage rise as a rise above value.
