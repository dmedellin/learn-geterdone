@chapter Nothing seems to change
@title text="Co-operation" sub="Capital, Volume I, Chapter 13" art=thumb
Capitalist production starts where one capital employs many workers at once. At first, Marx says, nothing seems to change. It's a medieval workshop, simply enlarged.

@art enlarged-workshop
If a twelve hour day is embodied in six shillings, twelve hundred such days are embodied in twelve hundred times six shillings, together or apart. Numbers alone don't change the rate of surplus value. Then come the changes that make numbers matter.

@chapter Averaging
@art average-platoon
The first is averaging. One worker is quick, another slow. But twelve together, twelve hours each, make a collective day of a hundred and forty four hours, and the differences cancel. Burke, writing as a farmer, said that in so small a platoon as five labourers, individual differences vanish. So the law of value holds for an employer only when he employs many.

@chapter Shared means of production
@art one-workshop
The second is economy. A room for twenty weavers must be larger than a room for one. But it costs less labour to build one workshop for twenty than ten workshops for two each. The shared building gives up less value to each yard of cloth.

@math heading="Twelve masons and their tackle" items="alone: 10s / 12 pieces = 10d a piece@ten pence a piece;;together: 12 · 12 = 144 pieces@a hundred and forty four;;10s / 144 = ⅚d a piece@five sixths;;combined day: 12 · 12 h = 144 h@combined day"
In the lab, a mason alone needs ten shillings of ladder, scaffold and tools, and makes twelve pieces a day: ten pence a piece. Twelve masons sharing the same tackle make a hundred and forty four pieces. Each carries five sixths of a penny. And their combined day is a hundred and forty four hours.

@words text="Workshop for twelve: 60s → 5d a piece"
Build a room for twelve at sixty shillings, six times one worker's ten, and it still halves the constant capital, to five pence a piece. What falls isn't the building's cost, but the value it passes to each unit.

@chapter A new power
@art masons-ladder
The third is a new power. A squadron of cavalry isn't the sum of its riders. Twelve masons in a row, passing stones up a ladder, move them faster than twelve men each climbing with his own load. A group working on a building from every side has hands and eyes both before and behind.

@art harvest
Some work must be done at a critical moment, a field harvested before the weather turns. One man can carve twelve hours out of a natural day. A hundred men together make a day of twelve hundred hours, at the moment it's needed.

@chapter Who pays?
@quote text="He pays them the value of 100 independent labour-powers, but he does not pay for the combined labour-power of the hundred." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume I, Chapter 13"
So who pays for all this? The capitalist contracts with each worker separately, for that worker's own labour-power. He pays them the value of a hundred independent labour-powers, but he does not pay for the combined labour-power of the hundred.

@words text="A power immanent in capital"
Because this power costs capital nothing, and the worker doesn't develop it until his labour belongs to capital, it looks like a power capital has by nature. In none of the lab's cases is a wage an input. The wages buy the labour-powers, and the combination comes with them.

@chapter The conductor
@art orchestra
Combined labour needs direction. A single violin player is his own conductor. An orchestra requires a separate one. Marx grants the need, and denies the inference. Under capital, directing is also supervising people whose interests run against the employer's. He's a leader of industry because he's a capitalist, not the other way round.

@words text="Coase, 1937: coordination as a service"
The obvious reply sees the organiser's work as productive, paid at its price. Coase argued in nineteen thirty seven that firms exist because coordination inside them is cheaper than through the market. Marx notes that large capitals hand supervision to managers paid a wage, while capital's return remains.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Each is paid in full, so all is paid for@The first|Shared means are worth less@The second|Direction as a purely technical job@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the extra is paid for because each worker is paid in full. The second is reading the shared means of production as worth less, rather than spread wider. And the third is treating direction as a purely technical job.
