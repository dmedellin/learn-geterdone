@words text="Bigger numbers, fewer steps?"
Finding a gcd can take fewer steps for bigger numbers. Here's proof.

@math items="gcd(1071, 462): 3 divisions@one thousand and seventy one;;gcd(89, 55): 9 divisions@eighty nine"
Euclid's algorithm finds the gcd of one thousand and seventy one and four hundred and sixty two in three divisions. Eighty nine and fifty five take nine.

@art fibonacci-squares
Eighty nine and fifty five are consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Every quotient is one, the slowest the algorithm can ever go. The quotients decide, not the size.
