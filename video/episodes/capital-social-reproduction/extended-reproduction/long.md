@chapter A new arrangement
@title text="Reproduction on an Enlarged Scale" sub="Capital, Volume II, Chapter 21" art=thumb
Now Marx runs his scheme of accumulation, year by year. He starts from what he calls the initial diagram.

@math heading="Initial diagram" items="I:  4000c + 1000v + 1000s = 6000@four thousand;;II:  1500c + 750v + 750s = 3000@fifteen hundred;;I(v + s) = 2000 > IIc = 1500@two thousand against"
Department one: four thousand of constant capital, a thousand of variable, a thousand of surplus value. Department two: fifteen hundred, seven fifty and seven fifty. Now department one's v plus s is two thousand against department two's c of fifteen hundred, a gap of five hundred.

@art rearranged
The total is the same nine thousand as the simple scheme, and department one is the same. What changed is department two. It's not the quantity, Marx says, but the destination of the elements that changes.

@art keep-at-home
That gap of five hundred is exactly the half of department one's surplus value it'll keep for itself, as material for next year's growth.

@chapter The first year
@math heading="Department I accumulates half" items="I accumulates:  ½ · 1000 = 500 = 400c + 100v@four hundred;;I's demand on II:  1000 + 100 + 500 = 1600@sixteen hundred;;II adds:  1600 − 1500 = 100c, and 50v@add a hundred"
Department one accumulates five hundred, four hundred as new constant capital and a hundred as new variable. Its demand on department two is its thousand of wages, the hundred of new wages and the five hundred consumed: sixteen hundred. So department two must add a hundred to its constant capital, and fifty to its variable.

@art ii-follows
Department two's accumulation isn't chosen. It's computed. It's whatever is needed to absorb what department one sells it.

@math heading="After one year" items="I:  4400c + 1100v@forty four hundred;;II:  1600c + 800v@sixteen hundred;;next product:  6600 + 3200 = 9800@ninety eight hundred"
The capitals become forty four hundred and eleven hundred in department one, sixteen hundred and eight hundred in department two. At the same rate of surplus value, next year's product is sixty six hundred plus thirty two hundred: ninety eight hundred.

@chapter Year by year
@art growth-stairs
Run it on. Marx's products are ninety eight hundred, then ten thousand seven hundred and eighty, then eleven thousand eight hundred and fifty eight. After the first year, both departments grow by a tenth a year.

@words text="9,000 → 13,043⅘ in four periods" sub="the lab keeps every fraction"
In the fourth year Marx rounds off fractions, and one printed total carries a slip. The lab keeps every fraction, and over four periods the product grows from nine thousand to thirteen thousand and forty three and four fifths.

@chapter When it's refused
@art refused
Now give department two a surplus value of only a hundred, instead of seven fifty. Balance needs it to accumulate a hundred and fifty: a hundred of constant capital and fifty of variable. It only has a hundred. The first period is refused.

@words text="I(v + s) = IIc is irreconcilable with capitalist production"
Marx draws two conclusions. The equality of simple reproduction is irreconcilable with capitalist production, which accumulates.

@art great-crash
And if earlier accumulation ever left department two's c greater than department one's v plus s, the result would be overproduction in department two that could only be compensated by a great crash.

@chapter The great argument
@words text="Tugan-Baranovsky · Luxemburg · Bauer · Bukharin"
No part of volume two has been argued over more. Tugan Baranovsky read these schemes as proof that capitalism can expand without limit if the proportions are kept. Rosa Luxemburg objected that Marx makes department two accumulate passively, and asked who buys the growing product. Bauer and Bukharin answered her.

@words text="If things are to proceed normally"
What in the chapter bears on their dispute is the condition Marx states: what department two must do if things are to proceed normally. The chapter doesn't show what makes department two keep to it. The lab takes department two following as given, which is exactly the step Luxemburg disputed.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Departments grow at any rates@The first|Growth needs a bigger product@The second|Rounded figures are exact@And the third"
The first slip is thinking the two departments can grow at any rates they like. Once department one is set, department two's accumulation is fixed. The second is expecting enlarged reproduction to need a larger product. Marx's smaller diagram has only eight thousand two hundred and fifty two. And the third is reading Marx's rounded figures as exact.
