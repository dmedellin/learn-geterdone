title: Never cancel this factor in a differential equation
---

@art lost-root
Cancel one factor, and a whole solution of your equation disappears.

@math items="y′ = y² − y@y squared minus y;;÷ y:  only y = 1@divide;;y·(y − 1):  y = 0 and y = 1@factored"
Take y prime equals y squared minus y. Divide by y, and it looks as if there's one equilibrium, at one. But factored, it's y times y minus one, with two, zero and one.

@words text="Factor and set to zero"
The constant zero solves the equation, and the division never saw it. So factor and set to zero. Never cancel.
