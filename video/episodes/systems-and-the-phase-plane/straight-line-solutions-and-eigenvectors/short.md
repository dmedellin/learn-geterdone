You found two, two. The answer says one, one. Who's wrong?

@math items="A·(2, 2) = (6, 6) = 3·(2, 2)@two, two;;A·(1, 1) = (3, 3) = 3·(1, 1)@one, one"
For the matrix with rows two, one and one, two, A times two, two is six, six, three times two, two. And A times one, one is three, three, three times one, one.

@words text="An eigenvector is a direction"
Neither. They're the same line through the origin, and only the eigenvalue, three, is a single number.
