title: One negative eigenvalue isn't enough

@art saddle-one-line
One eigenvalue is negative. So the origin is stable, right?

@math items="rows 1 2 and 3 0:  λ = −2, 3@minus two and three;;e^(−2t)·(2, −3) → 0@does tend;;e^(3t)·(1, 1) grows@grows"
For rows one, two and three, zero, the eigenvalues are minus two and three. The solution along two, minus three does tend to the origin. But the one along one, one grows.

@words text="Δ = −6 < 0: saddle"
The determinant is minus six, so it's a saddle. Stability needs every eigenvalue to decay.
