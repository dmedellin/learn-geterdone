@art near-miss
This point makes one rate zero. It still isn't a resting point.

@math items="x′ = y − x²,   y′ = x − y@x prime equals;;(2, 4):  f = 4 − 4 = 0@four minus four;;g = 2 − 4 = −2@two minus four"
Take x prime equals y minus x squared, and y prime equals x minus y. At two, four, f is four minus four, zero. But g is two minus four, minus two.

@words text="Both rates zero, at the same point"
So x is still, but y keeps falling. An equilibrium needs both rates zero at once.
