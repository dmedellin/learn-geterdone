@chapter The question
@title text="The Full Battery" sub="Reading Faster · Your Training Plan" art=thumb
A reader is more than a rate. So at the end of the Subject, the question isn't just how fast you read now. It's which of four measures have moved, and which of them can even say.

@chapter Four measures
@list heading="The battery" items="Effective rate@Effective rate|Skim gist@skim gist|Scan time@scan time|Recall@recall"
The battery has four rows. Effective rate, from tests at natural pace with questions. Skim gist, the gist questions answered after a timed skim. Scan time, in seconds per target found. And recall, the idea units recalled out of the passage's units.

@art four-savings
Why four? Because time spent reading is saved in four places. Understanding more per minute. Knowing when a skim is enough. Finding a fact without reading for it. And keeping what was read, so it needn't be read again.

@art four-dials
The four move separately, and a reader can improve on one and not the others. But only one of them, the effective rate, has a baseline range and a series of tests behind it.

@chapter Fresh, in one sitting
@art fresh-sitting
Do the battery fresh, at a time you'd otherwise do the Daily Drill, and not after another drill. A tired battery measures the tiredness, and one spread over days measures the days. Start with the warm-up, a test reading about how the Dutch kept low land dry.

@words text="Fill from my log"
Then fill the profile from your log. It takes your baselines and tests for the effective rate, and the first and latest of your skims, scans and free recalls for the other three rows.

@chapter After practice
@math heading="After-practice preset" items="baselines 238, 245, 251: median 245, top 251@two thirty eight;;tests 262, 270, 266: median 266@two sixty two;;266 − 251 = 15 above: a change@fifteen above"
Here's a reader some weeks on. Baselines of two thirty eight, two forty five and two fifty one: a median of two forty five. The last three tests are two sixty two, two seventy and two sixty six. The median is two sixty six, fifteen above the top of the range. By the Subject's rule, a change.

@words text="Verdict tile: “above once”" sub="it judges the latest test alone"
One catch. The profile's verdict tile judges the latest test alone, so it prints above the baseline range once, with its one in four warning. The rule asks for the median of the last three, and that's also two sixty six.

@math heading="The other three rows" items="gist: 2/3 → 3/3@two of three;;scan: 12 s → 8 s a target@twelve seconds;;recall: 5/12 → 7/12@five of twelve"
The same reader's other rows. Gist after a skim, from two of three to all three. Scan time, from twelve seconds a target to eight. Free recall, from five of twelve idea units to seven.

@art range-only-one
What can be said of these? Each is one reading before and one after, on a different text each time. The gist moved by one question, the smallest step it can take. Each is promising, and none has a range, so each has too few readings to say.

@chapter Rate flat
@art profile-bars
The rate flat preset is the reader a one number view forgets. Effective rate two forty five, then two forty four, inside the range. Gist two of three both times. Recall five of twelve both times. But scan time went from twelve seconds a target to seven.

@words text="Not yet a result" sub="but the row that says where to measure next"
On the one number view, nothing happened. On the profile, this reader finds a fact in a little over half the time. One reading each, so not yet a result, but the row that says where to measure next.

@chapter Make a row say something
@art method-kit
To make a row say something, measure it the way the effective rate was measured: three readings to give a range, then tests. A skim or a scan on a fresh text every week or two, logged. Then take the battery again in a month or so.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="One number sums up a reader@The first|A before and an after is a change@The second|The preset's tile is the Subject's rule@And the third"
The first slip is believing one number sums up a reader. The second is reading a before and an after as a change. And the third is taking the preset's verdict tile as the Subject's rule. The rule is the median of the last three tests.

@chapter Try it
@words text="Range 238 to 251 · tests 250, 262, 247 · scan 12 s → 7 s" sub="which has changed?"
Your turn. A baseline range of two thirty eight to two fifty one. Latest tests of two fifty, two sixty two and two forty seven. Scan time from twelve seconds a target to seven. Which has changed by the rule? Neither. The median, two fifty, is inside, and scan time is one reading against one.

@end text="A method, not a speed"
What the Subject leaves you with is not a speed but a method: a log, a rule for reading it, a routine, and four measures. Take your battery on Learn. That's learn dot get er done dot I O.
