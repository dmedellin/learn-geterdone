@chapter The question
@title text="Reading Your Curve" sub="Reading Faster · Your Training Plan" art=thumb
A log of readings draws a curve, and a curve invites you to look at its highest point. Has your reading changed? The Subject's rule looks somewhere else.

@art peak-pick
Among a dozen readings, the highest is high partly because it was picked for being high. Some of its height is that day's luck, and the next reading will most likely fall back toward the rest.

@chapter The rule
@art baseline-band
Your baselines give a range: how far apart your readings fall with nothing changed. A single test above the top of that range happens about one time in four by chance. That's because a fourth reading from the same reader is the highest of the four about one time in four.

@art median-of-three
So a change is evidence when the median of the last three tests is above the range. The median of three is the middle one, so one unusually good or bad day can't move it past the other two. A change that lifts it has shown up on at least two days.

@chapter A real gain
@math heading="Real-gain log (effective wpm)" items="baselines: 238, 245, 251 → range 238 to 251@two thirty eight;;tests: 262, 270, 281 → median 270@two sixty two;;270 − 251 = 19 above@nineteen above"
Take the real gain log. Its baselines are two thirty eight, two forty five and two fifty one. Its tests are two sixty two, two seventy and two eighty one. The verdict doesn't rest on the two eighty one. The median is two seventy, nineteen above the top of the range. That's evidence of a change.

@chapter Noise
@math heading="Noise log" items="tests: 244, 262, 239@two forty four;;262: 11 above, once@eleven above;;median 244: inside the range@inside the range"
Now the noise log: the same baselines, and tests of two forty four, two sixty two and two thirty nine. The two sixty two is the best session, eleven above the range. A reader who stopped there would announce a new level, but the median is two forty four, inside the range. The two sixty two is the one in four day.

@chapter Rate only
@math heading="Rate-only log" items="354 × 4/6 = 236@three fifty four;;360 × 4/6 = 240@three sixty;;357 × 4/6 = 238 → median 238, inside@three fifty seven"
Then the rate only log. Its tests were read at three fifty four, three sixty and three fifty seven words a minute, with four questions right each time instead of five or six. The effective rates are two thirty six, two forty and two thirty eight. The median is two thirty eight, the bottom of the range. Inside.

@art rate-vs-effective
The rate rose by sixty words a minute or more. A curve of rate would have called this a gain. It's a reader turning pages faster and understanding less, which is why every figure on this curve is an effective rate.

@chapter What counts
@art practice-dashed
Which entries does the verdict leave out? Practice at the pacer, because its rate was set for you. In the real gain log, practice readings at three thirty to three sixty are faster than every test, so the lab draws them lighter and leaves them out.

@list heading="Left out of the verdict" items="Practice at the pacer@Practice|Conditions of compare drills@conditions|Skims, scans and recall@skims|Readings without questions@without questions"
Practice, then. The conditions of the compare drills too, because each was a technique on trial. Skims, scans and recall, because they measure something else. And any reading without questions, because it has no effective rate. What's left is short: about one test a week.

@art harder-passages
One thing the rule can't see is the passage. A test on a harder passage reads lower for that reason alone, and the practice pool grows harder toward its end. If your tests fall just as the pool gets hard, read the fall as the passages, until tests on standard ones say otherwise.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="My best session is my new level@The first|Counting practice readings@The second|Reading a curve of rate@And the third"
The first slip is believing your best session is your new level. The second is counting practice readings in the curve, which shows how fast the pacer was set. And the third is reading a curve of rate, which can't tell a faster reader from a faster page turner.

@chapter Try it
@words text="Baselines 240, 246, 252 · tests 250, 249, 258" sub="the verdict?"
Your turn. Baselines of two forty, two forty six and two fifty two. Then tests of two fifty, two forty nine, and two fifty eight, the latest. What's the verdict? The median is two fifty, inside the range. The two fifty eight is above it once. No evidence of a change. Write the verdict in your log, with the date.
