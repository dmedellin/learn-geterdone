title: Why a witness and a philosopher know different things

@art two-shelves
A witness and a philosopher, Hobbes says, need two different kinds of knowledge.

@list items="Witness: fact, from sense and memory@witness|Philosopher: consequences, if this then that@philosopher"
The witness needs knowledge of fact. She saw it or she didn't, and that's absolute. The philosopher needs science, which is conditional: if the figure is a circle, any line through the centre halves it.

@words text="History records · philosophy demonstrates"
So history, however learned, isn't a lesser science. It's another kind of knowing.
