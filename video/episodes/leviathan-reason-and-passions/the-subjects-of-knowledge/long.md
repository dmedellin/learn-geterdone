@chapter The witness and the judge
@title text="The Several Subjects of Knowledge" sub="Leviathan, Chapter 9" art=thumb
Chapter nine is one page of text and a table of every science. It starts in a courtroom.

@art courtroom
Think of a trial. The witness is asked what she saw, and nothing else. She saw it, or she didn't. The judge, if he's reasoning, is asked something different: what follows from what.

@quote text="There are of Knowledge two kinds; whereof one is Knowledge of Fact; the other Knowledge of the Consequence of one Affirmation to another." cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 9"
Hobbes turns that courtroom difference into the whole of chapter nine. There are of knowledge two kinds: one is knowledge of fact, the other knowledge of the consequence of one affirmation to another.

@list heading="Two kinds of knowledge" items="Fact: sense and memory, absolute, for a witness@a witness|Science: consequences, conditional, for a philosopher@a philosopher"
Knowledge of fact is sense and memory. It's absolute, and it's what's required in a witness. Science is conditional: if the figure is a circle, any line through the centre halves it. That's what's required in a philosopher.

@chapter Two kinds of book
@art two-shelves
Each kind has its sort of book. The register of fact is history. Natural history records effects of nature that don't depend on man's will: metals, plants, animals. Civil history records the voluntary actions of men in commonwealths. The registers of science are books of philosophy, which demonstrate what follows.

@words text="Not a lesser version: another kind of knowing"
So a book of history, however learned, records what was seen. A book of philosophy demonstrates consequences. One isn't a lesser version of the other. It's another kind of knowing.

@chapter The table
@art branching-table
Then the table, printed on its side with brackets running left to right. At the root stands science, also called philosophy. It splits into consequences from the accidents of natural bodies, natural philosophy, and from the accidents of politic bodies, politics and civil philosophy.

@list heading="Natural philosophy" items="Quantity and motion: geometry, arithmetic, astronomy, navigation@quantity and motion|Qualities, physics: optics, music@qualities|Passions and speech of men: ethics, poetry, rhetoric, logic, just and unjust@the passions"
Natural philosophy splits again. Under quantity and motion: geometry, arithmetic, astronomy, geography, navigation. Under qualities, physics: optics, music, meteorology. And under the passions and speech of men: ethics, poetry, rhetoric, logic, and the science of just and unjust.

@art two-branches
Civil philosophy splits in two. The consequences of founding a commonwealth for the rights and duties of the sovereign, and for the duty and right of subjects.

@chapter Nested definitions
@math heading="The table preset" items="geometry is mathematics: true@geometry is mathematics;;geometry is natural philosophy: true@natural philosophy;;ethics is physics: true@ethics is physics;;politics is civil philosophy: true@politics is civil;;ethics is civil philosophy: absurd@absurd"
The lab writes part of the table as nested definitions. Each science is the sum of the branches above it. So geometry is mathematics, and geometry is natural philosophy. Ethics is physics. Politics is civil philosophy. But ethics is civil philosophy is absurd. It would put one science under natural and politic bodies at once.

@art schools-division
That's where Hobbes differs from what he was taught. The schools split philosophy into theoretical and practical, with ethics and politics together as practical. Hobbes hangs ethics from the passions of men, like optics from qualities. A defender of the old division would say ethics has its own subject, the ends of human action.

@chapter Misplaced sciences
@math heading="The misplaced preset" items="geometry is cosmography: false@geometry is cosmography;;arithmetic is geometry: false, no figure@figure;;navigation is mathematics: false@navigation;;politics is natural philosophy: absurd@absurd"
Now put sciences in the wrong place. Geometry is cosmography: false. Arithmetic is geometry: false, the figure is missing. Navigation is mathematics: false. But politics is natural philosophy is absurd, a politic science under natural bodies. Placing a science is a claim about what it follows from, and it can fail by missing a part or by clashing.

@words text="The lab's sorting, not Hobbes's"
One caution. True, false, absurd and insignificant are the lab's own sorting. Hobbes counts a false general affirmation as absurd too.

@chapter Can politics be a science?
@art circle-and-city
The table doesn't rank the sciences by worth. And putting politics under civil philosophy doesn't claim it's already been demonstrated. That's the work of the book. The old objection is that men in societies don't hold still like a circle. Hobbes's answer is that the commonwealth is an artificial man, made by human art.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Counting history as a science@The first|Assuming ethics stands with politics@The second|Reading the table as a ranking@And the third"
The first slip is counting history as a science. It's knowledge of fact. The second is assuming ethics must stand with politics. In this table, it doesn't. And the third is reading the table as a ranking, when it only says what each science follows from.
