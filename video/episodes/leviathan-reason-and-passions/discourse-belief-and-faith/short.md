title: Believing someone means holding two opinions

@art livy-cow
When you believe a historian, Hobbes says you're really trusting a man.

@quote text="So that in Beleefe are two opinions; one of the saying of the man; the other of his vertue." cite="Hobbes, Leviathan, Chapter 7"
In belief are two opinions: one of the saying of the man, the other of his virtue.

@words text="Not God, but Livy"
So if you don't believe Livy's story of a talking cow, you don't distrust God. You distrust Livy. Drop the second opinion, and the conclusion no longer follows.
