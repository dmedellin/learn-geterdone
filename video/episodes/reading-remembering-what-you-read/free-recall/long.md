@chapter The question
@title text="Free Recall" sub="Reading Faster · Remembering What You Read" art=thumb
You finish a passage. You answer five of six questions right. But close the page and try to write it all down, and how much comes back? That's free recall, and it asks more of memory than picking an answer from four.

@chapter The drill
@art camel
The drill times one reading of How a Camel Saves Water, a passage of three hundred and eighty three words, at your usual pace. Don't slow down to memorise. The point is what an ordinary reading keeps.

@art hidden-page
When you press Done, the passage disappears and a box opens. Write down everything you remember, in any order, in notes or in sentences, until nothing more comes.

@art idea-units
Then the passage's twelve idea units appear under what you wrote. Each is one thing the passage said, like the hump stores fat, not water. Tick each one your writing contains, whatever words you used.

@chapter Tick strictly
@words text="“The hump is fat” ✓ · “something about the hump” ✗"
Tick a unit when your writing has its substance. The hump is fat earns the first unit. Something about the hump does not.

@art tick-strict
And a unit you could have written but didn't stays unticked. The drill measures what came back without help, and seeing the list is help. The kit never reads your writing, so the count is exactly as honest as you are.

@chapter The numbers
@math heading="Typical preset" items="383 × 60 / 95.75 = 240 wpm@three eighty three;;recall = 7/12 ≈ 58.3%@seven of the twelve;;recalled rate = 240 × 7/12 = 140@one forty"
The typical reader took ninety five point seven five seconds over three eighty three words, two forty words a minute, and ticked seven of the twelve units. That's a recall share of about fifty eight point three percent, and a recalled rate of two forty times seven twelfths: one forty words a minute.

@math heading="Strong preset" items="10/12 = 5/6 ≈ 83.3%@ten of the twelve;;240 × 10/12 = 200@two hundred"
The strong preset is the same reading with ten of the twelve ticked: five sixths, about eighty three point three percent, and a recalled rate of two hundred. The recalled rate is the part of your reading you could give back.

@chapter Recall versus recognition
@words text="7 of 12 units · 5 of 6 questions"
After your ticks, the drill asks the passage's six multiple choice questions. Expect recall to be the lower of the two. The typical preset pairs seven of twelve units with five of six questions. That gap isn't a contradiction.

@art recall-vs-recognition
A multiple choice question puts the right answer on the screen, and it only has to look familiar. Free recall gives you nothing to look at, so a fact you'd know if you saw it doesn't count. And recall asks for the passage's own ideas, not four options a question setter chose.

@words text="Recall is the harsher number" sub="Roediger and Karpicke (2006) measured by free recall"
Roediger and Karpicke, in two thousand six, measured what students kept from prose by free recall, the method this drill uses. Recall is the harsher number, and the more useful one for a reading you need later: what you can tell someone, or act on, when the page is closed.

@chapter Compare with yourself
@art compare-yourself
There's no published norm for these twelve units, and they aren't equal in weight. The hump storing fat is the passage's opening claim. The eyelashes are one item in a list. So compare with your own recall on the next passage, scored by the same rules.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Recognising is remembering@The first|Ticking what the list reminds you of@The second|Slowing down to memorise@And the third"
The first slip is thinking that recognising the right answer is remembering it. The second is ticking what the list reminds you of, which turns the drill back into multiple choice. And the third is slowing down to memorise. The recalled rate already charges a fast, forgetful reading for what it lost.

@chapter Try it
@words text="400 words · 120 s · 9 of 12 units" sub="recalled rate?"
Your turn. A reader takes a hundred and twenty seconds over four hundred words, and ticks nine of twelve idea units. What's the recalled rate? The rate is two hundred words a minute, and two hundred times nine twelfths is one fifty.
