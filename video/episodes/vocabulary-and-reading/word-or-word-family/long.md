@chapter What is a word?
@title text="Word or Word Family" sub="Vocabulary and Reading" art=thumb
The last two lessons counted words. This one asks what a word is. When someone says a reader needs to know ninety-eight percent of the words on a page, they've counted in a certain way. The lab counts in another way.

@art root-endings
The first way is a flemma. A flemma is a word and the forms made from it by the endings you met in the first courses: s, e-d, i-n-g, e-r and e-s-t, plus the forms that don't follow the rules. Walk, walks, walked and walking are one flemma. So are good, better and best.

@art root-family
A family is bigger. It's a flemma and the words made from it with a beginning, or with a different kind of ending. Happy, unhappy and happiness are one family.

@words text="the list: flemmas  ·  Nation's 98%: families" sub="two different things to count"
And here's the point. The word list counts flemmas. Nation's ninety-eight percent counts families. So the two figures aren't counting the same thing.

@chapter Three steps
@art three-gates
The lab reads a page in three steps. First, is the word on the list, by the rules? Then it's covered. Second, if not, is it a name? Third, if not, it takes off one beginning or one ending, and asks again. A word that passes the third step is counted as a family word.

@math heading="The passage, step by step" items="the list  ·  91.0%@for the list;;and names  ·  94.3%  ·  +3.3@with names;;and families  ·  95.6%  ·  +1.3@with families"
So the three numbers are in order, each including the one before. On the passage, it's ninety-one percent for the list, ninety-four point three with names, and ninety-five point six with families. Names add three point three points. Families add one point three.

@words text="12 words in 936  =  1.3 points" sub="delightful · unwelcome · unwilling · recollect · overtaken"
Those one point three points are twelve words, each appearing once. Delightful. Unwelcome. Unwilling. Recollect. Overtaken. And twelve in nine hundred and thirty-six is one point three points.

@chapter The other texts
@math heading="What a family adds" items="the passage  ·  94.3%  →  95.6%@On the passage;;the play  ·  94.6%  →  95.4%@On the play;;the modern documents  ·  92.7%  →  93.7%@On the modern documents"
On the passage, as you saw, the step adds one point three. On the play, it adds point eight, from ninety-four point six to ninety-five point four. On the modern documents, it adds one point, from ninety-two point seven to ninety-three point seven. There, the first row is disability, dis plus ability, and it appears ten times.

@chapter What the step cannot see
@art cut-piece
The family step cuts a spelling and looks at what's left. For delightful, the cut is right: delight, plus f-u-l. A reader who knows delight has most of what delightful says.

@art false-match
But in the play, resides is cut into r-e and sides, and endure into end and u-r-e. Those rows are wrong. To reside has nothing to do with sides, and to endure has nothing to do with an end. The lab passes both, because it reads spellings, so you have to read the rows yourself.

@chapter Why the gap is small here
@art small-step
The design notes behind this course put the gap between counting flemmas and counting families at three to six points on general text. That's an estimate, not a count you can see. The lab shows between point eight and one point three on these texts, partly because it uses only a short list of beginnings and endings.

@words text="95.6%  <  98%" sub="below 98% on every text"
Either way you count, the answer for these texts is the same: below ninety-eight percent. The words that stand between you and an easy read are the ones still in the table of words not covered. On the passage, there are forty-one.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="holding a share against 98% without asking what it counts@The first|thinking a family is any word inside another@The second|thinking the family step finds words to learn@And the third"
The first slip is holding the lab's share against ninety-eight percent without asking what each one counts. The second is thinking a family is any word that contains another. And the third is thinking the family step finds the words you need to learn. It finds the ones you may not need to learn.

@chapter Try it
@words text="delight, delightful · walk, walked · kind, unkind · care, careless" sub="which pair is one flemma?"
Your turn. Which pair is one flemma: delight and delightful, walk and walked, kind and unkind, or care and careless? Pause and choose. Walk and walked. Walked is just walk plus e-d. The other three pairs are one family, but two flemmas.
