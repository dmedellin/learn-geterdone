@chapter Have we found others?
@title text="The Truth of Solipsism" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part Two, chapter 4" art=thumb
The last lesson ended with a common world, shared in behaviour and in speech. Then the chapter doubts its own result. Has it found other people at all?

@words text="his behaviour, and even his words, are not the other"
I do perceive the other's grief or anger in his conduct, his face and his hands, without any inner experience of my own to compare. But his behaviour, and even his words, are not the other.

@chapter Paul's grief
@art paul-grief
Paul suffers because he's lost his wife. I suffer because Paul is grieved. Our situations can't be laid one on the other. For him these situations are lived through. For me they are displayed. Even a project we share isn't one project, because Paul is Paul and I'm myself.

@art unequal-love
Reciprocity isn't guaranteed either. In a couple where one loves more, one throws his whole life into the love while the other stays free.

@words text="There is here a solipsism rooted in living experience and quite insurmountable."
So the chapter's strongest sentence. There is here a solipsism rooted in living experience, and quite insurmountable. I'm the one by whom every experience is lived.

@chapter Two moments of one phenomenon
@words text="Solitude and communication … two “moments” of one phenomenon"
Can I escape as an impartial spectator, or in God? The subjectivity found here isn't God's. And yet other people do exist for me. If my experience didn't present them, I couldn't even call them inaccessible. Solitude and communication are two moments of one phenomenon.

@quote text="Contrary to the social world I can always use my nature as a sensory being, close my eyes, stop up my ears, live as a stranger in society, treat others, ceremonies and institutions as mere arrangements of colour and light, and strip them of all their human significance. … The truth of solipsism is there." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part Two, chapter 4, p. 419"
Now the passage. Against the social world I can always close my eyes, stop up my ears, and treat ceremonies as mere arrangements of colour and light. The truth of solipsism is there.

@art fly-into-being
But I can fly from being only into being: from society into nature, from the real world into one made of its fragments. Even a solipsist philosophy is written for a community of speakers.

@chapter The objectifying look
@art stranger-gaze
Then a view the chapter says is asserted, widely read as Sartre's. The other's gaze turns me into an object, and mine him. The reply: only if both of us withdraw into the core of our thinking nature. A dog's gaze embarrasses no one. And let the stranger say one word, and he already ceases to transcend me.

@chapter The lab's table
@list heading="Four cases" items="The baby opens its mouth: n, y@the baby|Paul's grief: y, y@grief|The dialogue: n, n@the dialogue|The stranger's silent gaze: −, y@stranger's"
The lab codes four cases, analogy first, then the look. The baby who opens its mouth: analogy fails, the look absorbs. Paul's grief: both absorb. The dialogue: both fail. The stranger's silent gaze: analogy is silent, the look absorbs.

@math heading="All preset" items="analogy: 1 absorbed, 2 failed, 1 silent@analogy absorbs;;look: 3 absorbed, 1 failed@the look;;survivors: none · split: the baby@split"
So analogy absorbs one, fails two, and is silent on one. The look absorbs three and fails one. Survivors: none. The split is the baby alone, since a silence isn't a failure.

@art woven-dialogue
Both absorb the grief. That's the table's truth in solipsism. Both fail the dialogue, where neither of us is an object for the other. That's the coexistence lived beneath it, stated in prose, not scored.

@chapter A revealed subjectivity
@words text="Transcendental subjectivity is a revealed subjectivity … and is for that reason an intersubjectivity."
To exist is to be in and of the world. Even the philosopher's meditation is an act, a spoken word, and so a dialogue. Transcendental subjectivity is a revealed subjectivity, revealed to itself and to others, and so an intersubjectivity.

@art waterloo-fringe
The social is a permanent field of existence. Stendhal's Fabrice wanted to see Waterloo like a landscape and found only confused episodes. The true Waterloo comes about on the fringes of all the perspectives.

@chapter Mistakes
@list heading="Don't say" items="Merleau-Ponty refutes solipsism@refutes|The objectifying look is the chapter's view@the chapter's view|Lived solipsism means only I exist@only I exist"
Don't say Merleau-Ponty refutes solipsism; he grants its truth. Don't take the objectifying look for the chapter's view. And don't read lived solipsism as the theory that only I exist.
