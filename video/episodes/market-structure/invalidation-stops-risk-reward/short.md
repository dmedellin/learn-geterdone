@art stop-structural
Your stop isn't where the loss hurts. It's where the idea is wrong.

@math items="0.50% × $25,000 = $125@half a percent;;105.00 − 97.80 = 7.20@ninety seven eighty;;125 ÷ 7.20 → 17 units@round down"
Risk half a percent of twenty five thousand: one hundred twenty five dollars. Entry one hundred five, stop ninety seven eighty, seven twenty per unit. Divide, and round down to seventeen units.

@words text="Wider stop → fewer units"
A wider structural stop just means fewer units. You never squeeze the stop to buy more.
