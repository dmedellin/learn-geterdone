@chapter Where courses start
@title text="Be Is Mostly a Main Verb" sub="Helping Verbs" art=thumb
Most courses meet the verb be in sentences like she is walking, or they were singing. Be and an ing form make an action that's still going on.

@art expect-vs-find
It's a simple rule, and it's true. But on a real page, it's the rare use. Let's count.

@math heading="Pride and Prejudice, Chapter XXVI" items="137 forms of be@one hundred and thirty-seven;;6 followed by an -ing word  ·  4.4%@six"
One chapter of Pride and Prejudice has one hundred and thirty-seven forms of be. A scan reads the word after each one, past not and short adverbs. It finds just six followed by an ing word. That's four point four percent.

@chapter What the other 131 are
@math heading="After be, in the chapter" items="noun phrase  ·  37@thirty-seven;;preposition or place  ·  26@twenty-six;;adjective  ·  19@nineteen;;participle  ·  13@thirteen;;pronoun next  ·  3  ·  something else  ·  33@three put"
Thirty-seven are followed by a noun phrase. Twenty-six by a preposition or a place. Nineteen by an adjective the scan knows. Thirteen by a participle. Three put a pronoun next, and thirty-three are filed as something else.

@words text="her brother is the cause" sub="be links the subject to what comes after"
After a noun phrase, an adjective or a place, be is the verb. Its job is to link the subject to what comes after. Her brother is the cause. There's no other verb for it to help.

@art link-bridge
Think of this be as a bridge. On one side is the subject. On the other is what the subject is, what it's like, or where it is. Her brother is the cause. I am not in love.

@chapter Five jobs
@math heading="Five rows, five jobs" items="noun phrase  ·  her brother is the cause@her brother;;participle  ·  you are warned against it@you are warned;;adjective  ·  I am not afraid of@I am not afraid;;place  ·  I am not in love@in love;;-ing form  ·  Caroline and Mrs Hurst were going out@were going out"
Here's one row for each job. A noun phrase: her brother is the cause. A participle: you are warned against it. An adjective: I am not afraid of. A place: I am not in love. And an ing form: Caroline and Mrs Hurst were going out. Only that last one is a progressive.

@art five-jobs
In the chapter, the progressive turns up six times in one hundred and thirty-seven. The noun phrase is the largest group, and the ing form is the smallest named one.

@chapter Never a bare verb
@words text="I am agree ✗  ·  she is work here ✗" sub="after be: -ing, a participle, or no verb"
And here's something useful. Not one of the one hundred and thirty-seven is followed by a bare verb. I am agree and she is work here are mistakes the chapter never makes. After be comes an ing form, a participle, or no verb at all.

@chapter The participle puzzle
@math heading="Same word, same place" items="you are warned against it  ·  what is done to you@what is done;;she was satisfied with  ·  how she felt@how she felt"
The thirteen participle rows are a mix. You are warned against it says what is done to you. She was satisfied with says how she felt. A passive and an adjective use the same kind of word in the same place.

@art same-shape-two
A scan that reads only the word can't tell them apart, so they're kept in one class. Only the meaning tells which is which.

@chapter What the scan lost
@art lost-rows
In the rows filed as something else, you'll find I am sure and I am certain, adjectives the scan doesn't know. And one of them is a progressive: his marriage was now fast approaching. The scan stepped over now, met fast, and stopped.

@math heading="By hand" items="7 of 137  ·  5.1%@seven;;whole novel, quoted: 4.7%@four point seven"
So by hand, seven of the one hundred and thirty-seven forms are followed by an ing verb, five point one percent. Over the whole novel, counted once, it's four point seven percent of five thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight forms. In two modern documents, five point six. The picture is the same in eighteen thirteen and today.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Thinking be is mostly the helper of -ing@The first|Calling every participle after be a passive@The second|Thinking a rare form isn't worth learning@the third"
The first slip is thinking be is mainly the helper of the ing form. The second is calling every participle after be a passive. She was pleased may be how she felt. And the third is reading a low share as a form not worth learning. A sentence that needs the progressive can't do without it.

@chapter Try it
@art pick-was
Your turn. In which sentence is was the main verb? She was going home. The letter was sent. He was waiting for you. My visit was not long. Long is an adjective, so in the last one, was is the verb.
