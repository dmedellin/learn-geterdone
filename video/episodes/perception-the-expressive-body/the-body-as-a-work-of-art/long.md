@chapter Of space, not in it
@title text="The Body as a Work of Art" sub="Phenomenology of Perception · Part One, Chapter 4" art=thumb
What kind of unity does your body have? Chapter four answers with a comparison, and it's not the one you'd expect.

@words text="our body is not primarily in space: it is of it"
The Cartesian tradition made an object's spatial limits its essence: to exist is to exist part outside part. One's own body teaches the reverse. Our body is not primarily in space: it is of it. The phantom arm is felt where no arm is, so a limb's felt presence isn't settled by its objective place.

@chapter Reaching for the telephone
@art telephone-ways
You're sitting at a table and want the telephone. Your hand moves, your upper body straightens, your leg muscles tauten, and the work is shared out without your arranging it. You can stretch the arm further, lean forward, or half stand up.

@math heading="Telephone preset" items="three ways, each a way of reaching the telephone@three ways;;core: hand-at-the-telephone@the core;;varies: every posture of arm, trunk and legs@varies"
The lab codes the three ways. The core, what every way shares, is hand at the telephone. What varies is every posture of arm, trunk and legs. What they share is the result, not a part of the body. All these movements are available in virtue of their common meaning.

@chapter Not a law, either
@art cube-law
So maybe the body is perceived through a law of construction, the way you know every face of a cube from its geometry. That's the intellectualist's answer, and it may work for an external object, because the object stands in front of me.

@words text="I am not in front of my body, I am in it, or rather I am it."
The body doesn't. I am not in front of my body, I am in it, or rather I am it. Its variations and their constant can't be set before me. And notice: people don't recognise their own hand in a photo, but know their own walk on film at once.

@chapter Like a work of art
@quote text="The body is to be compared, not to a physical object, but rather to a work of art." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part One, Chapter 4, pp. 174–175"
Here's the comparison.

@art cezanne-patches
In a picture, the idea can't be conveyed except by showing the colours. Read an analysis of Cezanne without seeing the pictures, and you're left choosing among several possible Cezannes. Change a novel's order, and you change its meaning. The expression is indistinguishable from the thing expressed.

@quote text="It is a nexus of living meanings, not the law for a certain number of covariant terms." cite="Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Part One, Chapter 4, pp. 174–175"
A touch in the upper arm signifies a touch in the forearm and shoulder and a certain look of the arm, because they all take part in one action, not because they're facets of one intelligible arm.

@chapter One signature, four ways
@art same-scribble
The lesson adds its own example. Sign your name small on paper, large on a blackboard, with your left hand, or with a foot in sand.

@math heading="Signature preset" items="limbs: fingers, arm, left hand, leg → all vary@limbs;;core: letter shapes, stroke order, rhythm@core;;muscles only: core none@by their limbs;;a tracing: lacks rhythm@tracing"
The limbs are fingers, arm, left hand and leg, so every limb varies. The core is the letter shapes, the stroke order and the rhythm. Code the four by their limbs alone, and the core is none. And a slow tracing by someone else keeps every shape, and lacks the rhythm.

@words text="An invariant is almost a law"
But notice what the lab did. An invariant of variations is very nearly the law for covariant terms, the thing the body isn't. It's a list drawn up from outside. What it shows exactly is negative: whatever holds the four together isn't a part.

@chapter The stick and the gaze
@art cane-instrument
Every habit is both motor and perceptual. It's tempting to think the blind man reads pressures on his hand as signs of the stick, and the stick as a sign of the kerb. But habit relieves him of that. The stick is no longer something he perceives, but an instrument with which he perceives.

@art colour-swatches
Koffka reported that a child who learns to tell blue from red finds other colours easier too. Not by grasping a category. The gaze is a natural instrument, like the stick. Learning to see colours is acquiring a style of seeing, a new use of one's own body.

@chapter Mistakes and practice
@list heading="Don't say" items="The body is one like a machine@machine|The lab's core is the body's unity@core|The comparison means the body is beautiful@beautiful"
Don't say the body is one like a machine, or like any object under a law. Don't take the lab's core for its unity. And don't hear the comparison as saying the body is beautiful or designed. Your turn: open a door with your hand, your elbow, your hip. What's the core?
