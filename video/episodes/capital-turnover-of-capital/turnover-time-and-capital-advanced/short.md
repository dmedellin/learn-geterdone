title: Why nine hundred pounds of capital isn't enough

@art idle-weeks
Nine weeks to make it, three to sell it. The factory stops for three.

@math items="capital I = 9 · £100 = £900@nine hundred;;capital II = 3 · £100 = £300@three hundred more"
At a hundred a week, nine weeks of work need nine hundred. But to keep working while that sells, you need three hundred more.

@words text="Twelve hundred, not nine hundred"
Marx's point: a continuous business always has part of its capital in circulation, not at work.
