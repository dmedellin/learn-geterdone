@art payroll-office
A cashier adds no value to anything. So where does his profit come from?

@math items="180 / 1,125 = 16%@sixteen percent;;cashier: 16% × 125 = 20@twenty"
In the lesson's illustration, a cashier's hundred and twenty five joins Marx's capitals. The same one hundred and eighty of surplus value is spread over all of it: sixteen percent. The cashier draws twenty.

@words text="Manufacturers 162 → 144 · merchant 18 → 16"
That twenty is exactly what the manufacturers and the merchant give up. On Marx's account, it's a deduction from surplus value.
