title: Why a quick merchant needs less money

@art four-mills
Three thousand pounds can move twelve thousand pounds of linen a year. How?

@math items="£3,000 × 4 = £12,000@times four;;£12,000 / 4 = £3,000@twelve thousand over four"
Marx's merchant has three thousand pounds. If he turns it over four times a year, times four makes twelve thousand pounds of linen. Put the other way, twelve thousand over four is all the capital he needs.

@words text="The quicker the turnover, the smaller the capital"
So the quicker the turnover, the smaller the capital tied up in selling. And on Marx's account, the selling itself creates no value.
