@art two-prices
For most of history, a merchant's profit had no average to obey.

@words text="Buy cheap, sell dear"
The old merchant moved between communities whose prices had nothing to do with each other. He alone saw both, so he bought where goods were cheap and sold where they were dear.

@math items="old trader: buy £60, sell £100 → £40@forty pounds;;capitalist merchant: £18 on £100@eighteen"
In the lesson's illustration, that's forty pounds on sixty. The capitalist merchant, held to the general rate, makes eighteen on a hundred.
