@chapter The principle
@title text="Frankfurt Cases and the Ability to Do Otherwise" sub="Identity, Modality and Freedom" art=thumb
If you couldn't have done otherwise, then what you did wasn't up to you. And it's hard to blame someone for what wasn't up to them. That's the principle of alternate possibilities, and it has a long history of seeming obvious.

@words text="PAP" sub="responsible only if you could have done otherwise"
Call it PAP. A person is morally responsible for an act only if they could have done otherwise. Harry Frankfurt built a case in which the first condition is false, and the verdict about blame doesn't seem to follow.

@chapter Jones and Black
@art jones-and-black
Jones is deciding whether to shoot someone. Black wants him to, and has arranged things so that if Jones shows any sign of deciding not to, Black will bring about the decision and the act by a device. Jones shows no such sign. He decides for his own reasons, and shoots. Black does nothing.

@math heading="Three variables" items="D = 1:  Jones decides@D says;;B = ¬D:  Black acts only if Jones doesn't@B says;;A = D ∨ B:  the shooting occurs either way@And A says"
Build the model. D says Jones decides to shoot, and it's true. B says Black acts, and Black acts exactly when Jones doesn't decide. And A says the shooting occurs, if either Jones decides or Black acts.

@chapter The but-for test
@art backup-device
At the actual values, D is one, B is zero, and A is one. Now flip D to zero. Then B becomes one, because Black acts, and A is still one. Flipping Jones's decision alone leaves the act in place. So the decision isn't a but for cause. That's the formal content of, Jones could not have done otherwise.

@chapter Holding Black fixed
@math heading="Hold B at its actual value" items="hold B = 0@Hold B at zero;;flip D to 0:  A = 0 ∨ 0 = 0@flip D;;D is a cause, holding B fixed@a cause holding"
Now hold Black's inaction fixed. Hold B at zero, and flip D to zero. A is zero or zero, which is zero. The act doesn't occur. With Black's inaction fixed, the act depends on Jones's decision. So the lab reports D as a cause holding B fixed.

@art spare-tyre
This is the structure of preemption. Black is a backup that was never used, and a backup never used doesn't take the credit from the decision that did the work. A spare tyre in the boot doesn't take over from the tyre that carried the car.

@art actual-path
Frankfurt's conclusion is that Jones is responsible, though he couldn't have done otherwise. The act issued from his own decision, in the actual sequence of events, and Black played no part. If so, PAP is false. What grounded responsibility was the act's actual source.

@chapter Has Black been described too kindly?
@art sign-reader
The case has been pressed hard. One reply says Black must read some sign to know whether to act, and Jones could have shown it or not. That small alternative might be enough. Another says if the sign reliably predicts a decision Jones hasn't made yet, determinism has been assumed about Jones's choice. The equation B equals not D gives Black a perfect reader, which is exactly what's disputed.

@words text="Semi-compatibilism" sub="no ability to do otherwise, still responsible"
And notice what the case doesn't do. It doesn't make anyone free, since it grants that Jones can't do otherwise. You can hold that determinism removes the ability to do otherwise, and still hold that responsibility survives. That's called semi compatibilism.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Thinking responsibility requires doing otherwise@The first|Thinking Black makes Jones's decision irrelevant@The second|Treating the case as a proof of freedom@And the third"
The first slip is thinking responsibility requires that you could have done otherwise. If the case is fair, it has a counterexample. The second is thinking Black's presence makes Jones's decision irrelevant. Black was a backup, never used. And the third is treating the case as a proof that people are free. It argues against one premise only.

@chapter Try it
@art two-tests
Try the lab's third model. Jones doesn't decide, and Black acts. Make Black the cause to test, flip it, then hold the decision fixed and flip it again. Say what Black is in that case, and why. Then ask whether the model is fair: what does Black read, and does that reading assume Jones's choice is settled in advance?
