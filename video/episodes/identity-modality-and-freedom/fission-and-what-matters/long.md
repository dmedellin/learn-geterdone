@chapter The case
@title text="Fission and What Matters" sub="Identity, Modality and Freedom" art=thumb
Here's a thought experiment, stated so nothing in it is impossible by logic alone. Each half of a person's brain can sustain a mind. The two halves are removed, and placed in two new bodies.

@art split-brain
Each of the two people who wake is psychologically continuous with the original, in every way the last lesson asked for. Memories, intentions, character. Call them A and B. Which one is you?

@chapter Three claims
@list heading="Hard to deny together" items="A and B are each continuous with me@how the case was built|Continuity is enough for survival@enough for survival|Identity is one-one@one to one"
Three things are hard to deny together. A and B are each continuous with you, because that's how the case was built. Continuity is enough for survival. And identity is one to one. If A is you and B is you, then A is B. But they lead two lives, in two bodies.

@math heading="Five sentences" items="1.  ca@One;;2.  cb@Two;;3.  ca → ia@Three;;4.  cb → ib@Four;;5.  ¬(ia ∧ ib)@Five"
Written out, with the survival claim split over both survivors, that's five sentences. One, A is continuous with me. Two, B is. Three, if A is continuous, A is me. Four, if B is continuous, B is me. Five, A and B aren't both me.

@art five-locks
The lab finds no row that makes all five true. One and three give A is me. Two and four give B is me. Five denies both. And the smallest inconsistent subset is all five. Drop any one, and a model appears. So every sentence is a place where the set can be repaired. And each repair is a position someone has defended, with its own price.

@chapter The exits
@art branching-line
Dropping one or two denies the stipulation. It says only one survivor is truly continuous. That changes the case, instead of answering it.

@art closest-continuer
Dropping three or four is more interesting. A closest continuer theory says continuity gives identity only when no rival is as close. In a tie, neither survivor is you. The price is that your survival depends on whether someone else exists. If only one half had taken, you'd have survived. With both, you don't.

@art overlapping-people
Dropping five says both A and B are you. One version holds that there were always two people before the division, overlapping in one body. The cost is a count of persons nobody made before the case arose.

@chapter Parfit's exit
@math heading="Parfit: continuity gives what matters" items="ca → ma@A gets;;cb → mb@B gets;;¬(ia ∧ ib):  kept@Keep the one to one"
The exit that changes the question is Parfit's. Identity, he says, is the wrong thing to want. What matters in survival is continuity and connectedness, and both A and B have it in full. Replace three and four with claims that continuity gives what matters. A gets it, and B gets it. Keep the one to one claim, and the set has a model.

@art what-matters
Whether A is you is left without an answer that matters. It's the loss of that answer, not of a person, that Parfit asks you to accept. Whether he's right that nothing is lost is the philosophical question, and the lab leaves it open.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Believing one of the two must be me@The first|Hearing Parfit as saying fission is like death@The second|Taking an inconsistent set to prove the case impossible@And the third"
The first slip is believing one of the survivors must be you. The sentences don't say so, and the case is symmetric. The second is hearing Parfit as saying fission is as bad as death. On his view, it's close to ordinary survival. And the third is taking an inconsistent set to prove the case impossible. The set needs repair. The case is untouched.

@chapter Try it
@words text="drop 3:  ib forced,  so not ia" sub="drop 4:  ia forced,  so not ib"
Work through the drops yourself. Drop three, and B is forced to be you, so A isn't. Drop four, and it's the reverse. Notice that nothing prefers either. The case was built to be symmetric, so a view that wants a single survivor has to add a tie break the five sentences don't contain. Then, for each exit, name a case where its cost shows.
