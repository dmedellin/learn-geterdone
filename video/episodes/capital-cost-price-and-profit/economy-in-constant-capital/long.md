@chapter A saving in buildings
@title text="Economy in the Use of Constant Capital" sub="Capital, Volume III, Chapter 5" art=thumb
Hold the surplus value and the wages fixed. How can the rate of profit still rise? Only one way: spend less on constant capital.

@quote text="If the surplus-value is given, then the rate of profit can be increased only by a reduction of the value of the constant capital required for the production of commodities." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 5"
That's the rule, and it's the third case from the chapter on two factors, put to work.

@words text="Overcrowding = a saving in buildings" sub="in the language of the capitalist"
The phrase that carries the chapter is this. Marx lists the overcrowding of narrow, unsanitary rooms with labourers, or in the language of the capitalist, a saving in buildings. The same entry in the books can be a better engine, or a room too small to breathe in.

@chapter The arithmetic
@art shrinking-c
The lab cuts c and recomputes. On Marx's familiar four hundred c, a hundred v and a hundred s, the rate of profit is twenty per cent.

@math heading="400c + 100v + 100s" items="shared building, −£100:  100/400 = 25%@a hundred;;waste reused, −£40:  100/460 = 5/23@forty;;guards not bought, −£20:  100/480 = 5/24@twenty"
Cut c by a hundred, as when one engine and one roof serve more workers, and the rate rises to twenty five per cent. Cut it by forty, by reusing waste, and it's five twenty thirds. Cut it by twenty, the guard rails and ventilation not bought, and it's five twenty fourths.

@words text="c → 0 would make p′ = s′"
The profit is a hundred throughout. The rate rises because the same surplus is measured against a smaller capital. If c could fall to nothing, the rate of profit would equal the rate of surplus value. That's its upper limit.

@chapter Where savings come from
@art shared-engine
Where do the savings come from? First, the working day. Work eighteen hours instead of twelve, and one week is stretched into one and a half. Second, scale. One central engine costs less than many small ones, and a shared building costs less per worker.

@quote text="The capitalist thus appropriates the advantages of the entire system of the division of social labor." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 5"
Third, other industries. When cotton, iron or machines get cheaper elsewhere, the spinner's c falls, and his rate rises.

@art rags-to-cloth
Fourth, what used to be thrown away. Woollen rags worth about four pounds a ton became worth as much as forty four, because the shoddy trade learned to remake them into cloth. Fifth, inventions. Marx notes drily that the first firms to use them usually go bankrupt, and cheap successors take the benefit.

@chapter At the workers' expense
@art single-shaft
Then the evidence for savings at the workers' expense. Around eighteen sixty, fatal accidents in English collieries averaged fifteen men a week. Many mines had a single shaft, so no ventilation and no escape.

@art unguarded-shaft
Manufacturers resisted the law requiring guards on shafts. In eighteen fifty five their association raised more than fifty thousand pounds to fight the factory inspectors. A Glasgow firm guarded all its machinery with old iron for nine pounds one shilling. Its subscription to the association would have been eleven.

@quote text="The issue was to prove that killing is no murder when done for profit." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 5"
That's Marx's verdict on the association's purpose. Capitalist production, he says, however sparing with materials, is utterly prodigal with human life.

@chapter Same numbers
@art same-number
And here's the point of the sameness. Cut twenty from c with a better boiler, or by not buying guards, and the rate rises to exactly five twenty fourths either way. The rate of profit can't tell them apart, so the drive to raise it reaches for both.

@words text="Is risk priced in wages?" sub="Smith · compensating differentials"
A standing objection is older than the chapter. Adam Smith argued that wages vary with the hardship of a trade, so dangerous work earns a premium. The report Marx quotes has miners accepting danger for little above a day labourer's wage. Whether risk is priced is an empirical question the lab can't answer.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Economy in c is only good management@The first|A saving in c makes more surplus@The second|Every saving is the capitalist's own@And the third"
The first slip is treating economy in constant capital as only good management. The second is thinking a saving in c produces more surplus value. It doesn't; the rate rises on a smaller capital. And the third is crediting every economy to the individual capitalist, when most come from combined labour or other industries.
