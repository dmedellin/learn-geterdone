@chapter Costs plus a margin?
@title text="Cost Price and Profit" sub="Capital, Volume III, Chapter 1" art=thumb
Think about how a price is usually explained: the maker's costs, plus a margin on top. That's the view this chapter starts from, and takes apart.

@words text="Toward the surface of society"
Volume one went inside production. Volume two followed capital through circulation. Volume three, Marx says, moves step by step toward the forms capital takes on the surface of society, where capitals meet each other, and the ordinary consciousness of the people in them.

@chapter Cost price
@art value-bar
Marx's figures: a capital of five hundred pounds. Twenty is wear of the instruments, three hundred and eighty is materials, and a hundred buys labour-power, at a rate of surplus value of a hundred per cent.

@math heading="Marx's £600 commodity" items="value:  400c + 100v + 100s = £600@six hundred;;cost price:  k = c + v = £500@cost price"
The product is worth six hundred. Take away the hundred of surplus value and five hundred is left, which only replaces what was spent. That five hundred is the cost price, written k.

@quote text="The capitalist cost of the commodity is measured by the expenditure of capital, while the actual cost of the commodity is measured by the expenditure of labor." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 1"
So the two are vastly different amounts.

@art two-costs
The surplus value costs the capitalist nothing, because it costs the labourer unpaid labour.

@chapter What cost price hides
@words text="c: £400 → £600 · value £600 → £800"
Change the two parts of cost price in turn. Raise the means of production from four hundred to six hundred, and the cost price and the value both rise by two hundred, to seven hundred and eight hundred. Constant capital passes its own value into the product.

@art dearer-wages
Now raise wages from a hundred to a hundred and fifty. The cost price rises to five fifty, but the value stays at six hundred, with only fifty of surplus value. The labour adds new value whatever the wage. A dearer wage only changes how it's split.

@quote text="The distinction between constant and variable capital has disappeared." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 1"
In the cost price, that difference is invisible.

@chapter Profit on the whole capital
@art machine-and-wear
Suppose the instruments were worth twelve hundred. Only the twenty of wear enters the cost price, so the capital advanced is sixteen eighty. After the sale, the capitalist has eleven eighty of machinery and six hundred of product, a hundred more than he started with.

@words text="value = k + p"
Seen that way, the hundred is as much an increase of the whole sixteen eighty as of the five hundred used up. Surplus value, in its alleged capacity as offspring of the whole capital, takes the form of profit. The word alleged is doing work: on Marx's account only v produces it.

@chapter Below value, still a profit
@quote text="But the capitalist may sell a commodity at a profit even when selling it below its value." cite="Marx, Capital, Volume III, Chapter 1"
Sold at its value, the commodity realises the whole surplus value as profit. But there's room below.

@math heading="Cost price £500 · value £600" items="at £600:  profit £100@at its value;;at £560:  profit £60, £40 below value@five hundred and sixty;;at £500:  profit £0@at cost price;;at £480:  profit −£20@four hundred and eighty"
Sell at its value, and the profit is a hundred. Sell at five hundred and sixty, and the profit is sixty, though the sale is forty below value. Sell at cost price, and the profit is nothing. Sell at four hundred and eighty, and it's a loss of twenty.

@art price-range
Between cost price and value, there's practical playroom, and Marx says competition and underselling live in it.

@chapter Torrens and Proudhon
@art corn-in-corn-out
Robert Torrens put the opposite view. A farmer advances a hundred quarters of corn and gets a hundred and twenty back, so profit can't be part of his expenditure. It must come from buyers. Marx grants the first step, but replies that something can't come out of nothing. Torrens has only moved the creation out of production into exchange.

@words text="20 lb of yarn: 24s + 3s + 3s = 30s"
Proudhon took cost price for the real value. Marx's reply: twenty pounds of yarn worth thirty shillings contain twenty four of means of production, three of wages and three of surplus value. Sell at cost, twenty seven shillings, and the buyer gets two pounds of yarn for nothing.

@chapter Where people slip
@list heading="Common slips" items="Above cost means above value@The first|Cost price is the real value@The second|The whole capital is in the cost price@And the third"
The first slip is thinking a sale above cost price is a sale above value. The second is treating the cost price as the real value. And the third is counting the whole capital advanced in the cost price, when fixed capital enters only by its wear.
