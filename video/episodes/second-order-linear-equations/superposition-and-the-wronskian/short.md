Two solutions, and they still can't fit a start at one with rate zero.

@math items="y₁ = e^(−t),  y₂ = 2·e^(−t)@e to the minus t;;y′(0) = −y(0)@minus its starting value"
Take e to the minus t and two e to the minus t. Both solve y double prime plus three y prime plus two y. But every combination has a starting rate equal to minus its starting value.

@words text="W(0) = 0" sub="W = y₁·y₂′ − y₁′·y₂"
Their Wronskian at zero is zero. That's the warning sign.
