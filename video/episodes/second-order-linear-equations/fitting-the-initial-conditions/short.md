@art rise-from-zero
It starts at zero. So its starting rate is zero, right? No.

@words text="y(0) = 0,  y′(0) = 1"
The starting rate is a second piece of data. You don't get it by differentiating the number y of zero.

@math items="y = e^(−t) − e^(−2t)@e to the minus t"
For y double prime plus three y prime plus two y, starting at zero with rate one, the fit is e to the minus t minus e to the minus two t. It starts at zero and rises at rate one.
