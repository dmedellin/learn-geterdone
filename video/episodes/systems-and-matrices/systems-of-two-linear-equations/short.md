title: Why two lines never meet exactly twice

@art three-cases
Can two straight lines meet in exactly two points? Never. Here's why.

@art two-points
Through two points there's exactly one line. So if two lines share two points, they're the same line, and they share every point.

@list heading="Two equations, two unknowns" items="One solution@cross once|No solution@parallel|Infinitely many@the same line"
That's why a system of two linear equations has one solution when the lines cross once, none when they're parallel, and infinitely many when they're the same line. Nothing else.
