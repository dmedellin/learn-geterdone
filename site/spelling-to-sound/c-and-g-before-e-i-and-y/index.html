C and G Before E, I and Y

The letters c and g each have two sounds, and the letter after them chooses. Before e, i or y, c is said as s and g as the sound at the start of judge. Anywhere else, c is k and g is the hard sound in go. The table scores both rules. One is perfect and one is not.

Core ideas

What this lesson establishes

Three ideas. The third is the one a student needs most.

1

The next letter chooses the sound

A c or a g followed by e, i or y takes its soft sound: s for c, and j for g. Followed by anything else, including a, o, u and a consonant, a letter that is not a, e, i, o or u, it takes its hard sound. You read one letter and you have the answer.

2

A score counts only the words the rule can be tried on

Many words have a c or a g that is not a test of this rule. In chair and back the c is part of a pair of letters. In sign the g is not said. In science the c sits beside an s that could be the source of the sound. The table leaves these out and lists them with the reason, so the score is about the rule and not about other things.

3

The two rules are not equally good

Soft c is right on all 348 words it can be tried on. Soft g is right on 177 of 187. The ten misses are short, common words, and they are the same kind of word every time, so you can learn them as one list.

The material

The ten words where g keeps its hard sound

The table scored 187 words for the g rule, and ten of them break it.

Take two letters and ask what sound each makes. For c the answer is two sounds. It is k in cat and s in city. For g it is also two: the hard sound in go and the sound at the start of gentle.

You have probably been given a rule for this. Before e, i or y, the letter is soft. Anywhere else it is hard. The question for this lesson is a plain one: how often is that right?

What the table counts

Before the page was built, every word of the 2,800 with a c or a g in it was picked out, and the way it is said was read from a dictionary, a book that records how the word is said. The page prints those words, runs the rule on each one, and compares the two. It scores a word only if the c or the g is the one place the sound could come from. A word with two c letters is set aside, because one score cannot say which c made the sound. So is a word where the letter is part of ch, ck, sc, cc, cq or qu, and a word where another letter, s, k, q, x or z, could make the sound instead.

That leaves 348 words with a c that tests the rule, and 400 that were set aside. For g it leaves 187 and sets 143 aside. Open the setting called the words set aside and every one of them is there with its reason.

The c rule is right every time

On the 348 words, the rule is right 348 times, which is 100.0%. Center, certain, face, price and century are said with s. Call, come, cut and cold are said with k. There is no miss to read, and that is a result too: for this list, one letter after the c is all a student needs.

Eight words were set aside because the dictionary gives them both sounds or neither. They are ancient, appreciate, efficient, financial, ocean, official, politician and racial. In seven of them the c is said as sh; for ancient the dictionary's first entry gives the sound at the start of church. That is a different rule, and the last lesson of this course takes up the endings several of them share.

The g rule fails on ten words

On the 187 words for g, the rule is right 177 times, which is 94.7%. Gentle, giant, age, large and energy follow it. The ten that do not are altogether, begin, forget, give, gear, get, gift, girl, target and together.

Read them again. In every one, the g sits before e or i and is said hard. That is why this list is worth knowing: all ten are common words, and you will use them every day.

What the score does not say

The dictionary is American and the table reads only its first entry. A word that has two ways to be said is scored on the first. Nine words were set aside for g because the dictionary gives both sounds or neither, sign and foreign among them. The rule says what the spelling suggests, and the dictionary says what people say.

Score both rules, then read the misses

Choose a rule. The page reads the letter after the c or the g in each word of the list, says what the rule predicts, and checks it against the first way a dictionary says the word. The dictionary is American. The setting called every word scored shows the right answers too, and the setting called the words set aside shows what was left out and why.

Method

Saying a word with a c or a g in it

Four steps, in this order.

1

Find the letter after the c or the g

Look only at that one letter. If the c or the g is the last letter, there is nothing after it and the hard sound is the answer.

2

If it is e, i or y, use the soft sound

City and cell begin with s. Gentle and giant begin with the sound at the start of judge.

3

If it is anything else, use the hard sound

Cat, cold and cup begin with k. Go and good begin with the hard g.

4

For g, check the short list of words that break it

Get, give, girl and seven more keep the hard sound before e or i. There is no such list for c.

Three groups from one rule

Each line reads the letter after the c or the g and gives the sound. The last line is the one that fails.

city, cell c before i, e: said s cat, cold, cup c before a, o, u: said k gentle, giant g before e, i: said j get, give, girl g before e, i: said g, a miss

Get is one of the first words a student meets, and it breaks the rule. So do give, girl, gift, begin, forget, gear, target, together and altogether. In all ten, the g comes before e or i and is said hard. Nothing goes wrong the other way: on the 187 words, a g followed by anything else is never said as j.

Common mistakes

1
Thinking the two rules are equally goodThey look the same, and they are taught together, so a student expects them to work the same way. Soft c is right 348 times in 348. Soft g is right 177 times in 187, and the ten misses include get, give and girl.
2
Scoring every word that has a c or a g in itIn back and chair the letter is part of a pair, and in sign it is not said. Those words do not test the rule. The table sets 400 words aside for c and 143 for g, and prints each with its reason.
3
Leaving out the yThe rule names three letters, and y counts like e and i. Cycle begins with s for that reason; the table does not score it, since it has two c letters. The words it does score with a y after the letter, policy and agency for c and energy and strategy for g, all follow the rule.

Check your understanding

✓
Finish when you can say, before you look it up, how a word with c or g in it is said, and name the ten words that break the g rule.

You should be able to read the letter after a c or a g, choose the sound, read the score of each rule off the table, say which words were left out of the score and why, and say the ten words where g keeps its hard sound.

Note: A perfect score and a score of 94.7% look close. They are not the same kind of rule. With c you apply the rule and stop. With g you apply the rule and then ask whether the word is one of ten.
