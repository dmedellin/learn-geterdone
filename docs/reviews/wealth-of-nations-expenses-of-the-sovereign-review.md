## Fixes

**Accuracy against the text**

1. L1: "The Greeks served this way until the Persian wars" changed to "until after the
   second Persian war" (l. 22620-22622).
2. L1: "he does not deny Caesar's or Cromwell's" changed to "he grants the examples of
   Caesar and Cromwell" (l. 23038-23040). This was garbled, not wrong.
3. L1 mistake: the Tartar militia follows chiefs it obeys "in peace", not "every day"
   (l. 22832-22834).
4. L2: cut "Pay of that kind is closer to what Smith says of English schoolmasters in
   Part III". There is no such passage for judges, and the sentence muddled the lab.
5. L3: the turnpike note was added "after the first two editions" (l. 23662), not
   after "the first editions". Also fixed "a source of a half a million".
6. L4: "The African company, which parliament charged with the forts, drew about
   £13,000" now names the company of merchants trading to Africa, the regulated company
   of 1750 (l. 24023-24025, 24075). Before, it could be confused with the Royal African
   Company, whose £10,000 from 1730 (l. 24196) follows in the next paragraph.
7. L4: the "seldom allows the dividend … to exceed the ordinary rate" sentence is about
   any company with a perpetual monopoly, not the East India Company's servants in
   particular (l. 24572-24576). Reworded to say so.

**Accuracy about others**

8. L2: Bentham. "Argued in his writings on judicial reform that a court which charges
   for its own use is taxing justice" became his *Protest against Law Taxes* of the
   1790s: charges on going to law fall hardest on the poor and can shut them out of
   justice.
9. L4: Mill (1848). The claim about what Mill held was written from memory and I
   could not confirm it, so I removed it. It is replaced by a safe fact: under the
   limited-liability laws of the next century the joint-stock form carried on far more
   than Smith's four trades. Philip Stern, on the Company as a political body, is
   accurate and was kept.
10. L5: Wollstonecraft (1792). The vague "what parents judged useful was the problem"
    is now her actual objection: an education aimed at pleasing, with national
    co-educational day schools as her remedy.
11. L5: the Scottish parish-school charge on landowners had no citation. It now names
    the Act of 1696 for settling schools, with the levy on each parish's heritors, and
    says what that means for Smith's "partly paid by the public".
12. L6: Chaves and Gorski (2001) studied religious participation, not peace. The
    "or more peace" was dropped, and the lesson now says that Smith's own question,
    about peace and moderation, is a different one.
13. Checked and kept: Dupuit (an engineer writing in the 1840s, on a toll keeping off
    users whose gain is below it), the fourth-power road-wear finding (twentieth
    century), Ferguson on the militia, the Hume passage (Smith does not name him,
    l. 25621; it is from Hume's *History of England*), Smith at Balliol and Glasgow
    fees, and Pennsylvania (l. 25736).

**Labs**

14. L6 `scotland` showed four empty tiles (fees £0, 0%, £0, "0% from fees"). I merged
    it with `three-clergies` into one `scotland` preset: Smith's average minister,
    `34257/472` ≈ £72.58, beside the lesson's parish priest and friar. I checked every
    tile by hand (priest £20 of £60, ≈ 33.33%; friar £30, 100%; +£0.1 a hearer) and
    re-pinned from `--observe`. The prose, panel intro and note were updated to match.
15. L4 shows dividends through advantage/profit, where three tiles (true profit, its
    rate, wage share 0%) repeated or were zero. The kit offers no better view, so the
    prose now says what those tiles mean: the proprietors give no labour, so nothing is
    wages. That is the reverse of Book I's apothecary.
16. L3 step 4 said the "heaviest user" pays most, but in `heavy-trade` the lab's
    heaviest-carriage tile is the waggon while the coal-cart pays most. The step now
    says "whoever moves the most tons".
17. L1 `greece` prints "no cost given". The prose now explains why: the citizen of a
    small agrarian state served without pay in a short campaign (l. 22618-22621).
