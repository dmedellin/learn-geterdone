## Fixes made

**Lesson 1, The Annual Labour of Every Nation**

1. Concept 3 said "the whole of Book I is spent on" skill. Book I covers the causes of the improvement *and* the distribution of the produce (PSU l. 143-146). Now: "Book I opens with it".
2. The body matched Smith's two circumstances to the formula's two factors in the formula's order. That made skill sound like the first factor, but Smith's first circumstance (skill) is the formula's second factor. The paragraph now says the order is reversed and names each one (l. 116-120).
3. "sometimes driven to abandon": the text says "frequently reduced, or at least think themselves reduced" (l. 127-129). Now "often driven, or think themselves driven".
4. "it is why he says the first circumstance seems to matter more" credited the lesson's invented figures as Smith's reason. Now it reads as Smith's claim put into the lesson's own figures. The point that skill is unbounded while the share at work is a fraction is now marked as the lesson's way of seeing it, not Smith's words.
5. Sahlins (1972) worked from other scholars' field studies, so "from field studies" became "drawing on other scholars' field studies".
6. The "Money and measures, once" definition is the Subject's one statement of the units. It now says this lesson uses plain units, gives a worked example (£2 3s 6d = 522d), and separates the pound of money from a pound weight of silver, pointing to course 2.
7. The closing line said lesson 2 answers "the second factor". The pin factory is about the first circumstance, skill, and the line now says so.

**Lesson 3, The Propensity to Truck, Barter and Exchange**

8. Added Smith's lean, "as seems more probable", that the propensity comes from reason and speech (l. 481-484).
9. The text gave Smith a conclusion he does not draw. It said the `slight-difference` preset was "the point of Smith's next paragraph", namely that a small natural difference means a small gain, so a large division must have grown its own differences. Smith's paragraph (l. 557-575) argues that the differences are made by the division. The paragraph now says the lab's arithmetic is about differences people already have, that Smith reverses the order, and that a fixed-hours lab cannot show his point. The second mistake and the worked example's "after" text were corrected to match.
10. The note said "a tribe of hunters and a village"; there is no village in Chapter 2. Now "a tribe of hunters or shepherds" (l. 537-538).

**Lesson 4, The Extent of the Market**

11. The figure was misread. Smith says a Highland district could not take even a thousand nails: "impossible to dispose of one thousand" (l. 635-637). The summary said the nailer "could sell a thousand", the body called the preset "exactly this", the step said "Smith's Highland figure is a thousand", and the note called 1,000 "a reasoned guess". All four now treat 1,000 as an upper bound that the lab uses as the demand.
12. The `ladder` paragraph said "the first four places" cannot keep a nailer. The port keeps 3, so only the first three cannot. Changed to "three".
13. "The goods that bear a price in proportion to their weight are the only ones the wagons can move" became Smith's "price was very considerable in proportion to their weight" (l. 662-663).
14. "Egypt was the first country improved because the Nile..." became Smith's hedged "seems", with "probably one of the principal causes" (l. 706-714). Smith's caveat is about "the great extent of this antiquity", not the antiquity itself (l. 716); this was fixed in the body and in the third mistake.
15. "Passes over the caravan routes that historians have since described" became "says nothing of the overland caravan trade".
16. "why every society has ended by choosing a metal" became "why people settled at last on a metal". Smith's own word is "men seem at last..." in I.4.
