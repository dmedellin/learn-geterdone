## Fixes

**Accuracy**

1. L1: the quotation began in mid-clause ("&ldquo;to maintain any but productive
   hands…"). Rebuilt around "never is immediately employed" (l. 10839) and "It
   pays the wages of productive labour only" (l. 10843).
2. L1: "the noblest and the meanest" both had value "regulated by the very same
   principles". The text says that of the meanest only, and says the noblest
   produce nothing that could later buy equal labour (l. 10812–10815).
3. L1: "like Glasgow and the Dutch towns" changed to the text's "many English,
   and … most Dutch towns" (l. 10941). Glasgow now appears in Smith's contrast
   with Edinburgh.
4. L1 note: cut "and the next chapter's": Chapter 4 does not define productive
   labour.
5. L2: "Smith's answer" to Mandeville. Chapter 3 never names him, and the text
   now says so.
6. L3: "the other side of Chapter 8 of Book I" changed to "Chapters 8 and 9".
   Profit falling as stock grows is I.9's argument.
7. L4: the butcher passage had the poor workman putting "into his larder what
   he needed in his trade". Smith's point is that buying a month's provisions
   takes stock out of his trade (l. 11738–11741).
8. L4: the waterfall objection was given to an unnamed critic, but it is
   Ricardo's own reply to this passage. The paragraph also ended "Smith's own
   caution is that the order is not a law of nature: it is what private profit and
   the policy of Europe happen to have made of it". The text says the order is
   natural and that Europe inverted it (l. 12381–12386). Corrected, and List's
   *National System* is now dated 1841.
9. L4 note: Marx was named as a critic of the ranking without support in the
   body. It now reads "Ricardo and List among them".
10. L5: "the figures of 4 and 1/3 are Smith's own". He gives ranges ("three or
    four times", "two or three years", l. 12004–12006), so they are now "the ends
    of Smith's own ranges".
11. L5: Holland is "by far the richest" in proportion to its land and people
    (l. 12172–12173), not "for its size".
12. L5: the Ricardo sentence is softened to what *Principles* does: it measures
    the gain from trade by comparative costs, not by the home labour employed.

**Labs**

13. L5 `three-branches`: the tile shows "80.00×", which no prose stated. The
    body now gives it.
14. L5 mistakes: "Change the second preset's returns to once a year" described
    the preset's existing setting as a change. Corrected.

**Quiz**

15. L3 Q1: the `why` said 3,000 "divides by it twice over", which is false, and
    120 had no explanation. They are replaced by 12 (rent ÷ rate) and 1,500 (the
    price at 4 per cent), both explained.
16. L4 Q3: the `why` read "Dividing by 40 gives 12.5", but no 40 appears in the
    question, and the distractor 2 had no explanation. They are replaced by 12,500
    (multiplying) and 475 (subtracting).
17. L4 Q4: the `why` now answers "retailers add nothing" by citing the retailer's
    profit as the value his capital adds (l. 11785ff.).
18. L5 Q2: "thirty-six" ("not a product of anything") is replaced by fourteen,
    which comes from adding the factors.

**Prose**

19. L2 step 3: "shrinks, at an increasing rate in pounds only if the factor is
    below zero" is replaced by "shrinks by the same share each year, so by fewer
    pounds each year".
