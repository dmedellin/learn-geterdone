# Second review: Credit and Fictitious Capital (Course 12)

Course `capital-credit-and-fictitious-capital`, package `content/capital/c12_credit/`,
Volume III, Part V, Chapters 25–36. Reviewed 2026-10-24 against the Kerr text
(`volume-3-kerr.txt`, ll. 3596–5332, every chapter read, and Engels' Preface
ll. 300–316 on how he assembled Part V), following `docs/capital/SECOND-REVIEW.md`.
Sections 1–6 were written before the pedagogy assessment was opened. Section 7
was written after it.

**Result.** The arithmetic is sound. Every figure in the prose, worked lines and
quiz answers recomputes exactly, and every preset's tiles match the prose. Three
quiz `why`s gave wrong reasons for distractors, and those are fixed. The quotation
checker reports 0 misses on 268 segments.

The most serious error was a logic error in "Money Capital and Real Capital I"
(Chapter 30). The lesson read the lab's positive gap, 500 of surplus product
unsold, as 500 of money in the sellers' hands, deposited at the bank. If the
product is unsold, the sellers have not received the money for it. Marx's picture
is that the commodity capital cannot complete its round, while the money that
would have bought it lies idle in the banks. The lesson repeated this error in
eight places, and all eight are now rewritten.

The next most serious problems were these:
- In "Bills of Exchange and the Credit System", the circle quiz asked about a bill
  that cannot exist.
- In "Medium of Circulation and Capital", the chapter was called "Marx's
  throughout".
- In "The Currency Principle and the Bank Act of 1844", the course said Chapter 34
  was "mostly" Engels'. In fact Marx's text is about 28 KB of it and Engels'
  brackets about 17 KB.

The two Kerr slips the course pointed out are kept as readings. Both appeals to
the German are dropped (section 2).

Final preview line: `OK` (13 pages rendered; 12 pages executed and swept, 12
with pinned figures, 0 failing).

## 2. The two Kerr slips, judged from the English alone

**Chapter 28, l. 3953, "the same price of money, for instance a five pound note,
passes from one sphere to the other".** The context makes "price" unreadable. What
passes from one sphere to the other is a single physical note, given as the
example. Earlier in the same chapter, at l. 3941, Marx speaks of the money in
circulation as "pieces of individual coin". The context does not prove which word
was meant, but "piece" fits and nothing else obvious does. The lesson now says
"price" makes no sense there and "reads as a slip, most likely for 'piece'". The
claim that Marx's German is *Geldstück* is dropped.

**Chapter 32, l. 4486, "it is by no means a demand for money as a means of
payment".** As printed, the sentence contradicts its own first clause, which is
about the demand for means of payment in a crisis. The contrast earlier in the
chapter forces the reading. At l. 4462, in a crisis "every one borrows only for the
purpose of paying", and in the recovery loan capital "is demanded for the purpose
of buying". At l. 4061, in Chapter 28, Marx draws the same line between means of
payment and means of purchase. So "means of purchase" is the forced sense, and the
lesson keeps that reading. What the English cannot show is whose slip it is: the
translator's, the printer's or the manuscript's. The sentence "Marx's German has
*Kaufmittel* … so the slip is the translation's, not the manuscript's" is replaced
with "Whether the slip is the translator's, the printer's or the manuscript's, the
English alone cannot say."

The §C entries 4 and 8 in `docs/capital/PLAN.md` were changed to match.

## 6. Voice and recommendations (not changed)

- The lesson's own verdict "the best critics of the Bank Act" in lesson 4's summary was changed to "the leading critics". Nothing else in the voice needed fixing. The sarcasm passages (lessons 2 and 6) report Marx's tone rather than performing it.
- **Recommendation, lesson 5:** the step "Compare with what was invested" still treats fictitious capital as an excess over money paid in. Marx's point is that the paper is a duplicate whatever its price. The concept now says so, but the step and the worked "after" ("The share's extra £100 is a price for paper") could say it too.
- **Recommendation, lesson 6:** the lab cannot show the deposits. A second tile, or a line in the panel giving the investors' unspent hoard, would make the corrected reading visible rather than only stated. This is the kit's business, not the course's.

## 7. Where I disagree with the pedagogy assessment

(Written after reading `docs/pedagogy/capital-credit-and-fictitious-capital.md`.)

I agree with the assessment on the arithmetic, the tiles, the voice and the
structure. Its repairs to lesson 8's worked lines, lesson 3's quiz 3, the
Overstone "commerce" figure and the `cap` read-out are all correct, and I left
them in place. I also agree that twelve lessons earn their place. I disagree on
the following points.

1. **The two slips do not "stand" on the German.** The assessment answers the
   slip question by citing *dasselbe Geldstück* and *Kaufmittel*, and it had the
   lessons name both words as the ground "so a reader can see the claim is not a
   guess". No German text is in the project. The assessment admits this under
   Remaining issues, so the German words were themselves unchecked claims
   presented as evidence.

   Judged from the English alone:
   - For l. 4486 the reading is forced by the chapter's own contrast (l. 4462).
     But the English cannot show that the slip is "the translation's, not the
     manuscript's", and that conclusion is withdrawn.
   - For l. 3953 the context shows that "price" makes no sense and that "piece"
     fits. It does not prove which word was meant, so the lesson now says "reads
     as a slip, most likely for 'piece'".

   The assessment called the lesson's earlier "flags the reading as its own"
   too modest. I think that modesty was nearer the evidence than the
   replacement.

2. **The course was less honest about Engels' hand than the verdict says.** The
   assessment says the honesty question "is answered yes, and in more places
   than the brief asked for". It missed three things:
   - Lesson 4 said the chapter is "Marx's throughout", which the assessment
     quotes approvingly as Engels' own word. Engels' bracket at ll. 4037–4047
     and his gloss at l. 4052 sit inside it.
   - Lesson 10 said "Most of Chapter 34 is in Engels' hand", and the assessment
     repeats it ("the one lesson whose passage is Engels'… says so three
     times"). The passage is Engels', but the chapter is mostly Marx's, about
     28 KB against Engels' 17 KB.
   - Lessons 2, 7, 8, 9, 11 and 12 said nothing about the state of the
     manuscript, although `__init__.py` promises a chapter-by-chapter account.

   All of these are now fixed (section 3).

3. **The hoard lab's reading was wrong, and the assessment endorsed it.** The
   assessment says the `hoard` panel and steps "say exactly what the two groups
   do and what the sign means". They said a positive gap is product unsold *and*
   "the same sum held as money". Both cannot be true of the same 500: if nobody
   bought the product, nobody paid for it. This was the most serious error in
   the course (section 1, #11), and the assessment lists the tile among its
   refutations without catching it.

4. **"Every other distractor… could not be defended" is too strong.** The
   answers were all right. But three `why`s explained a distractor wrongly:
   - lesson 4, "subtracts the velocities"
   - lesson 5, "a factor of ten"
   - lesson 6, deposits "not the same as fictitious capital", against Chapter 29

   Separately, lesson 1's quiz 2 described a bill that cannot exist in the
   circle it sets up. The assessment does not mention any of these.

5. **Fidelity misses.** The assessment traced "some forty paraphrases" but left
   standing seven statements that the text does not support:
   - Norman's view, given as "Interest is the price of commodities used in production".
   - The rate of interest that "moves on its own".
   - The note's "made the rise in interest harder to bear".
   - The weakened "stem the fall" point in lesson 3.
   - The 1845–47 renewals credited to Engels' bracket.
   - "Marx's last remark in the chapter" in lesson 12, which is followed by four pages of notes.
   - "Of drains caused by crop failures Marx says only…" in lesson 11.

   It also accepted "Tooke told the Lords' committee", which I could not tie to
   the Lords.

6. **The remedy for the German gap.** The assessment proposes adding a German
   extract to the shared folder. That would help. Until then, I think no lesson
   should rest a claim on a German word the project cannot check, which is the
   rule the brief applied to this review.
